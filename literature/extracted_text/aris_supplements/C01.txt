                                                                                                                                   1. INTRODUCTION
                                                                                                                                   Over the past few decades, and particularly in the last 10 years, the advancement of autonomous
                                                                                                                                   medical robots has attracted increasing attention (1, 2). Recent advances in fundamental sensing
                                                                                                                                   systems and artificial intelligence have made progress toward the creation of intelligent robotic
                                                                                                                                   colleagues that can work with medical staffs in hospitals (3–5). Among the various subfields of
Downloaded from www.annualreviews.org. University of Southampton (ar-182708) IP: 152.78.0.24 On: Thu, 29 Oct 2026 14:49:28




                                                                                                                                   medical robotics, robotic sonography has particularly garnered attention from both the scientific
                                                                                                                                   and industrial sectors (6–8). This surge in interest can be attributed to the fact that robotic ul-
                                                                                                                                   trasound (US) examinations are generally noninvasive compared with procedures involving other
                                                                                                                                   surgical robots, resulting in fewer ethical, legal, and regulatory concerns.
                                                                                                                                       Due to the advantages of being portable, real-time, and free of ionizing radiation, medical
                                                                                                                                   US has gained widespread use in primary healthcare. The rapid evolution of medical imaging
                                                                                                                                   modalities throughout the last century has transformed imaging into an indispensable element
                                                                                                                                   of standard screening and diagnostic protocols. However, the development of interactive and
                                                                                                                                   dynamic imaging for treatment guidance and intervention has lagged behind. This can be at-
                                                                                                                                   tributed, in part, to the intricate patient and procedure-specific demands that such imaging entails.
                                                                                                                                   Moreover, its considerable reliance on user proficiency and usability considerations has also con-
                                                                                                                                   tributed to its slower advancement (9). To improve reproducibility and ensure consistent diagnosis,
                                                                                                                                   Salcudean et al. (10) introduced the use of robotic systems to assist in US acquisition. By accurately
                                                                                                                                   maneuvering the US probe, robotic US systems (RUSSs) are expected to standardize examination
                                                                                                                                   protocols and optimize imaging quality (11, 12). In light of the increasing demand for healthcare
                                                                                                                                   interventions and the uneven distribution of experienced sonographers, there is a pressing need for
                                                                                                                                   the development of RUSSs with a high degree of autonomy. The applications for representative
                                                                                                                                   clinical scenarios are summarized in Figure 1.
                                                                                                                                       Robotic systems have demonstrated the potential to surpass human capabilities in certain
                                                                                                                                   domains. Advanced mechanical and material designs, coupled with innovative control methods,
                                                                                                                                   enable robots to operate remotely in challenging environments, such as the lunar surface (26) and
                                                                                                                                   the deep sea (27). Nevertheless, there is still a big gap between robotic systems and humans re-
                                                                                                                                   garding their capabilities for deep understanding and modeling of the dynamic world. Similar to
                                                                                                                                   their human counterparts, intelligent robotic sonographers should possess the ability to compre-
                                                                                                                                   hend human anatomy, diseases, physiology, and the physics of US imaging. Only by possessing
                                                                                                                                   these capabilities can a robotic sonographer reason about proper actions while performing US
                                                                                                                                   examinations and, at the same time, taking the utmost consideration for patient safety. The two
                                                                                                                                   key abilities in this context are perception and execution, which are tightly entangled and can
                                                                                                                                   mutually boost each other. Precise scene understanding will benefit the action reasoning process,
                                                                                                                                   while accurate maneuvering of the US probe will further facilitate understanding. Unfortunately,
                                                                                                                                   such intelligent systems have yet to emerge within the community. But thanks to the boom of
                                                                                                                                   machine learning and deep learning, researchers are approaching this ultimate goal from various
                                                                                                                                   perspectives. Jiang et al. (28) introduced the concept of “the language of sonography” (p. 1) by in-
                                                                                                                                   ferring the underlying reward function from a few US examination demonstrations from experts
                                                                                                                                   to understand the intention of the sonographer. Baumgartner et al. (29) proposed SonoNet, a deep
                                                                                                                                   convolutional neural network (CNN), to automatically detect 13 fetal standard planes in real time.
                                                                                                                                   In addition, when searching for standard planes, Droste et al. (30) presented a behavior-cloning
                                                                                                                                   framework to mimic sonographers.
                                                                                                                                       Several survey articles have investigated RUSSs from different perspectives. For instance, Jiang
                                                                                                                                   et al. (6) discussed advancements in terms of the complexity of the technologies, while others have
                                                                                                                                   categorized papers based on autonomy levels (7, 8). In contrast, this survey article uniquely fo-
                                                                                                                                   cuses on the technologies aimed at enhancing the intelligence of RUSSs. Section 2 presents an

                                                                                                                             2. HARDWARE AND CONTROL IN ROBOTIC ULTRASOUND
                                                                                                                             To acquire high-quality US images for diagnostic or analytical purposes, RUSSs should be
                                                                                                                             able to maneuver the US probe toward the target region while precisely regulating several
                                                                                                                             acquisition parameters (11). These parameters primarily involve contact forces, positions, and
                                                                                                                             orientations. The desired values are either determined from the clinician’s side within the
                                                                                                                             teleoperated/semiautonomous paradigms (10, 31) or derived from intuitive/intelligent algorithms

                                                                                                                                                                               www.annualreviews.org • Machine Learning in Robotic Ultrasound Imaging               337
                                                                                                                                   (12, 32) running in the background. Consequently, the evolution of RUSSs’ mechanism design and
                                                                                                                                   control strategy is prominently centered around enhancements in imaging quality, control accu-
                                                                                                                                   racy, and execution safety. To highlight the main focus of applying machine learning to RUSSs, this
                                                                                                                                   section provides a brief summary of relevant hardware and control methods used for developing
                                                                                                                                   RUSSs. A more systematic summary can be found in a previous survey paper (6).

                                                                                                                                   2.1. Mechanism Design
                                                                                                                                   Safety stands as the highest priority in the development of RUSSs. The implementation of spe-
                                                                                                                                   cially designed mechanisms can passively limit the applied forces on the patient and avoid potential
                                                                                                                                   failure of the sensor or the electrical system, thereby bolstering safety throughout US scanning.
                                                                                                                                   To this end, Tsumura & Iwata (33) developed a hardware platform to position a US probe for
                                                                                                                                   obstetric examination. The platform includes a linear actuator to limit the contact force and a
                                                                                                                                   ring-guide mechanism to passively adjust the US probe’s posture relative to the body surface. To
                                                                                                                                   ensure safety at the actuator level, Wang et al. (34) investigated a customized spring-loaded ball
                                                                                                                                   clutch joint to passively restrict the exerted force.
                                                                                                                                       To facilitate quick deployment and fast adaptation to different scanning tasks, it is also common
                                                                                                                                   to use general robotic manipulators to develop RUSSs (13, 35). In this case, the mechanism design
                                                                                                                                   focuses more on refining the US probe holder component. Tan et al. (16) proposed a dual arm
                                                                                                                                   system with a flexible spring-based US probe clamping device for breast US imaging. To passively
                                                                                                                                   adapt to the irregular contact contours, Facundo-Flores et al. (36) adopted a soft probe, which pro-
                                                                                                                                   vides good surface adaptation and force transfer, as part of the RUSS. Wang et al. (37) developed
                                                                                                                                   a compliant joint-based adjusting mechanism attached to the robot manipulator to facilitate spine
                                                                                                                                   US examinations. Recently, a motorized probe holder for a robot manipulator has been reported.
                                                                                                                                   Bao et al. (38) designed a motor-spring-based mechanical US probe holder to enable online force
                                                                                                                                   adjustment and operation continuity. This design pattern can be a promising way to achieve com-
                                                                                                                                   pliant contact for safety and active posture alignment between the probe and contact surface for
                                                                                                                                   better image quality.

                                                                                                                                   2.2. Control Method
                                                                                                                                   In the context of US scanning, the full control of RUSSs can be decoupled into two subspaces:
                                                                                                                                   one-degree-of-freedom force control along the imaging direction of the probe and five-degree-
                                                                                                                                   of-freedom motion control for navigation along a scanning path (6). To orient a US probe,
                                                                                                                                   velocity-based hybrid force–motion control schemes are commonly used in the community (39,
                                                                                                                                   40). Given that US examinations require extensive contact with patients, impedance/admittance
                                                                                                                                   control is frequently employed to dynamically modulate contact force and position, primarily for
                                                                                                                                   safety concerns (31, 41, 42). To suppress the accumulated control error, Wang et al. (43) proposed
                                                                                                                                   a practical full-task-space compliant control framework with motion estimation and feedback
                                                                                                                                   reinitialization for RUSSs. Nevertheless, the performance of impedance/admittance control re-
                                                                                                                                   lies heavily on the precise estimation of the environmental parameters, specifically stiffness and
                                                                                                                                   damping terms.
                                                                                                                                       Due to the interpatient variation of tissue properties, varying levels of force are often required
                                                                                                                                   to obtain high-quality images (21, 44). To accommodate changes in contact conditions, Duan
                                                                                                                                   et al. (45) proposed a quadratic-programming-based control framework with variable impedance
                                                                                                                                   for US-guided scoliosis assessment. Xiao et al. (46) employed variable impedance control to regu-
                                                                                                                                   late the contact force for better visualization in a human-in-the-loop environment. To effectively
                                                                                                                                   govern the contact dynamics, Santos & Cortesão (47) proposed a hierarchical control structure
                                                                                                                                   where the Cartesian force control takes the primary priority and orientation is controlled within

                                                                                                                             338   Bi et al.
                                                                                                                             the null space. Their work incorporated online estimation of contact stiffness, enabling con-
                                                                                                                             trol adaptation before contact. Alternatively, Kim et al. (48) utilized the Hunt–Crossley model
                                                                                                                             to formulate a control law with online parameter estimation. Abbas et al. (49) devised an adaptive
                                                                                                                             event-triggered force–motion control scheme for transversal abdomen scans. The control scheme
                                                                                                                             combines an adaptive backstepping motion controller with a proportional–integral–derivative
Downloaded from www.annualreviews.org. University of Southampton (ar-182708) IP: 152.78.0.24 On: Thu, 29 Oct 2026 14:49:28




                                                                                                                             (PID) force controller, and the update of control input is triggered based on Lyapunov-based
                                                                                                                             analysis. To integrate RUSSs into real-world scenarios, various efforts have been made to opti-
                                                                                                                             mize the acquisition process using multisensor feedback. These optimizations include adjusting
                                                                                                                             the US probe’s posture (11, 12, 15, 50, 51), adapting contact forces (46, 52), refining scanning
                                                                                                                             trajectories (39, 53), and so on.
                                                                                                                                 The integration of robotic systems has showcased the capacity to elevate the precision and
                                                                                                                             consistency of US image acquisition. We consider the soft mechanics and control techniques in
                                                                                                                             RUSSs to be relatively well developed, and the key challenge lies in comprehending dynamic
                                                                                                                             environments and action reasoning—a crucial hurdle on the path to realizing intelligent robotic
                                                                                                                             sonographers. In the following sections, we provide some insights into the application of machine
                                                                                                                             learning algorithms that try to address this challenge.

                                                                                                                             3. MACHINE LEARNING IN ROBOTIC ULTRASOUND
                                                                                                                             This section categorizes the machine learning approaches in RUSSs into modular and direct ap-
                                                                                                                             proaches for action reasoning (Figure 2). Direct approaches use advanced learning frameworks
                                                                                                                             that continuously update and optimize for the best control strategy by directly observing the en-
                                                                                                                             vironment and output corresponding actions. Modular approaches, in contrast, include mainly
                                                                                                                             control algorithms that integrate the outcomes of deep learning models into rule-based control
                                                                                                                             laws. In modular approaches, intermediate perception results are explicitly extracted, while in di-
                                                                                                                             rect approaches, the intermediate clues are implicitly processed inside a decision-making network.
                                                                                                                             Both approaches have their own advantages and disadvantages. Direct approaches require less
                                                                                                                             supervision from experts, while modular approaches are more predictable.

                                                                                                                                   3.1. Modular Approaches for Action Reasoning
                                                                                                                                   For human sonographers, action reasoning is based on their understanding of US image forma-
                                                                                                                                   tion in US examinations. In modular approaches, machine learning algorithms explicitly model
                                                                                                                                   these implicit interpretation processes of sonographers. Two of the most commonly used analyt-
                                                                                                                                   ical techniques are semantic segmentation and registration. The former provides morphological
Downloaded from www.annualreviews.org. University of Southampton (ar-182708) IP: 152.78.0.24 On: Thu, 29 Oct 2026 14:49:28




                                                                                                                                   and positional information on the target anatomy, while the latter makes it feasible to leverage
                                                                                                                                   preoperative or generic models. This section discusses both techniques in detail.

                                                                                                                                   3.1.1. Segmentation. Semantic segmentation of the US image is a commonly used method in
                                                                                                                                   RUSSs to specify and localize the target anatomy. In practice, the semantic segmentation pro-
                                                                                                                                   cess is also frequently carried out implicitly in the sonographer’s mind during US examinations.
                                                                                                                                   To enable autonomous tracking of the target tissue, the outcomes of the segmentation process
                                                                                                                                   are incorporated into the robot’s control loop. Such frameworks have been widely implemented
                                                                                                                                   for different US scanning tasks. Merouche et al. (54) implemented a fast-marching segmentation
                                                                                                                                   method to locate the vessel lumen, subsequently directing the robot to maintain the vessel at the
                                                                                                                                   center of the US view. Similarly, Akbari et al. (55) segmented seroma in breast US using a threshold
                                                                                                                                   method to provide feedback to the robot control loop.
                                                                                                                                      However, such conventional segmentation methods often lack the intuitive understanding
                                                                                                                                   of the anatomical information because they rely on gray-level probability density functions.
                                                                                                                                   Therefore, the performance of these methods often suffers from noise, speckle, and artifacts.
                                                                                                                                   Recently, deep CNNs have emerged as a powerful alternative to conventional segmentation
                                                                                                                                   methods, demonstrating phenomenal success in medical image analysis (56). In general, there
                                                                                                                                   are two characteristics that need to be properly considered: contour and texture. Contour refers
                                                                                                                                   to the statistical shape model of the target anatomy, providing a robust cue for the model dur-
                                                                                                                                   ing segmentation tasks. However, not all anatomies exhibit stable statistical shapes, especially in
                                                                                                                                   cases involving lesions or abnormalities. Hence, the model should also be able to distinguish the
                                                                                                                                   segmentation targets based on their unique textures. In the context of CNNs, anatomy contour ex-
                                                                                                                                   traction depends largely on the network’s capability to address large-scale relationships within the
                                                                                                                                   image, whereas the efficiency of texture recognition is closely linked to the network’s performance
                                                                                                                                   in perceiving local details of images.
                                                                                                                                      Currently, one of the most popular and effective network architectures for biomedical image
                                                                                                                                   segmentation is U-Net (57). Its success is attributed mainly to the skip connections at different
                                                                                                                                   scales of the encoder–decoder architectures. Such implementation ensures that the network can
                                                                                                                                   take advantage of fine details provided by high-level features and global information extracted
                                                                                                                                   from low-level features. Based on a real-time segmentation of tubular structures using U-Net,
                                                                                                                                   Jiang et al. (58) proposed a framework to simultaneously compute the radius of the vessel and op-
                                                                                                                                   timize the vascular centerline to determine the scanning path. Koskinopoulou et al. (59) designed
                                                                                                                                   a dual robot system for venous intervention guided by a segmentation of the veins using Mask
                                                                                                                                   R-CNN, which is a segmentation network designed for simultaneous detection and segmentation
                                                                                                                                   of objects. The aforementioned two RUSSs have been validated on phantoms. To further validate
                                                                                                                                   the practical applicability of autonomous RUSSs in real-world conditions, Chen et al. (60) inte-
                                                                                                                                   grated an external camera with US vessel detection outcomes from U-Net. This enabled automatic
                                                                                                                                   US scanning and subsequent reconstruction of the patient’s femoral artery. In an autonomous ve-
                                                                                                                                   nous puncture mechanism designed by Chen et al. (61), a semisupervised segmentation network,
                                                                                                                                   semi-ResNeXt-Unet, was used to address the problem of insufficient labels. The accuracy of the
                                                                                                                                   vein segmentation was tested on volunteers and later used to guide the puncture robot. To im-
                                                                                                                                   prove the stability of artery segmentation in US images, Jiang et al. (62) introduced DopUS-Net,
                                                                                                                                   which combined Doppler images with B-mode images. During the autonomous scanning of radial

                                                                                                                             340   Bi et al.
                                                                                                                             arteries, the presence of the Doppler effect is constantly monitored, and an out-of-plane fan mo-
                                                                                                                             tion of the US probe is executed when necessary to ensure good segmentation. Doppler images
                                                                                                                             were also employed by Ning et al. (63) as weak supervision for the segmentation network.
                                                                                                                                However, it is worth noting that U-Net and its variants are not specifically designed for US
                                                                                                                             images. The interpatient and even intermachine differences of US images are relatively larger

                                                                                                                             than those observed in optical or CT images. As a result, distinct US-oriented learning ap-
                                                                                                                             proaches are necessary to pave the way toward stable and reliable segmentation performance.
                                                                                                                             In this regard, recent works that have explicitly integrated US physics into learning models, such
                                                                                                                             as CACTUSS (Common Anatomical CT-US Space) (64) and LOTUS (Learning to Optimize
                                                                                                                             Task-Based US Representations) (65), appear to offer a promising solution, which is discussed in
                                                                                                                             detail in Section 4.2.

                                                                                                                             3.1.2. Registration. Registration is a commonly employed way for RUSSs to map an unseen
                                                                                                                             patient-specific scenario to a generic template, preoperative CT, or MRI. This is, in fact, what
                                                                                                                             human sonographers do regularly: They use their full understanding of human anatomy acquired
                                                                                                                             throughout their education and training to explore the anatomy of interest and navigate to re-
                                                                                                                             quired US views for a given clinical task. For robotic sonographers, CT or MRI atlases could play
                                                                                                                             the role of learned mental mapping.
                                                                                                                                 It should be noted that this section focuses only on the application of the registration approach
                                                                                                                             on RUSSs, rather than providing a comprehensive summary of technical details about registration
                                                                                                                             (which can be found in other articles, e.g., Reference 66). Registration techniques have often been
                                                                                                                             used for path planning in developing autonomous RUSSs, particularly for the ones from preop-
                                                                                                                             erative CT or MRI to the current setup. To this end, Hennersperger et al. (67) employed a classic
                                                                                                                             iterative-closest-point algorithm to align the patient surface extracted from a live RGB-D camera
                                                                                                                             to preoperative MRI. Langsch et al. (68) performed a rigid registration between US volume and
                                                                                                                             MRI/CT volume using the linear correlation of linear combination (LC2 ) similarity metric to de-
                                                                                                                             termine the scanning path for aorta. To tackle the interpatient variation, Virga et al. (21) applied
                                                                                                                             nonrigid registration to elastically register the patient to a generic MRI-based template using
                                                                                                                             a Gaussian mixture model. Specifically for articulated joint motion, Jiang et al. (25) proposed a
                                                                                                                             nonrigid registration method to autonomously generate a scanning path for the limb examination
                                                                                                                             based on a generic MRI template. The robustness and effectiveness of this approach have been
                                                                                                                             evaluated on different volunteers.
                                                                                                                                 The aforementioned approaches were proven to be robust in their scenarios, such as the aorta
                                                                                                                             and limb artery examinations. However, for specific applications, such as thoracic examination or
                                                                                                                             intervention within limited acoustic spaces, additional physical constraints should be further taken
                                                                                                                             into consideration to develop an effective and robust system. Due to the high acoustic impedance
                                                                                                                             of bone, an acoustic shadow will be cast and further hinder the visibility of the tissue underneath.
                                                                                                                             To address this challenge, Jiang et al. (69) first proposed a skeleton graph–based nonrigid registra-
                                                                                                                             tion method enabling the transfer of a preplanned path in the limited intercostal space from CT
                                                                                                                             template to patients. To further address the cumbersome tuning of hyperparameters, an evolved
                                                                                                                             solution using a directed dense skeleton graph–based registration was proposed (70).
                                                                                                                                 Besides path planning, registration has also been seen as a potential and promising solution
                                                                                                                             to address the complex issue of patient movement during the scanning process. To develop a
                                                                                                                             motion-aware robotic sonographer, Jiang et al. (71) presented a framework to autonomously de-
                                                                                                                             tect a patient’s motion and update the trajectory based on the registration between the markers’
                                                                                                                             locations (sparse point cloud) obtained before and after the motion using an external camera. Ad-
                                                                                                                             ditionally, an advanced study using the dense point clouds extracted from an RGB-D camera was
                                                                                                                             proposed and validated on a human-like arm phantom with an uneven surface outliner (72).


                                                                                                                                                                   www.annualreviews.org • Machine Learning in Robotic Ultrasound Imaging   341
                                                                                                                                       The aforementioned approaches were mainly developed based on extracted point clouds. Such
                                                                                                                                   approaches are good for robotic applications because they balance registration accuracy and com-
                                                                                                                                   putational efficiency; however, they usually cannot provide pixel-wise precise registration. To
                                                                                                                                   address this problem, Hennersperger et al. (67) carried out an online US–MRI deformable regis-
                                                                                                                                   tration by computing the LC2 similarity to ensure accurate path transfer. LC2 was proposed for

                                                                                                                                   3.2. Direct Approach for Action Reasoning
                                                                                                                                   In this section, we discuss the recent advancements in robotic learning in RUSSs. In contrast to
                                                                                                                                   the last section, the focus here is on works that deduce appropriate actions based on the interaction
                                                                                                                                   through methods such as reinforcement learning (RL) and learning from demonstration (LfD).

                                                                                                                                   3.2.1. Learning from interactions. RL is a famous interactive learning paradigm that has
                                                                                                                                   achieved superhuman performance in various games (76). The core idea of RL is to build a map-
                                                                                                                                   ping between the observed states and the optimized actions through numerous interactions with
                                                                                                                                   the unknown environment, guided by predefined reward functions. This process can be modeled
                                                                                                                                   as a Markov decision process. Nowadays, with the development of deep learning, neural networks
                                                                                                                                   are utilized as powerful approximators when facing a complex state–action space. In the field of
                                                                                                                                   robotic US, initial attempts have been made to exploit the usage of RL. Unlike most RL tasks,
                                                                                                                                   where the true state of the agent is directly observable, most US acquisition tasks can only be
                                                                                                                                   modeled as partially observable Markov decision processes. Thus, the success of the search is de-
                                                                                                                                   termined largely by whether the model can correctly infer the true state (i.e., the relative position
                                                                                                                                   between the probe and the scanning target) from its observations (i.e., US images).
                                                                                                                                       Hase et al. (77) implemented a deep Q-learning model to navigate the US probe toward the
                                                                                                                                   sacrum of the spine, while considering only two translational movements. Li et al. (78) extended
                                                                                                                                   the dimension of the action space of the Q-learning method from two to five to complete a similar
                                                                                                                                   acquisition task for spine US. They implemented a confidence map (79) as part of the reward
                                                                                                                                   function to include an image quality assessment in the optimization loop. Instead of using US
                                                                                                                                   images as the observation, Ning et al. (80) utilized a scenery image as the state representation and
                                                                                                                                   employed an RL-based proximal policy optimization approach to realize stable localization of the
                                                                                                                                   standard plane. However, the generalization ability of the aforementioned approaches is limited
                                                                                                                                   by the insufficient number of training data. To address this problem, Bi et al. (81) proposed to
                                                                                                                                   utilize a segmentation network to keep only the task-related features and wipe out the unnecessary
                                                                                                                                   background information from the US image. The training can then be performed with simulated
                                                                                                                                   artificial data rather than acquiring real patients’ US volumes. Instead of using the segmented
                                                                                                                                   mask as an intermediate space to bridge between the simulation environment and real scenario,
                                                                                                                                   Li et al. (82) proposed to use simulated US images from CT as an intermediate domain for training
                                                                                                                                   of RL agent. During the inference stage, the real US images are transferred to the US simulation
                                                                                                                                   domain using a generative adversarial network (GAN) so that the training of RL can be done
                                                                                                                                   solely in the CT space.

                                                                                                                                   3.2.2. Learning from demonstration. LfD is another approach to achieve autonomous US
                                                                                                                                   examinations, where the model learns directly from experts’ demonstrations. LfD methods can
                                                                                                                             342   Bi et al.
                                                                                                                             be further categorized into guidance networks, where the model predicts actions based on US
                                                                                                                             images, and assessment networks, which estimate preference scores for individual US images to
                                                                                                                             reveal the intentions of sonographers during screening. As a valuable side benefit, the latter one
                                                                                                                             can further be used to indicate “the language of sonography” (28).
                                                                                                                                 To mimic how sonographers perform US examinations, Droste et al. (30) proposed US-
Downloaded from www.annualreviews.org. University of Southampton (ar-182708) IP: 152.78.0.24 On: Thu, 29 Oct 2026 14:49:28




                                                                                                                             GuideNet to predict the action of the operator as well as the goal position of the standard
                                                                                                                             plane using imitation learning. The demonstrations consist of paired US frames and the ori-
                                                                                                                             entations of the probe recorded from an inertial measurement unit sensor. A gated recurrent
                                                                                                                             unit was implemented to exploit the information of sequential data. However, it remains a large
                                                                                                                             challenge to extract practical clues from the US images for action prediction. As a result, Men
                                                                                                                             et al. (83) integrated the gaze signal of the operator into the network to provide extra guidance.
                                                                                                                             The proposed Multimodal-GuideNet is able to simultaneously estimate the gaze of the operator
                                                                                                                             and the probe motion. Instead of only providing instructions to the operator during scanning,
                                                                                                                             Deng et al. (84) implemented the learned policy directly from demonstrations to control the
                                                                                                                             robot. The US frames together with contact forces and probe positions were recorded as expert
                                                                                                                             demonstrations.
                                                                                                                                 Rather than cloning the movement of the expert from the given demonstrations, another way
                                                                                                                             of achieving LfD is through inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) (85), which infers a reward func-
                                                                                                                             tion from demonstrations. Regarding US standard plane acquisition, such a reward function refers
                                                                                                                             to the preference of sonographers, demonstrating the anatomical and diagnostic value of the US
                                                                                                                             image. The common assumption for IRL is the positive correlation between the reward value and
                                                                                                                             the occurrence frequency of a given state in demonstrations (86). However, in most cases, the US
                                                                                                                             navigation is not optimal, which indicates the existence of a large number of redundant actions
                                                                                                                             in the demonstrations. To address the problem of suboptimal demonstrations, Burke et al. (87)
                                                                                                                             proposed a probabilistic temporal ranking method. It assumed that the US image in the latter
                                                                                                                             stage of the demonstration is more likely to have higher diagnostic importance than the image
                                                                                                                             in an earlier stage. Such an assumption is realistic for US screening, where the search normally
                                                                                                                             terminates at the point when the standard plane is properly visualized. By relating the importance
                                                                                                                             of the US image to its relative position to the end point of the demonstration, Jiang et al. (28)
                                                                                                                             proposed a global probabilistic spatial ranking. A feature disentanglement technique was also in-
                                                                                                                             troduced to the network to improve its performance on unseen scenarios of ex vivo phantoms and
                                                                                                                             in vivo carotid US data.

                                                                                                                             4. ADVANCEMENTS TOWARD INTELLIGENCE
                                                                                                                             IN ROBOTIC ULTRASOUND
                                                                                                                             In this section, we demonstrate some important aspects that need to be properly addressed for
                                                                                                                             RUSSs, i.e., data scarcity, the necessity of integrating physics into the design of deep learning
                                                                                                                             networks, and efficient data representation.

                                                                                                                             4.1. Data Scarcity
                                                                                                                             For RUSSs to be successfully applied in clinical practice, they must be both robust and reliable.
                                                                                                                             However, achieving these attributes presents several challenges. A primary obstacle is the limited
                                                                                                                             availability of ground truth data essential for training machine learning algorithms. Traditional
                                                                                                                             approaches to data collection not only consume significant time and resources but often necessitate
                                                                                                                             expert involvement for accurate labeling. This creates bottlenecks in developing machine learning
                                                                                                                             models. This section presents methodologies to either circumvent or minimize the requirement
                                                                                                                             for labeled data.


                                                                                                                                                                  www.annualreviews.org • Machine Learning in Robotic Ultrasound Imaging   343
                                                                                                                                   4.1.1. Ultrasound simulation. By employing physics-based US simulations, researchers can
                                                                                                                                   generate a wealth of synthetic but realistic data, serving as ground truth for machine learning
                                                                                                                                   models. There are three main approaches for US simulation, i.e., interpolative methods, physics-
                                                                                                                                   based generative models, and deep learning–based techniques. These methods aim to generate
                                                                                                                                   realistic 2D or 3D images by capturing the complexities of tissue interactions with US waves.
Downloaded from www.annualreviews.org. University of Southampton (ar-182708) IP: 152.78.0.24 On: Thu, 29 Oct 2026 14:49:28




                                                                                                                                       Interpolative methods, as implemented by d’Aulignac et al. (88) and Goksel & Salcudean (89),
                                                                                                                                   serve as a fast and efficient means of image reconstruction. These techniques employ previously
                                                                                                                                   acquired US images and use them as a basis to generate new images through interpolation al-
                                                                                                                                   gorithms. The approach excels in quick image processing and realistic domain representation.
                                                                                                                                   However, it struggles to accurately portray directional artifacts, and its capability to produce novel
                                                                                                                                   views is constrained by the set of recorded images.
                                                                                                                                       When it comes to physics-based generative simulation, wave simulation serves as one of the
                                                                                                                                   most accurate, albeit computationally demanding, approaches. One of the most popular libraries,
                                                                                                                                   developed by Treeby & Cox (90), is the k-Wave toolbox, which relies on solving coupled first-order
                                                                                                                                   acoustic equations in the k-space pseudospectral time domain.
                                                                                                                                       Following a different, computationally less expensive approach, convolution-based methods
                                                                                                                                   excel in visualizing subwavelength tissue structures by convolving a point spread function with
                                                                                                                                   a scatterer map (91). While effective for microscopic interactions, they lack accuracy in macro-
                                                                                                                                   scopic correlations. Ray tracing, leveraging geometric optics principles, fills this gap by modeling
                                                                                                                                   US propagation as rays interacting with tissue. Combining convolution with ray tracing of-
                                                                                                                                   fers a comprehensive approach, capturing both microscopic and macroscopic interactions in US
                                                                                                                                   simulations (92).
                                                                                                                                       Additionally, by leveraging presegmented medical imaging data, patient-specific US simula-
                                                                                                                                   tion can be acquired. For instance, Salehi et al. (93) used segmented MRI data as input and applied
                                                                                                                                   convolutional ray tracing for appearance optimization. Velikova et al. (64) used this approach for
                                                                                                                                   training a segmentation network only on simulated US data, based on CT and associated labels. An
                                                                                                                                   enhanced version of the ray-tracing simulation was presented by Mattausch et al. (94). Combin-
                                                                                                                                   ing surface models with Monte Carlo surface interaction sampling for macroscopic interactions
                                                                                                                                   and convolution-based simulations for scattering phenomena enables the creation of an accurate
                                                                                                                                   representation of the underlying texture.
                                                                                                                                       Deep learning–based techniques, particularly GANs, offer a data-driven mechanism for US
                                                                                                                                   image synthesis. Research such as that by Tom & Sheet (95) and Hu et al. (96) has exploited
                                                                                                                                   the capabilities of GANs to generate highly realistic US images. In addition, the application of
                                                                                                                                   CycleGANs, demonstrated by Vitale et al. (97), has shown potential in reconciling the discrepan-
                                                                                                                                   cies between simulated and real US images, making this a promising technique for training and
                                                                                                                                   validating machine learning models in robotic US applications.

                                                                                                                                   4.1.2. Data augmentation. To increase the diversity of training data, another intuitive approach
                                                                                                                                   is data augmentation. Out-of-domain and synthetic images are created based on the training
                                                                                                                                   data using either conventional image transformation methods or deep learning–based genera-
                                                                                                                                   tive models. As a traditional augmentation method, Zhang et al. (98) proposed a deep stacked
                                                                                                                                   transformation framework, which has been tested on different medical imaging modalities and
                                                                                                                                   demonstrated promising results in increasing generalization capability. Focusing on a kidney US
                                                                                                                                   segmentation task, Yin et al. (99) utilized a nonrigid image registration method to register and alter
                                                                                                                                   the shape of one kidney with other kidney samples in the training dataset so that the anatomical
                                                                                                                                   correctness of the augmented kidney can be guaranteed. Lee et al. (100) introduced a nonlin-
                                                                                                                                   ear mixed-example augmentation method by fusing and mixing images from different classes to
                                                                                                                                   generate new training samples for standard plane classification of fetal US.

                                                                                                                             researchers have also implemented these models in the area of US image analysis (101, 102). To
                                                                                                                             increase interpretability during image generation, Shi et al. (103) integrated expert annotations
                                                                                                                             into a GAN to generate anatomy- and domain-specific synthetic US images for thyroid nodule
                                                                                                                             classification. For a similar application, Zhang et al. (104) implemented a progressive GAN-based

                                                                                                                             the label information and the image embedding are disentangled to increase the manipulability
                                                                                                                             during synthesis. To realize deformable augmentation for 3D US volumes, Wulff et al. (106) im-

                                                                                                                             4.2. Physics-Inspired Machine Learning for Ultrasound
                                                                                                                             In the previous sections, we have shown how the latest breakthroughs in the field of machine
                                                                                                                             learning and deep learning have elevated the conception of RUSSs to a higher level. Nonetheless,
                                                                                                                             medical images are different from normal optical images. The intensity value of each pixel no
                                                                                                                             longer represents only the color but also reflects the unique properties of human tissues. US, as
                                                                                                                             a mechanical wave, has a much slower propagation speed than X-rays or light, which makes the
                                                                                                                             resulting images very vulnerable to disturbances and full of noise. Such issues have posed extra
                                                                                                                             challenges for a comprehensive understanding of US images. Thus, researchers have attempted to
                                                                                                                             exploit extra clues from either the physiological context or US formation principles. Physiology-
                                                                                                                             inspired approaches refer to network architectures in which prior clinical knowledge is explicitly
                                                                                                                             integrated to facilitate a deep understanding of the US images. On the other hand, taking the
                                                                                                                             US formation principles into consideration when designing the network structures appears to
                                                                                                                             be another promising solution. By doing so, the US images are treated not as a distribution of
                                                                                                                             grayscale values but as a representation of human tissues.

                                                                                                                             4.2.1. Physiology-inspired image analysis. Unlike normal optical images, US images are hard
                                                                                                                             to interpret without sufficient medical knowledge. A deep understanding of anatomy is one of the
                                                                                                                             key features that differentiates experienced sonographers from trainees. Inspired by the interpre-
                                                                                                                             tation process of the medical experts, physiological knowledge is explicitly integrated into the deep
                                                                                                                             learning–based US analysis network. To improve the segmentation performance of vessels in US
                                                                                                                             images, Jiang et al. (25) proposed optical flow–based U-Net (OF-UNet), which explicitly utilizes
                                                                                                                             the continuity of vessels. Guidance is provided to the network based on the optical flow image and
                                                                                                                             the predicted segmentation mask from the previous frame. During breast US examination, sono-
                                                                                                                             graphers utilize the information of previous frames to determine whether a lesion-like anatomy
                                                                                                                             is indeed a diseased tissue or not. Based on such observations, Yu et al. (108) proposed UltraDet,
                                                                                                                             which uses inverse optical flow progress to trace back in previous frames in order to detect negative
                                                                                                                             symptoms that indicate false-positive classifications. To produce anatomically plausible seman-
                                                                                                                             tic segmentation of cardiac US, Painchaud et al. (109) proposed a framework to guarantee the
                                                                                                                             anatomical correctness of resulting segmentation maps. By transferring all the ground truth la-
                                                                                                                             bels from image space to latent space using a conditioned VAE, a manifold of valid cardiac shapes

                                                                                                                                                                   www.annualreviews.org • Machine Learning in Robotic Ultrasound Imaging   345
                                                                                                                                   in the feature space can be determined. Then, a mapping between the anatomically incorrect seg-
                                                                                                                                   mentation mask and the manifold of valid shapes is performed to improve the shape correctness.
                                                                                                                                   To correct the force-induced US image deformation, Jiang et al. (110, 111) explicitly considered
                                                                                                                                   tissue stiffness maps to compute an accurate and consistent anatomical geometry in RUSSs.
                                                                                                                                       To resolve the challenge of detecting tiny tissues in US images (e.g., nerves), Dou et al. (112)

                                                                                                                                   the segmented surrounding organs (i.e., carotid artery, thyroid, and trachea), a Bayesian shape
                                                                                                                                   alignment module is applied to generate a region proposal for locating the recurrent laryngeal
                                                                                                                                   nerve. To generate temporal consistent segmentation results for cardiac US, Painchaud et al. (113)
                                                                                                                                   proposed a framework to perform postprocessing on the segmentation results. An autoencoder was
                                                                                                                                   introduced to map the segmentation masks into an interpretable latent space. For a sequence of
                                                                                                                                   cardiac US images, a smoothing process is applied to each attribute of the latent representations
                                                                                                                                   until a temporal consistency metric is satisfied. The periodic motions of arteries are frequently
                                                                                                                                   utilized by doctors as a strong prior for detection and localization in clinical practice. Huang
                                                                                                                                   et al. (17) employed such prior knowledge in the network design to facilitate the segmentation
                                                                                                                                   results for small arteries (i.e., radial arteries). Motion magnification techniques were introduced
                                                                                                                                   to extract a pulsation map, which highlights the region where the periodic motions occur. The
                                                                                                                                   pulsation map then serves as an attention map to guide the segmentation network.

                                                                                                                                   4.2.2. Ultrasound physics-inspired image analysis. The grayscale values in US images rep-
                                                                                                                                   resent the intensity of the reflected US waves. For visualization reasons, the source signals are
                                                                                                                                   mapped onto a color space between 0 and 255, so that they are interpretable by humans. A lot of
                                                                                                                                   information is lost during such a visualization process. However, this is not necessarily true for
                                                                                                                                   machines. With growing computational power, it is becoming feasible to learn how to interpret
                                                                                                                                   the source data directly. Therefore, Gare et al. (114) proposed W-Net, combining raw radio fre-
                                                                                                                                   quency data with B-mode images to optimize the segmentation results. By extracting information
                                                                                                                                   from the radio frequency data, W-Net is able to surpass the performance of a U-Net with a smaller
                                                                                                                                   network size. Another distinct property inherent to US is the Doppler effect. Doppler images are
                                                                                                                                   widely used in clinical practice to detect and measure blood flow. The Doppler effect has also been
                                                                                                                                   applied to provide weak supervision for the vessel segmentation network (115). Jiang et al. (62)
                                                                                                                                   later combined Doppler images with B-mode US images to provide extra guidance to the net-
                                                                                                                                   work for radial artery segmentation. Since the Doppler effect is unstable during autonomous US
                                                                                                                                   scanning, a RUSS was designed with the capability to reacquire a high-quality Doppler signal by
                                                                                                                                   performing out-of-plane fan motion.
                                                                                                                                       A significant challenge in US imaging is the scarcity or absence of information in regions with
                                                                                                                                   attenuation or shadowing, a factor that substantially impedes the interpretability of these areas.
                                                                                                                                   Based on the principles of US wave propagation, Karamalis et al. (79) introduced confidence maps,
                                                                                                                                   which modeled the confidence assessment task as a random walk problem. The resulting confi-
                                                                                                                                   dence map was then utilized by several RUSSs as a criterion for US image quality optimization (32,
                                                                                                                                   72, 116). Later, Klein & Wells (117) proposed to integrate radio frequency data into the random
                                                                                                                                   walk–based confidence estimation. Meng et al. (118) introduced a learning-based method to es-
                                                                                                                                   timate the shadow areas for fetal US using a weakly supervised learning approach since it is not
                                                                                                                                   feasible to provide pixel-level shadow annotations from human operators.
                                                                                                                                       Inspired by physics-based US simulation from CT segmentation maps, Velikova et al. (64)
                                                                                                                                   proposed to compose an intermediate CT–US space through US simulation parameterization.
                                                                                                                                   The segmentation is performed solely in the intermediate space, while CT and US images are
                                                                                                                                   transferred to such a common anatomical space through a specific parameterized US simulator
                                                                                                                                   during training and through a GAN during inference, respectively. Going one step further, instead

                                                                                                                             4.3. Efficient Data Representation
                                                                                                                             The effectiveness of a CNN is dependent mainly on its ability to extract distinct features. The
                                                                                                                             redundancy in feature space largely hinders the inference performance of deep learning models,
                                                                                                                             especially when the diversity of the training dataset is limited. To increase the conciseness of the
                                                                                                                             data representation, a disentanglement between task-related and task-irrelevant features is an es-
                                                                                                                             sential step toward robust and reliable intelligent assessment of US images. Using V-Net (121) as
                                                                                                                             the backbone, Degel et al. (122) proposed a 3D segmentation framework that uses a discrimina-
                                                                                                                             tor to predict the specific domain of the input US volume, and the corresponding adversarial loss
                                                                                                                             forces the segmentation network to ignore the domain information. Meng et al. (123) also utilized
                                                                                                                             adversarial loss to regularize the network to extract distinct features for corresponding classifica-
                                                                                                                             tion tasks separately. The performance of the proposed segmentation network was validated on
                                                                                                                             fetal US standard plane classification and shadow detection tasks. To increase the cross-domain ro-
                                                                                                                             bustness of the network, Meng et al. (124) implemented feature space clustering and demonstrated
                                                                                                                             promising results in handling the domain shift in fetal US standard plane detection. Combining
                                                                                                                             feature space alignment and an adversarial learning strategy, Ying et al. (125) proposed a classifi-
                                                                                                                             cation scheme for benign and malignant thyroid nodules and showed high generalization ability
                                                                                                                             across different US machines. For the same task, instead of using a distance-based feature align-
                                                                                                                             ment method, Zhang et al. (126) used a graph convolution network (127) to assess the relations
                                                                                                                             between the source and target domain data and further realize cross-device generalization. An-
                                                                                                                             other, more intuitive way to decouple the domain- and task-related features is through minimizing
                                                                                                                             a metric that can directly measure the shared information between the two variables. To this
                                                                                                                             end, mutual information (128), which measures the dependency between two random variables,
                                                                                                                             has been applied in various classification tasks in computer vision (129–131). In the US field,
                                                                                                                             Meng et al. (132) realized unseen domain generalization for fetal US standard plane detection
                                                                                                                             through mutual information minimization. Following a similar concept, Bi et al. (133) extended
                                                                                                                             the usage of mutual information to segmentation networks by introducing a cross-reconstruction
                                                                                                                             strategy.

                                                                                                                             5. OPEN CHALLENGES AND FUTURE PERSPECTIVES
                                                                                                                             Significant progress has been made toward achieving the ultimate goal of developing intelligent
                                                                                                                             robotic sonographers by enhancing the understanding of the environment using advanced ma-
                                                                                                                             chine learning algorithms. However, some challenges remain for the research community, such
                                                                                                                             as ethical and legal issues. Apart from those, emerging hardware design in the US area has re-
                                                                                                                             vealed brand-new potential for future research. In this section, we discuss the ethical problems
                                                                                                                             and provide insights for potential directions to stimulate future studies.

                                                                                                                             5.1. Ethics and Regulations
                                                                                                                             Given the rising enthusiasm within the market, the scientific community, and the medical field,
                                                                                                                             ethical and regulatory guidance is becoming increasingly important. A clear definition of respon-
                                                                                                                             sibility for such emerging systems could boost the research in the field of clinical translation,

                                                                                                                                                                   www.annualreviews.org • Machine Learning in Robotic Ultrasound Imaging   347
                                                                                                                                   in order to bring the prototypes from the laboratories to the market. The demand for proper
                                                                                                                                   regulations has been discussed and emphasized by pioneers in the area of applied medical
                                                                                                                                   robotics (6, 134–138).
                                                                                                                                       To tackle the ethical, regulatory, and legal issues for autonomous medical robots, Yang et al. (2)
                                                                                                                                   defined six levels of autonomy: no autonomy, robot assistance, task autonomy, conditional auton-
Downloaded from www.annualreviews.org. University of Southampton (ar-182708) IP: 152.78.0.24 On: Thu, 29 Oct 2026 14:49:28




                                                                                                                                   omy, high autonomy, and full autonomy. This taxonomy has been widely used to categorize the
                                                                                                                                   relevant techniques at their respective levels (136, 139). Different degrees of autonomy require
                                                                                                                                   distinct regulatory frameworks. In the case of medical robots with low autonomy levels, account-
                                                                                                                                   ability rests primarily with the clinicians, who maintain complete control over the system. Due
                                                                                                                                   to this well-defined attribution of responsibility, the transition to commercialization for such sys-
                                                                                                                                   tems tends to be more successful than it is for other systems. The da Vinci robot serves as the most
                                                                                                                                   prominent example of this success.
                                                                                                                                       Concerning the regulation of medical systems that exhibit a certain level of autonomy, two
                                                                                                                                   leading standard-development organizations—the International Organization for Standardization
                                                                                                                                   (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC)—have published their inaugural
                                                                                                                                   joint standard (IEC/TR 60601-4-1) to address the safety, transparency, and trustworthiness of
                                                                                                                                   contemporary medical devices (136). Unlike surgical robots, robotic sonographers are noninvasive
                                                                                                                                   and interact with patients who are not under anesthesia; thus, more specialized regulations are
                                                                                                                                   necessary.

                                                                                                                                   5.2. Emerging Ultrasound Imaging Systems
                                                                                                                                   The fundamental developments in new types of US sensing hardware will significantly impact
                                                                                                                                   current clinical intervention procedures and bring new opportunities. In this section, we highlight
                                                                                                                                   the emerging optical US systems and soft ultrasonic patches.

                                                                                                                                   5.2.1. Optical ultrasound. To enhance the versatility of US technologies, integrating opti-
                                                                                                                                   cal systems with acoustic methods offers an intriguing path forward. By leveraging laser-induced
                                                                                                                                   thermal expansion within biological tissues, optoacoustic imaging enables high-resolution,
                                                                                                                                   molecule-specific imaging. This capability is crucial for distinguishing various tissue types, iden-
                                                                                                                                   tifying pathological conditions, and pinpointing specific molecular markers (140). Building on
                                                                                                                                   recent progress, advancements in all-optical US (OpUS) transducers show particular promise
                                                                                                                                   for use in minimally invasive surgery. OpUS generates US via a fiber optic transducer using the
                                                                                                                                   photoacoustic effect and captures tissue reflections through optical interferometry, akin to op-
                                                                                                                                   tical coherence tomography (OCT) methods (141). These transducers excel in their remarkable
                                                                                                                                   miniaturization while preserving high-resolution imaging and mechanical flexibility, making them
                                                                                                                                   especially useful in endovascular applications like thrombus imaging (142).
                                                                                                                                       Notably, this technology allows real-time feedback, creating opportunities for robotic mon-
                                                                                                                                   itoring in surgical procedures and image-based diagnostics. A research team at Johns Hopkins
                                                                                                                                   University was the first to demonstrate a robotic tracking system guided by optoacoustic signals
                                                                                                                                   (143, 144). The approach involves embedding an optical fiber into the distal end of a surgical
                                                                                                                                   instrument. This setup allows a robotic manipulator, equipped with a US probe, to monitor and
                                                                                                                                   trace the position of the tool tip through analysis of the optoacoustic signal. The authors applied
                                                                                                                                   this approach to robotic catheters (143) and needle tracking (144).

                                                                                                                                   5.2.2. Ultrasonic patch. Given the recent advancements in unconventional US sensors such as
                                                                                                                                   US patches (145, 146), the field of US applications could be significantly expanded. These emerg-
                                                                                                                                   ing soft US patch technologies, thanks to their size and flexibility, facilitate extended monitoring
                                                                                                                                   of patients. This allows for continuous capture of physiological data, aiding in accurate clinical

                                                                                                                             348   Bi et al.
                                                                                                                             diagnoses and close tracking of disease progression. Such extended monitoring was previously
                                                                                                                             expensive and inefficient when utilizing traditional, bulky US probes.
                                                                                                                                In 2018, Hu et al. (145) presented a soft US patch consisting of a 10 × 10 array of piezoelectric
                                                                                                                             transducers for 3D imaging of deep tissues (145). They reported that the patch has excellent elec-
                                                                                                                             tromechanical coupling, minimal cross-talk, and more than 50% stretchability. Wang et al. (146)

                                                                                                                             beneath the skin. In another study, Wang et al. (147) presented a bioadhesive US array to provide
                                                                                                                             48 h of continuous imaging of various organs, including blood vessels, muscle, heart, diaphragm,
                                                                                                                             lung, and so on. Focusing on cardiac imaging, Hu et al. (148) proposed a wearable US patch

                                                                                                                             6. CONCLUSION
                                                                                                                             This review has focused on highlighting the significance of machine learning and deep learn-
                                                                                                                             ing technologies in enhancing the intelligence of RUSSs. To provide an overview of the ongoing
                                                                                                                             developments in the field, we initially discussed the specific robotic mechanisms and control sys-
                                                                                                                             tems employed in RUSSs, as well as their clinical applications. Modern robotic systems and control
                                                                                                                             methods have already demonstrated superhuman levels of reproducibility. However, they still face
                                                                                                                             challenges in understanding the physiological aspects of human tissue and diseases, different imag-
                                                                                                                             ing modalities, imaging physics, and artifacts. Additionally, there is a need to improve their ability
                                                                                                                             to promptly reason and make correct decisions in response to expected or unexpected changes
                                                                                                                             during acquisition. To achieve this, we discussed two categories of methods for action reasoning,
                                                                                                                             one based on implicitly interpreting input data and the other on explicitly interpreting it. Toward
                                                                                                                             the conclusion of the review, we also discussed the open challenges and future perspectives.
                                                                                                                                The development and deployment of intelligent RUSSs will be a challenging and exciting
                                                                                                                             endeavor. We believe robotic sonography and autonomous US examination represent the next
                                                                                                                             frontier in medical robotics and hold significant promise in future clinical practices. Furthermore,
                                                                                                                             we have highlighted the crucial importance of advancements in the area of ethics and regulations
                                                                                                                             and also emphasized the need for collaborative efforts from the scientific, industrial, and clinical
                                                                                                                             communities.
