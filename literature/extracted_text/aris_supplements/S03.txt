Autonomous robotic ultrasound scanning on complex surfaces: Automatic
trajectory generation and disturbance observer-based model predictive
admittance control

Keywords:                                                    Robotic ultrasound scanning facilitates clinical diagnosis by reducing operator burden and enhancing imaging re-
Trajectory planning                                          producibility. To achieve stable human-robot interaction on complex anatomical surfaces, this paper proposes an
Admittance control                                           autonomous compliant ultrasound scanning framework. First, an equidistant trajectory planning method based
Disturbance observer
                                                             on 3D point clouds is presented to satisfy probe pose constraints. By establishing an anisotropic geodesic distance
Model predictive control
Robotic ultrasound scanning
                                                             ﬁeld to characterize the surface topology of the human body, this approach achieves uniform global coverage
                                                             and avoids abrupt orientation shifts. Second, a disturbance observer-based model predictive admittance control
                                                             (DO-MPAC) scheme is developed to maintain a stable and compliant interaction force. Within this scheme, envi-
                                                             ronmental uncertainties are estimated and compensated for through an extended state observer (ESO), and the
                                                             dependency of the force error dynamics on the admittance parameters is mitigated through the design of a vir-
                                                             tual control law. Furthermore, a predictive optimization framework is utilized to guarantee optimal force track-
                                                             ing. Finally, simulations and physical experiments evaluate the proposed system against conventional, adaptive,
                                                             and sliding mode admittance controllers. Results demonstrate that in complex curved environments, DO-MPAC
                                                             achieves a faster transient response and higher steady-state precision, reducing the maximum force deviation
                                                             (MFD), integral of absolute error (IAE), and integral of time-weighted absolute error (ITAE) by at least 88.1%,
                                                             45.6%, and 43.0%, respectively.
