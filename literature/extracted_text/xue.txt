Model predictive variable impedance control towards safe robotic
interaction in unknown disturbance-rich environments
Junyuan Xue a,b, Wenyu Liang b,∗, Yan Wu b, Tong Heng Lee a

a Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, National University of Singapore, Singapore, 117582, Singapore
b Institute for Infocomm Research (I2R), A*STAR, Singapore, 138632, Singapore


A R T I C L E   I N F O            A B S T R A C T


Keywords:                                       Robotic systems have evolved to handle various significant interaction tasks in different environments. Under
Impedance control                                  these conditions, the involvement of humans in the environment drastically complicates such interaction tasks;
Adaptive control                                    as the safety of humans should be prioritized while seeking to achieve the desired task aim. It is thus paramount
Model predictive control                              that appropriate developments should be pursued with specific considerations for such safety-performance-
Safe interaction
                                              balanced interaction tasks on unknown soft environments (e.g., humans). Towards this end, we present an
                                                adaptive robust, and passive control scheme based on model predictive control and variable impedance
                                                   control that addresses this challenge. Under this control scheme, during robotic interaction tasks with complex
                                              environments (e.g., humans), the presented development and design incorporate safety thresholds that are
                                                     carefully satisfied via impedance adaptation, and realized by a safety-related mode-switching mechanism. Once
                                                 the safety thresholds are satisfied, task performance is then focused on. Additionally, a real-time adaptive
                                                 robust parameter estimator is designed and utilized to estimate the environment contact model for the model
                                                    predictive control, and thus this control scheme is robust against disturbances (e.g., which would invariably
                                                       arise from the inevitable small bounded human motions) during the interaction tasks. Finally, the key safety
                                          and performance attainments of the proposed control scheme are verified via experiments. The experiments
                                                 are conducted on two silicone rubber models and a human arm. These show that the proposed control scheme
                                                       effectively outperformed various currently available control schemes in these interaction tasks with unknown
                                             environment contact models, and bounded but unpredictable environment position shifts, such as in robotic
                                                ultrasound scanning applications.

   However, neither IC nor VIC inherently incorporates any con-
straints, potentially leading to failures in tasks requiring the robotic
system to obey constraints [19,20]. Model predictive control (MPC),
initially developed for the chemical process industry in the late 1970s
[21], has become a widely adopted technique [22] due to its ability to
optimize subject to multiple constraints. In recent years, MPC has also
been favored for interaction control, thanks to its optimization with
constraints [23]. Therefore, MPC can be considered for integration with
IC or VIC to complement their functionalities and address the constraint
issues.
   Several works have successfully integrated MPC with IC or VIC,
demonstrating the effectiveness of this combined approach in robotic
interaction tasks. In [24], an admittance control  is designed based
on MPC with constraints of velocity and interaction force. In [25],
the objective function for MPC is constructed based on the desired
impedance model with user-designed constraints. Furthermore, the
MPC is adopted in [26] to optimize the impedance parameters for
tracking error minimization. In [27], a learned model for MPC adapts
the impedance parameters to realize the VIC. In [28], the MPC  is                          Fig. 1. A robotic interaction task with a human.

                                                              VIC is designed to smoothly switch between safety and performance

                                                            upon abnormal interaction force or interaction force changing rate.

                                                             bounded disturbances, and the controlled system would remain passive
