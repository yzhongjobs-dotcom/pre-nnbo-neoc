Objective and proof scope

Within the local regular domain of the identified R15 model, small environment-descriptor changes permit the ANEOC predictor, under computable conditions, to generate executable near-optimal closed-loop impedance policies with explicit error bounds without resolving the full HJB–KKT system. Sufficient conditions determine correction, re-anchoring or re-solving when prediction alone is inadequate.

Distinguish the finite-dimensional root Z_N^*, feedback π_{N,*} recovered from its critic, and continuous optimal value V_D^* in a declared comparison domain. They are not interchangeable. Core conclusions concern the nominal model; supported budgets include identification, model and execution errors. Proving conditional theorems and certifying specific R15 numerical configurations are separate tasks.

The recorded proof-writing sequence completed the following items, stopping for review after each:
1. Local certified domain and regular finite-dimensional HJB–KKT branch.
2. Inexact single-step ANEOC root-prediction error.
3. Root-to-policy and actual-impedance error.
4. Policy-to-nominal-closed-loop trajectory error.
5. Computable feasibility certificates for the candidate itself.
6. Local near-optimality separating predictor and critic/configuration errors.
7. Multistep error recursion and sufficient publish/correct/re-anchor/re-solve triggers.
8. Conditional computational advantage over cached-J chord and one-step Newton/RTI.

No maximal certified radius, global HJB optimality, global stability, finite termination of all recovery paths or universal speed advantage is required. Item 1 certifies roots only; closed-loop release requires the conditions of items 3–7.

1. Local certified domain and regular finite-dimensional HJB–KKT branch

Starting from a possibly inexact anchor, this item gives checkable sufficient conditions for existence, uniqueness, preserved node active sets, and uniform inverse/sensitivity bounds on one nearby parameterized root branch.

1.1 Fixed problem and variables

Fix robot model, horizon T, cost weights, reference degree, critic basis/scales, n_W nodes (t_i,x_i), and m_u=17 action-constraint rows. Retain descriptor

  η=(θ_0,θ_f,b,γ_0,γdot_0,ρ)，                         （P1.1）

where θ_j=(E_j^*,μ_j,k_d,j) and ρ contains reference-polynomial coefficients; p_η=9+7(p_r+1). θ_0,θ_f are given nominal-problem values, not necessarily unknown true tissue parameters. Actual state x is the feedback argument and is not included in η.

Write action slack as s_i^u to distinguish it from scan position s_i. Let

  V_N(t,x;W,η)=Φ(x;η)+ψ(t,x)^T W，
  p_i=∇_xΦ(x_i;η)+J_ψ(t_i,x_i)^T W，
  A_i=A_u(t_i,x_i;η)， b_i=b_u(t_i,x_i;η)，
  G_i=G_g(t_i,x_i;η)， f_i=f_0(t_i,x_i;η)+G_i u_i，
  B_i=ψ_t(t_i,x_i)^T W+ℓ_0(t_i,x_i;η)+u_i^T R_g u_i+p_i^T f_i，
  S_i=2R_g u_i+G_i^T p_i+A_i^T λ_i，
  s_i^u=b_i-A_i u_i，
  Z=(W,{u_i,λ_i}_{i=1}^{n_W})，
  F(Z,η)=col_i(B_i,S_i,λ_i⊙s_i^u)。                    （P1.2）

R_g≻0; n_Z=n_W(1+4+m_u), so F:R^{n_Z}×R^{p_η}→R^{n_Z}.
Exact algebraic roots must also satisfy λ_i≥0 and s_i^u≥0. Denote the finite-system root Z_N^*(η); N only distinguishes finite approximation from continuous HJB objects.

Use predetermined fixed normalization for variables, parameters and residuals. Positive diagonal D_Z,D_η,D_F can define
  Z_phys=Z_ref+D_Z z， η_phys=η_ref+D_η e，
  F_scaled(z,e)=D_F^{-1} F_phys(Z_phys,η_phys)。
For simplicity Z,η,F below denote normalized objects, with Euclidean/induced operator norms. λ_i,s_i^u and contact margins are physical scalar functions of these coordinates, with scale transforms included in derivative bounds. Recover physical sensitivity as D_Z C_η D_η^{-1}. Positive row scaling preserves roots and complementarity.

1.2 Checkable local conditions

Given numerical anchor (Zbar,ηbar), do not assume F(Zbar,ηbar)=0. Choose finite r_Z>0,r_η>0 and construct

  B_Z={Z:||Z-Zbar||≤r_Z}，
  P_cert={η:||η-ηbar||≤r_η}，
  Q_cert=B_Z×P_cert。                                  （P1.3）

This is a local root-variable/parameter box (a product of two balls), not the robot-state feasible set. Complete deployment certification additionally needs trajectory and performance certificates.

Condition A: the same model expression gives F∈C² on an open neighborhood of Q_cert.

For R15, verify the following interfaces:

(a) Every contact evaluation entering F satisfies 0<a_min≤a≤a_max<ℓ_ref on a fixed open coverage branch. For some ε_c>0, |r-a| and |r+a| are at least ε_c, with r=b-s. Fix branch labels at each evaluation position; different nodes need not share a coverage branch.

(b) All sgn(v) evaluations retain a fixed sliding direction positively separated from v=0. Robot mass remains invertible, orientation stays in a smooth chart, and other coefficients have the smoothness needed for F∈C².

(c) Check dynamics/cost at (t_i,x_i), and contact evaluations in Φ(x_i;η) and its state gradient at terminal t=T. Checking only t_i is insufficient. Check actual evaluation positions for other piecewise expressions too. Nodes t_i are fixed; terminal evaluation means evaluating cost at x_i, not a predicted terminal trajectory state.

Appendix A.2 gives Ψ'(a)>0 with a positive lower bound on [a_min,a_max], so a=Ψ^{-1}(δ) is smooth nearby. Each open coverage formula in B–C is smooth; separation from edges bounds required higher derivatives. Together with (b),(c), this checks A. C¹ models may support first-order branches but do not certify the C² version needed for subsequent second-order prediction bounds.

Condition B: choose node active sets Aset_i and complements Iset_i and obtain strictly positive bounds

  λ_ij(Z)≥μ_λ>0，        j∈Aset_i，
  s_ij^u(Z,η)≥μ_s>0，    j∈Iset_i，
  for every (Z,η)∈Q_cert.                             (P1.4)

Omit conditions for empty index classes. Active slacks and inactive multipliers need not vanish away from roots; complementarity implies those equalities at exact roots. This allows residual-bearing Zbar without forcing a full ball onto a lower-dimensional active face.

Condition C: choose fixed R∈R^{n_Z×n_Z}, an approximate anchor-Jacobian inverse or equivalent operator. Obtain verified nonnegative q,β,B_η,L_ZZ,L_Zη,L_ηη such that

  sup_{Q_cert} ||I-RF_Z(Z,η)||≤q<1，                    （P1.5）
  sup_{η∈P_cert} ||R F(Zbar,η)||≤β，
  β+q r_Z<r_Z，                                       （P1.6）
  sup_{Q_cert}||F_η||≤B_η，
  sup_{Q_cert}||F_ZZ||≤L_ZZ，
  sup_{Q_cert}||F_Zη||≤L_Zη，
  sup_{Q_cert}||F_ηη||≤L_ηη。                           （P1.7）

Second-partial bounds use bilinear operator norms; R need not be the exact inverse. Condition (P1.5) itself implies invertibility of R and F_Z, so no unknown exact-root inverse defines the certificate. β must cover the whole parameter domain, not just the anchor residual at ηbar.

1.3 Theorem 1

Under A–C:

(i) Each η∈P_cert has exactly one root Z_N^*(η) in B_Z, with

  ||Z_N^*(η)-Zbar||≤β/(1-q)<r_Z。                       （P1.8）

(ii) The root satisfies all nodewise action KKT nonnegativity/complementarity conditions, active sets Aset_i, and strict margins at least μ_λ,μ_s. Each u_i^* uniquely minimizes the node Hamiltonian for W_N^*(η)=Π_W Z_N^*(η).

(iii) F_Z is invertible throughout Q_cert, with uniform bound

  ||F_Z^{-1}||≤κ，  κ=||R||/(1-q)。                     （P1.9）

(iv) Z_N^*(η) is C² in the interior of P_cert and locally extends as C² at its boundary. Sensitivity uniquely satisfies

  F_Z C_η=-F_η，  C_η=D_η Z_N^*，
  ||C_η||≤c_Z，  c_Z=κ B_η。                           （P1.10）

and

  ||D_η² Z_N^*||≤M_Z，
  M_Z=κ(L_ZZ c_Z²+2L_Zη c_Z+L_ηη)。                    （P1.11）

For η_1,η_2∈P_cert,

  ||Z_N^*(η_2)-Z_N^*(η_1)||≤c_Z||η_2-η_1||。            （P1.12）

Uniqueness concerns B_Z only and excludes no external roots. Nodewise Hamiltonian minimization is neither global W optimality nor exact continuous-HJB solution.

1.4 Proof

Step 1: invertibility and uniform inverse bound.

For any (Z,η)∈Q_cert let E=I-RF_Z. Since ||E||≤q<1, Σ_{k=0}^∞E^k converges absolutely and

  (RF_Z)^{-1}=(I-E)^{-1}=Σ_{k=0}^∞ E^k，
  ||(RF_Z)^{-1}||≤1/(1-q)。                            （P1.13）

Invertible square RF_Z implies both square factors R,F_Z are invertible. Also

  F_Z^{-1}=(RF_Z)^{-1}R，

which gives (P1.9), including approximate-inverse error.

Step 2: existence, uniqueness and location for every parameter.

Fix η∈P_cert and define the proof map

  T_η(Z)=Z-RF(Z,η)。                                   （P1.14）

Convexity of B_Z and the vector fundamental theorem of calculus give, for Z,Y∈B_Z,

  T_η(Z)-T_η(Y)
    =∫_0^1 [I-RF_Z(Y+s(Z-Y),η)](Z-Y) ds，

Thus ||T_η(Z)-T_η(Y)||≤q||Z-Y||. Moreover,

  ||T_η(Z)-Zbar||
    ≤||T_η(Z)-T_η(Zbar)||+||RF(Zbar,η)||
    ≤q r_Z+β<r_Z。                                    （P1.15）

Therefore T_η is a strict contraction mapping complete closed B_Z into itself. For explicit existence take any Z^0∈B_Z and Z^{k+1}=T_η(Z^k), yielding
  ||Z^{k+1}-Z^k||≤q^k||Z^1-Z^0||。
The geometric series converges, so iterates are Cauchy and converge in B_Z to Z_*. Continuity gives T_η(Z_*)=Z_*; invertible R gives F(Z_*,η)=0.
Two fixed points in B_Z have distance at most q times that distance, hence coincide. Every root is a fixed point, establishing uniqueness.

Finally apply to the fixed point
  ||Z_*-Zbar||≤q||Z_*-Zbar||+||RF(Zbar,η)||，
obtaining (P1.8). This iteration proves existence; it neither prescribes online execution nor establishes convergence of the later Newton corrector.

Step 3: strict complementarity and nodewise action minimization.

At a root, j∈Aset_i gives λ_ij^*≥μ_λ>0 by B; λ_ij^*s_ij^{u,*}=0 then gives s_ij^{u,*}=0.
For j∈Iset_i, s_ij^{u,*}≥μ_s>0 similarly gives λ_ij^*=0.
Thus every λ_i^*≥0 and s_i^{u,*}≥0, and the node active sets are exactly Aset_i.

Fix node W_N^*,η, let c_i=G_i^T p_i and Q_i(u)=u^T R_g u+c_i^T u. Root stationarity is
  ∇Q_i(u_i^*)=-A_i^T λ_i^*。
For any feasible A_i u≤b_i,

  Q_i(u)-Q_i(u_i^*)
    =(u-u_i^*)^T R_g(u-u_i^*)-(λ_i^*)^T A_i(u-u_i^*)
    =(u-u_i^*)^T R_g(u-u_i^*)
      +(λ_i^*)^T(b_i-A_i u)
    ≥(u-u_i^*)^T R_g(u-u_i^*)≥0。                      （P1.16）

The second equality uses (λ_i^*)^T(b_i-A_i u_i^*)=0. R_g≻0 makes the final term strictly positive for u≠u_i^*, giving the unique minimizer. Hamiltonian terms independent of u do not affect this.

Step 4: consistent global-in-domain root map and C² regularity.

First prove parameter continuity. For η_1,η_2∈P_cert, set Z_j=Z_N^*(η_j). The two fixed-point equations give

  ||Z_2-Z_1||
    ≤q||Z_2-Z_1||+||R|| B_η||η_2-η_1||。

The parameter segment lies in convex P_cert and Z_1∈B_Z, so (P1.7) applies. Rearrangement yields (P1.12), including continuity.

By (P1.8), every root is interior to B_Z. A and step 1 give C² F and invertible F_Z; the implicit-function theorem gives a C² root function nearby. Its values stay in B_Z for a sufficiently small parameter neighborhood, so step-2 uniqueness identifies it with Z_N^* on P_cert. These functions join into one C² branch; the open neighborhood in A gives boundary extensions.

Differentiating F(Z_N^*(η),η)=0 yields (P1.10); (P1.7),(P1.9) give ||C_η||≤κB_η.
Differentiate again in arbitrary parameter directions h,k, with C_h=C_ηh and C_k=C_ηk:

  F_Z D²Z_N^*[h,k]
    =-F_ZZ[C_h,C_k]-F_Zη[C_h,k]
     -F_Zη[C_k,h]-F_ηη[h,k]。                          （P1.17）

Evaluate derivatives at (Z_N^*(η),η); A ensures compatible mixed partials. For ||h||=||k||=1, (P1.7),(P1.9),(P1.10) give (P1.11). QED.

1.5 Constructing computable certificates

Finite sampled maxima cannot replace theorem-wide bounds. The following is one local sufficient implementation of the same theorem, not another control route.

First obtain reliable (P1.7) bounds on candidate Q_cert and reliable anchor bounds

  q_0≥||I-RF_Z(Zbar,ηbar)||，
  β_0≥||RF(Zbar,ηbar)||。

Integrating separately in Z and η permits

  q=q_0+||R||(L_ZZ r_Z+L_Zη r_η)，
  β=β_0+||R|| B_η r_η。                               （P1.18）

Check q<1 and β<(1-q)r_Z. Direct verification of (P1.5),(P1.6) may tighten bounds; tightest possible estimates are not required.

For B, componentwise derivative bounds on Q_cert
  ||D_Z λ_ij||≤L_λ,ij，
  ||D_Z s_ij^u||≤L_sZ,ij， ||D_η s_ij^u||≤L_sη,ij，
make it sufficient to check

  λ_ij(Zbar)-L_λ,ij r_Z≥μ_λ>0，                 j∈Aset_i，
  s_ij^u(Zbar,ηbar)-L_sZ,ij r_Z-L_sη,ij r_η≥μ_s>0，
                                                    j∈Iset_i。 （P1.19）

Use reliable lower bounds on the left if anchor λ/slack evaluations are inexact. λ is a linear root selector, so L_λ follows directly from scales. Since s_i^u=b_i-A_i u_i,

  D_Z s_i^u=-A_i D_Z u_i，
  D_η s_i^u[h]=D_ηb_i[h]-(D_ηA_i[h])u_i，              （P1.20）

slack derivative bounds use known action constraints and bounded u_i, without knowing the new root.

Similarly check contact conditions at all actual evaluation positions. For fixed (t_e,x_i), where t_e is t_i or T,
  δ_e(η)=d(q_i)-γ_0-t_e γdot_0， r_e(η)=b-s(q_i)。
Use parameter bounds on δ_e to ensure a_e∈[a_min,a_max], then let
  m_Ψ=min_{[a_min,a_max]}Ψ'(a)>0，
  L_a,e≤L_δ,e/m_Ψ。
If

  min{|r_e(ηbar)-a_e(ηbar)|,|r_e(ηbar)+a_e(ηbar)|}
     -(L_r,e+L_a,e)r_η≥ε_c>0，                         （P1.21）

both edge functions stay nonzero throughout P_cert. Connectedness and continuity preserve anchor signs, hence coverage branches. Check other positive margins similarly; include reliable half-width error on the left of (P1.21) when evaluation is inexact.

Bounds can use local analytical estimates or reliable interval calculations over fixed compact domains. Strict floating-point certificates must include rounding, half-width and derivative-evaluation errors. Automatic differentiation gives point derivatives, not automatic domain-wide bounds. Only a small neighborhood of the current finite root is needed, not global parameter enclosures or maximal radii.

Record at least configuration identifier, scales, (Zbar,ηbar),r_Z,r_η,R definition, β,q,κ,B_η, three second-derivative bounds, complementarity/contact margins and bound provenance. Basis/node/constraint-layout changes invalidate direct reuse. Unverified configuration bounds mean theorem conditions remain unverified, not certification passed.

1.6 Scope and subsequent interfaces

(a) Why assumptions are not circular.
(P1.5),(P1.6) are checked on known sets around a numerical anchor, without unknown Z_N^*(η) locations/inverses. They prove root existence and provide residual-aware enclosures. Pointwise F_Z condition numbers, small residuals or full-rank basis matrices do not replace these checks.

(b) Why nodewise LICQ does not additionally imply complete-root regularity.
This theorem directly checks full F_Z. R_g≻0 and independent active rows ensure only nodewise KKT regularity, not coupled critic collocation regularity. At an exact root, dependent active rows would admit nonzero dλ with A_i^T dλ=0; holding other variables fixed makes stationarity, Bellman and complementarity Jacobian products vanish, contradicting invertible F_Z. Thus full-root regularity already implies independent node active rows; no duplicate sufficient assumption is needed.

(c) Conservatism of sufficient conditions.
Failed certification means these bounds cannot support this continuation, not absence of roots, necessary active-set changes or unsafe policies. Near an exact regular root with strict complementarity and smoothness margins, choose R=F_Z^{-1}, shrink r_Z then r_η, and continuity yields the conditions. Numerical anchors also require sufficiently small β_0. This shows compatibility with R15 regular branches, not certification at arbitrary fixed tolerances/estimation steps.

(d) Separate node branches from closed-loop branches.
This protects evaluation branches forming finite F. Policy active sets at arbitrary real states, maintained contact and state feasibility require later policy/trajectory certificates. Fixed node active sets do not imply fixed trajectory active sets, and Q_cert does not establish closed-loop feasibility.

(e) Quantities passed to subsequent proofs.
Item 2 uses κ,c_Z,M_Z,P_cert,B_Z and anchor-error bound (P1.8). Item 3 establishes nonnode policy recovery; items 5,6 establish trajectory constraints and local performance. Item 7 uses domains/margins to decide continued theorem coverage.

This item establishes only a regular root branch and verifiable enclosure, not predictor deployment feasibility, near-optimality or acceleration.

Mathematical sources
Existence uses preconditioned fixed-point contraction and regularity uses the implicit-function theorem; (P1.13)–(P1.17) give the problem-specific derivation. For reliable numerical enclosure background see S. M. Rump's author page, Verification methods:
https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/rump/Research_Rump/topics.shtml
This source supports general reliable-verification methods, not a substitute proof of R15-specific claims.

End of item 1.


2. Inexact single-step ANEOC root-prediction error

Translate numerical-anchor residual, sensitivity-solve/derivative errors and prediction arithmetic into explicit new-parameter root-error bounds. A credible parameter-error set additionally permits uncertainty extension within the same nominal model family.

2.1 Inputs, comparison objects and conditions

Retain certified F,B_Z,P_cert,R,q,κ,c_Z,M_Z and second-partial bounds from item 1, with unchanged norms/scales. Given

  η_0,η_1∈P_cert， Δη=η_1-η_0， d_η=||Δη||，
  Zhat_0∈B_Z，
  Z_0^*=Z_N^*(η_0)， Z_1^*=Z_N^*(η_1)，
  C_0=D_ηZ_N^*(η_0)。                                 （P2.1）

η_0 is the nominal-problem label corresponding to Zhat_0; η_1 labels the new nominal problem. Zhat_0 may be the center Zbar from item 1, or another recorded numerical anchor in the same certified domain. η_0 and η_1 belong to the same convex P_cert, so their entire parameter segment is certified; small d_η alone cannot replace domain-membership checks.

First bound anchor error. Suppose numerical residual Ftilde_0 satisfies

  ||Ftilde_0-F(Zhat_0,η_0)||≤ε_F,0。

Compute reliable bounds

  ν_0≥||R Ftilde_0||+||R||ε_F,0，
  ρ_0=ν_0/(1-q)。                                     （P2.2）

Below, ||Zhat_0-Z_0^*||≤ρ_0 is proved. If other valid anchor-error bounds exist, their minimum may be used as ρ_0. This uses the anchor's current actual residual rather than repeatedly using the coarser β covering all parameters in item 1.

Define mathematically exact anchor derivatives

  J_0=F_Z(Zhat_0,η_0)， K_0=F_η(Zhat_0,η_0)。

Computed Jtilde_0,Ktilde_0 and returned Chat_0 satisfy

  ||Jtilde_0-J_0||≤ε_J，
  ||Ktilde_0-K_0||≤ε_K，
  ||Jtilde_0 Chat_0+Ktilde_0||≤ε_lin。                  （P2.3）

All three are reliable bounds. ε_lin includes rounding in residual evaluation of the returned matrix; ε_J,ε_K include implicit-contact-root and derivative errors. They may be zero but floating-point derivatives are not assumed mathematically exact. Reused older derivatives qualify only if their errors relative to these J_0,K_0 are bounded too.

Let ν_C reliably bound ||Chat_0|| and define

  ε_C=κ[ε_lin+ε_K+ε_J ν_C
              +(L_Zη+L_ZZ c_Z)ρ_0]。                 （P2.4）

The stored prediction is

  Z_p=Zhat_0+Chat_0 Δη+e_p， ||e_p||≤ε_p。             （P2.5）

Include matrix–vector multiplication, vector addition and other prediction-operation errors in e_p. If the implemented parameter difference is Δη+e_Δ, include Chat_0 e_Δ in e_p, with norm at most ν_C||e_Δ||. Keep η_0,η_1 as the problem labels defined in (P2.1), separating difference-rounding error from environmental estimation error.

No exact new root or C_0 is needed beforehand; (P2.2)–(P2.5) use numerical inputs and item-1 constants. Jtilde_0 need not be invertible: a returned Chat_0 satisfying (P2.3) suffices. Poor approximations may simply yield bounds too large for release.

2.2 Theorem 2: inexact single-step prediction bound

Under 2.1, sensitivity error satisfies

  ||Chat_0-C_0||≤ε_C。                                 （P2.6）

Stored prediction error relative to the new nominal root satisfies

  ||Z_p-Z_1^*||≤ρ_Z,nom，
  ρ_Z,nom=ρ_0+ε_C d_η+(M_Z/2)d_η²+ε_p。               （P2.7）

Substitution of (P2.4) explicitly gives

  ρ_Z,nom=
    [1+κ(L_Zη+L_ZZ c_Z)d_η]ρ_0
    +κ(ε_lin+ε_K+ε_J ν_C)d_η
    +(M_Z/2)d_η²+ε_p。                                （P2.8）

Thus anchor inexactness causes both an initial offset and additional step-proportional sensitivity error. These are not absorbed into an ideal quadratic remainder.

(P2.7) does not itself require Z_p∈B_Z, because Taylor expansion follows the exact branch. Applying item-1 uniform derivatives, residual certificates or margins at Z_p separately requires membership. A preprediction sufficient condition is

  ||Zhat_0-Zbar||+ν_C d_η+ε_p≤r_Z。                    （P2.9）

Direct postprediction checking of ||Z_p-Zbar||≤r_Z is often tighter. This only establishes root-variable domain membership.

2.3 Proof

Step 1: anchor residual to root error.

At fixed η_0, use T_{η_0}(Z)=Z-RF(Z,η_0) from item 1. Since Zhat_0 and Z_0^* both lie in B_Z and T_{η_0}(Z_0^*)=Z_0^*, we have

  Zhat_0-Z_0^*
    =R F(Zhat_0,η_0)+T_{η_0}(Zhat_0)-T_{η_0}(Z_0^*)。

Contraction and (P2.2) give

  ||Zhat_0-Z_0^*||≤ν_0+q||Zhat_0-Z_0^*||，

Rearrangement yields ||Zhat_0-Z_0^*||≤ρ_0.
This uses the certified uniform contraction, not an incorrectly extrapolated pointwise inverse bound over finite residual-to-root distances.

Step 2: sensitivity error.

Denote exact derivatives at the root by
  J_*=F_Z(Z_0^*,η_0)， K_*=F_η(Z_0^*,η_0)，
Theorem 1 gives J_* C_0+K_*=0 and ||C_0||≤c_Z.

The Zhat_0-to-Z_0^* segment lies in B_Z; second-partial bounds give

  ||J_0-J_*||≤L_ZZ ρ_0，
  ||K_0-K_*||≤L_Zη ρ_0。                              （P2.10）

Let r_lin=Jtilde_0 Chat_0+Ktilde_0. For exact anchor derivatives,

  J_0 Chat_0+K_0
    =r_lin+(J_0-Jtilde_0)Chat_0+(K_0-Ktilde_0)，

Hence

  ||J_0 Chat_0+K_0||≤ε_lin+ε_J ν_C+ε_K。              （P2.11）

Using J_* C_0+K_*=0, rearrange to

  J_0(Chat_0-C_0)
    =(J_0 Chat_0+K_0)
      -(J_0-J_*)C_0-(K_0-K_*)。                       （P2.12）

The anchor lies in Q_cert, so theorem 1 gives ||J_0^{-1}||≤κ. Left-multiply (P2.12) by J_0^{-1} and combine (P2.10),(P2.11) to obtain (P2.4),(P2.6). The derivation uses the certified inverse bound for exact J_0; it does not assume that a numerical matrix inverse is the exact inverse.

Step 3: integral Taylor remainder on the same branch.

Let z(s)=Z_N^*(η_0+sΔη), s∈[0,1]. By theorem 1, z is C², so

  Z_1^*=Z_0^*+C_0 Δη+r_T，
  r_T=∫_0^1(1-s)D_η²Z_N^*(η_0+sΔη)[Δη,Δη] ds，
  ||r_T||≤(M_Z/2)d_η²。                              （P2.13）

Subtracting (P2.13) from (P2.5) gives the exact decomposition

  Z_p-Z_1^*=(Zhat_0-Z_0^*)+(Chat_0-C_0)Δη+e_p-r_T。   （P2.14）

Taking norms and inserting the preceding bounds yields (P2.7),(P2.8). The triangle inequality in (P2.5) also shows (P2.9) implies Z_p∈B_Z. QED.

2.4 Parameter-uncertainty extension within the same model family

If MHE supplies η_1 but the comparison parameter η_1^dagger is unknown, independently justify

  η_1^dagger∈E_1，
  E_1⊂P_cert，
  sup_{η∈E_1}||η-η_1||≤ε_η。                          （P2.15）

η_1^dagger still parameterizes the same F; arbitrary unmodeled tissue need not be representable by this finite family. Theorem 1's root Lipschitz bound gives, for η_1^dagger∈E_1,

  ||Z_p-Z_N^*(η_1^dagger)||
    ≤||Z_p-Z_N^*(η_1)||
      +||Z_N^*(η_1)-Z_N^*(η_1^dagger)||
    ≤ρ_Z,nom+c_Z ε_η=:ρ_Z,par。                       （P2.16）

Thus (P2.16) holds uniformly on E_1 without independence of parameter and solution errors. For a spherical parameter-error set,

  ||η_1-ηbar||+ε_η≤r_η                               （P2.17）

is sufficient for inclusion in P_cert; check nonspherical inclusion directly.

Do not additionally add old true-parameter error. The (P2.7) anchor explicitly approximates nominal η_0, and ρ_0 measures error from Z_N^*(η_0). Count the new target-parameter difference once in (P2.16). Relabeling η_0 without matching roots/sensitivities requires rechecking (P2.2),(P2.3), not reusing the old budget.

MHE covariance Σ_η alone does not give deterministic inclusion (P2.15). With justified P(η_1^dagger∈E_1)≥1-α, use (P2.16) only on that event and retain its probabilistic meaning; coordinate coverage events if other certificates are probabilistic too. Without a valid error set retain nominal (P2.7). Later dynamics/trajectory/performance budgets handle structural-model and execution errors.

2.5 Optional postprediction residual tightening

After verifying Z_p∈B_Z, evaluate the new residual Ftilde_p once and obtain

  ||Ftilde_p-F(Z_p,η_1)||≤ε_F,p，
  ν_p≥||R Ftilde_p||+||R||ε_F,p。

The same contraction argument as step 1 of 2.3 gives

  ||Z_p-Z_1^*||≤ν_p/(1-q)=:ρ_Z,res。                   （P2.18）

Specifically, Z_p-Z_1^*=RF(Z_p,η_1)+T_{η_1}(Z_p)-T_{η_1}(Z_1^*). Norms and rearrangement yield (P2.18). Thus use

  ρ_Z=min{ρ_Z,nom,ρ_Z,res}                             （P2.19）

as the new nominal-root bound, or ρ_Z+c_Z ε_η for the parameter extension. Without residual evaluation take ρ_Z=ρ_Z,nom. Both refer to the same uncorrected candidate and neither resolves the full HJB–KKT system. Item 8 must count residual/certification costs; no re-solve does not mean zero validation cost.

2.6 Error orders and subsequent scope

(a) Ideal and inexact orders.
For Zhat_0=Z_0^*, Chat_0=C_0 and e_p=0, (P2.14) gives
  ||Z_p-Z_1^*||≤(M_Z/2)d_η²。
For uniformly bounded certified constants over small steps, ρ_0=O(d_η²), ε_C=O(d_η), ε_p=O(d_η²) suffice for total O(d_η²) root error. By (P2.4), ρ_0=O(d_η²) and ε_lin+ε_K+ε_Jν_C=O(d_η) also ensure the needed sensitivity accuracy.
Fixed anchor/arithmetic errors generally create floors; fixed sensitivity error adds O(d_η). Fixed ε_η in (P2.16) adds an uncertainty floor. An ideal quadratic Taylor remainder does not make total error or true performance loss quadratic.

(b) Small root error does not authorize direct node-action release.
Even if an exact new root has s_ij^{u,*}=0, prediction error may give s_ij^u(Z_p,η_1)<0. Strict complementarity protects exact-branch structure, not all nearby predicted KKT inequalities. Item 3 recovers feedback through candidate-critic Hamiltonian minimization; item 5 checks the candidate's complete trajectory feasibility.

(c) Actual interface supplied here.
Record η_0,η_1,Zhat_0,Chat_0, configuration identifier, numerical bounds and final ρ_Z; for parameter extension record E_1 provenance/coverage meaning. A later root budget ε_Z,allow permits checking ρ_Z≤ε_Z,allow, but root accuracy alone is not a publish criterion.
This supplies computable inputs to items 3–7, without proving continuous-HJB approximation quality, closed-loop constraints, near-optimality, acceleration or multistep recursion.

Mathematical sources
This item combines the root branch, uniform contraction and derivative bounds proved in theorem 1 with matrix-perturbation identities and integral Taylor remainders. It makes the inexact single-step formula in original R15 appendix J.4 explicit through residual and derivative-error bounds, without introducing a new optimal-control problem.

End of item 2.


3. Root-to-policy and actual-impedance error

Extract a candidate critic and solve the original four-dimensional Hamiltonian action minimization at actual query states. Bound root-to-feedback-gain-rate error and propagate it through g_a_dot=u_g to actual gains/impedance matrices.

3.1 Comparison objects, scales and query domain

Fix new nominal η_1 from item 2 and obtain

  ||Z_p-Z_N^*(η_1)||≤ρ_Z。                              （P3.1）

Z retains items 1–2 normalized coordinates. W,x,u_g,g_a,K,D use fixed main-text physical coordinates/units. Define the explicit conversion

  W_p=Π_W(Z_ref+D_Z Z_p)，
  W_*=Π_W(Z_ref+D_Z Z_N^*(η_1))，
  E_W=Π_W D_Z， ΔW=W_p-W_*，
  ||ΔW||≤||E_W||ρ_Z。                                 （P3.2）

Here Π_W selects weights from the full physical root. Physical norms are Euclidean in predetermined units; unit changes require corresponding constant changes. η remains normalized, with D_η included in model parameter derivatives.

Choose compact Ω_query containing queried (t,x), independently of collocation nodes; item-1 B_Z does not define it. Require evaluable models/value gradients and

  g_min≤g_a≤g_max， E_T≥E_T,min，
  U(t,x;η_1)={u:A_u(t,x;η_1)u≤b_u(t,x;η_1)}。

Original R15 rate bounds make U bounded; linear inequalities make it closed and convex. In original Eq.(31), u=0 is feasible, so U is nonempty. Here P_D≥0 follows from positive damping, and χ≥0. These conditions on Ω_query only make the action problem well-defined; they do not establish whole-trajectory force, pose or moment constraints.

Let

  H=2R_g， m_H=λ_min(H)>0，
  c(t,x;W,η)=G_g(t,x;η)^T
                 [∇_xΦ(x;η)+J_ψ(t,x)^T W]，
  Q(u;t,x,W,η)=1/2 u^T H u+c(t,x;W,η)^T u，
  π(t,x;W,η)=argmin_{u∈U(t,x;η)} Q(u;t,x,W,η)，
  π_p(t,x)=π(t,x;W_p,η_1)，
  π_*(t,x)=π(t,x;W_*,η_1)。                            （P3.3）

π_* is finite-root-critic feedback, not true HJB-optimal feedback. Candidate node actions/multipliers u_{p,i},λ_{p,i} are not directly executable at arbitrary states; recover policy using W_p and current (t,x). Four-dimensional action minimization evaluates the original law without resolving the full n_W-node HJB–KKT system.

Assume reliable bounds on Ω_query

  L_πZ≥sup_{(t,x)∈Ω_query}
       ||G_g(t,x;η_1)^T J_ψ(t,x)^T E_W||/m_H。         （P3.4）

Finite sample maxima alone are insufficient; use analytical or reliable enclosures from the known basis, channel and compact domain. States need not share an action active set.

3.2 Theorem 3: policy error and impedance-generation interface

Under 3.1, π_p and π_* exist uniquely at each (t,x)∈Ω_query, belong to common U(t,x;η_1), and satisfy

  ||π_p(t,x)-π_*(t,x)||
    ≤||G_g^T J_ψ^T ΔW||/m_H
    ≤L_πZ ρ_Z。                                       （P3.5）

Different active sets, weak activity and nonunique action multipliers are allowed. Strict complementarity away from collocation nodes is not required.

If the numerical solver returns feasible uhat_p∈U with computable objective-gap bound

  0≤Q(uhat_p)-Q(π_p)≤δ_Q，

then

  ||uhat_p-π_p||≤sqrt(2δ_Q/m_H)=:ε_Q，
  ||uhat_p-π_*||≤L_πZρ_Z+ε_Q。                         （P3.6）

Actual-gain conclusions are interval statements. Let absolutely continuous x_p(t),x_*(t) lie in the relevant Ω_query sections for I=[t_a,t_b] at common η_1. Their gain components satisfy almost everywhere

  g_p_dot=uhat_p(t,x_p(t))+d_g(t)，
  g_*_dot=π_*(t,x_*(t))， ||d_g(t)||≤dbar_g(t)。        （P3.7）

d_g denotes only stated bounded gain-implementation error; exact nominal integration has d_g=0. It does not replace unmodeled torque/contact error.
If reference feedback additionally satisfies between compared states

  ||π_*(t,x)-π_*(t,y)||≤L_πx||x-y||，                 （P3.8）

then with e_x(t)=||x_p(t)-x_*(t)|| and e_g,a=||g_p(t_a)-g_*(t_a)||,

  ||g_p(t)-g_*(t)||≤e_g,a
    +∫_{t_a}^t [L_πZρ_Z+ε_Q(s)+L_πx e_x(s)+dbar_g(s)]ds
    =:ρ_g(t)。                                        （P3.9）

(P3.8) uses physical x norms. If item 4 normalizes states, convert L_πx through fixed state scales. Items 4–5 establish existence, domain retention and independent e_x bounds; (P3.9) is a conditional interface, not a circular numerical certificate based on unknown e_x.

The stiffness/damping definitions in (28) give exactly

  ||K(g_p)-K(g_*)||_F²+||D(g_p)-D(g_*)||_F²
      =||g_p-g_*||²≤ρ_g(t)²。                          （P3.10）

Norms use fixed physical units and F denotes Frobenius norm. Reporting separately scaled stiffness/damping errors requires scaling corresponding components on both sides.

3.3 Proof

Step 1: action existence and uniqueness.

Nonempty compact U and continuous Q attain a minimum. H≻0 makes Q strictly convex; convex U gives uniqueness. Any finite critic weights qualify, even weights not at a root.

Step 2: same-state, same-parameter policy error.

Fix (t,x,η_1); abbreviate u_p=π_p,u_*=π_*,c_p=c(W_p),c_*=c(W_*). Convex minimizers satisfy

  (H u_p+c_p)^T(u_*-u_p)≥0，
  (H u_*+c_*)^T(u_p-u_*)≥0。

With Δu=u_p-u_*, addition gives

  Δu^T H Δu≤-(c_p-c_*)^T Δu
              ≤||c_p-c_*|| ||Δu||。                  （P3.11）

Since Δu^T H Δu≥m_H||Δu||², the result is immediate for Δu=0; otherwise division by ||Δu|| gives
  ||Δu||≤||c_p-c_*||/m_H。
Common t,x,η cancel terminal value gradients, hence

  c_p-c_*=G_g^T J_ψ^T ΔW
           =G_g^T J_ψ^T E_W(Z_p-Z_N^*(η_1))。          （P3.12）

Substituting (P3.1),(P3.4) yields (P3.5). Only common feasibility and strong convexity are used, without active-set differentiation.

Step 3: numerical action error.

The quadratic Q and π_p variational inequality give, for feasible uhat_p,

  Q(uhat_p)-Q(π_p)
    =(Hπ_p+c_p)^T(uhat_p-π_p)
      +1/2(uhat_p-π_p)^T H(uhat_p-π_p)
    ≥(m_H/2)||uhat_p-π_p||²。                          （P3.13）

The δ_Q bound gives the first part of (P3.6), and the triangle inequality gives the second.

Step 4: gain integration and impedance-matrix error.

Integrate (P3.7), adding/subtracting π_p(t,x_p),π_*(t,x_p) inside. Apply (P3.5),(P3.6),(P3.8) and gain-implementation bounds to obtain (P3.9). Absolute continuity permits finitely many nondifferentiable action switches.
Only Δκ_t,Δκ_n vary in K's diagonal and Δd_t,Δd_n in D's; all other entries agree. Their Frobenius squares sum to these four squares, giving (P3.10). QED.

3.4 Certifying numerical action error

(P3.6) does not need known Q(π_p). At a query abbreviate A=A_u,b=b_u,c=c(W_p). For any λhat≥0 define the dual lower bound

  d_Q(λhat)=-1/2(c+A^Tλhat)^T H^{-1}(c+A^Tλhat)
              -b^Tλhat。                              （P3.14）

by minimizing over u∈R^4 the Lagrangian
  1/2 u^T H u+c^T u+λhat^T(Au-b)
and completing the square. Feasible u has λhat^T(Au-b)≤0, so d_Q(λhat)≤Q(π_p), without strong duality or unique multipliers.
For feasible uhat_p set r_Q=H uhat_p+c+A^Tλhat and compute

  Q(uhat_p)-d_Q(λhat)
    =1/2 r_Q^T H^{-1}r_Q+λhat^T(b-Auhat_p)≥0。        （P3.15）

A reliable upper bound of the right side gives δ_Q and (P3.6). H is fixed four-dimensional positive definite; this does not correct the full HJB–KKT root.

Using (P3.15) requires verifying that uhat_p is feasible for the mathematical A,b and that λhat≥0, including model-evaluation and rounding errors. A small stationarity residual, or treating negative slack as numerical zero, does not establish the feasible-deployment conclusion (P3.6). If action feasibility cannot be certified, this item does not authorize release; subsequent trigger rules handle it. The exact policy (P3.5) is unaffected by this numerical implementation difficulty.

3.5 Additional regularity for state/parameter changes

(P3.5) does not imply (P3.8), since feasible A_u u≤b_u changes with x. The following locally sufficient L_πx check supports item 4 but is not an extra assumption for (P3.5).

Let y denote changing query data, such as x or η, at fixed W. For fixed active set Aset and corresponding A_A,b_A, if KKT signs persist, A_A has full row rank and local data are C¹,

  M_A [u;λ_A]=[-c;b_A]，
  M_A=[H,A_A^T;A_A,0]。                               （P3.16）

M_A is invertible: H v+A_A^T w=0 and A_A v=0 give v^T H v=0, hence v=0, then w=0 by full row rank. Differentiate along h to obtain

  M_A [D_yu[h];D_yλ_A[h]]
    =[-D_yc[h]-(D_yA_A[h])^Tλ_A;
        D_yb_A[h]-(D_yA_A[h])u]。                     （P3.17）

Reliable bounds on M_A^{-1},u,λ_A and data derivatives bound ||D_yu||; integrate along state segments for L_πx. Multiple patches require a verified finite segment partition, uniform derivatives and continuous joins; use the maximum bound. Adjacent feasible KKT extensions coincide at common boundaries by strong convexity. This permits verified switches, but node strict complementarity alone does not verify arbitrary-state switching.

Without smooth data, independent rows or derivative bounds, same-query (P3.5) still applies; (P3.8) needs another justification or trajectory certification remains unavailable. No global atlas over all states/active sets is required.

If item 2 supplies a parameter-error set E_1 and comparison is sought with the finite-root policy at η_1^dagger∈E_1, include the direct parameter effect on the policy. Suppose the required weights, query points and parameter segments satisfy certified

  ||π(t,x;W,η)-π(t,x;W,η')||≤L_πη||η-η'||，          （P3.18）

with W fixed; check via (P3.17) or another proved local bound. Let L_πZ cover these parameters too. Two-stage comparison and item-2 root error give

  ||π(t,x;W_p,η_1)-π(t,x;W_N^*(η_1^dagger),η_1^dagger)||
    ≤L_πZ(ρ_Z+c_Z ε_η)+L_πη ε_η。                   （P3.19）

Here W_N^* is in physical coordinates (P3.2). First compare weights at η_1, then vary η at fixed W_N^*(η_1^dagger), using (P3.5),(P3.18). The second term includes explicit terminal-gradient, G_g and constraint dependence; root error alone cannot replace it. Without certified (P3.18), retain nominal policy bounds and treat parameter error later as a bounded dynamics disturbance. Small cross-parameter policy distance does not establish true-parameter constraint feasibility.

3.6 Scope

(a) Deployment is Z_p→W_p→π_p(t,x)→u_g→integration to g_a→K(g_a),D(g_a). At a common actual state g_a is identical; replacing critics does not instantaneously change applied gains. No memoryless algebraic g_a=g_a(W) relation is proved.

(b) If the root error in item 2 is second order and L_πZ is uniformly bounded, the exactly recovered same-state nominal policy error is also second order. Whether actual gain trajectories retain that order additionally depends on ε_Q, e_x, initial gain differences and implementation deviations; it cannot be inferred in advance from (P3.5).

(c) Pointwise action feasibility is not trajectory-wide contact feasibility and does not guarantee both closed loops exist/remain in Ω_query. Item 4 bounds nominal trajectories; item 5 certifies candidate constraints.

(d) Item 8 handles computational advantage. Four-dimensional action evaluation and δ_Q certification are execution costs. Avoiding a full HJB–KKT re-solve does not avoid every optimization computation.

Mathematical sources
This item formalizes the strongly convex Hamiltonian action recovery in original R15 appendix I.3,L.2 as a theorem including scales and numerical action errors. Variational inequalities directly prove policy bounds; completing squares gives the dual lower bound; original Eq.(28) gain integration and matrix definitions give the actual impedance relationship.

End of item 3.


4. Policy-to-nominal-closed-loop trajectory error

For fixed descriptor/critic deployment intervals, prove existence, uniqueness and finite-time error bounds using a computable candidate-rollout tube. This avoids assuming domain retention before proving it with in-domain bounds.

4.1 Closed loops, interval and local conditions

Use item-3 physical coordinates, π_p,π_* and fixed η_1. For I=[t_a,t_b]⊂[0,T], define

  f_p(t,x)=f_0(t,x;η_1)+G_g(t,x;η_1)π_p(t,x)，
  f_*(t,x)=f_0(t,x;η_1)+G_g(t,x;η_1)π_*(t,x)，
  x_p_dot=f_p(t,x_p)， x_p(t_a)=x_p,a，
  x_*_dot=f_*(t,x_*)， x_*(t_a)=x_*,a。                （P4.1）

x_p is the exact nominal candidate trajectory; x_* uses the exact finite-root policy in the same model. Usually x_p,a=x_*,a; alternatively allow known e_a≥||x_p,a-x_*,a||. x_* is not a true HJB-optimal trajectory.

Root and parameter stay fixed here; item 7 handles repeated updates/switching. Finite-horizon bounds do not imply infinite-time stability.

In an open neighborhood of the trajectory tube to be certified, both Hamiltonian action problems must have unique solutions, with data and policies making f_p,f_* continuous and locally Lipschitz in x, uniformly on compact time intervals. Interpret time endpoints one-sidedly. This additional trajectory-uniqueness condition does not follow from item 3 pointwise action uniqueness alone. Item 3, (P3.16)–(P3.17), supplies checkable local policy-regularity conditions; verified continuous piecewise policies are also allowed without everywhere differentiable actions.

Obtain reliable finite constants for the required state pairs

  ||f_p(t,x)-f_p(t,y)||≤L_p||x-y||，
  ||f_*(t,x)-f_*(t,y)||≤L_*||x-y||，
  ||G_g(t,x;η_1)||≤Gbar，
  ||π_p(t,x)-π_*(t,x)||≤L_πZρ_Z，
  Δ_f=Gbar L_πZρ_Z。                                  （P4.2）

L_p,L_*≥0. Spherical time sections contain segments between their points, allowing derivative-based Lipschitz verification.

Exact unknown W_* is not needed to compute constants: item 3 gives
  ||W_*-W_p||≤||E_W||ρ_Z。
Uniformly verify regularity/constants over W_p and this weight enclosure. Constants checked only at W_p cannot be used directly at W_*.
For example, common-state-pair Lipschitz bounds L_0 for f_0, L_G for G_g and L_πx for feedback, with ||π||≤ubar:=||rbar_g||, permit

  L_f=L_0+L_G ubar+Gbar L_πx。                         （P4.3）

Substitute the candidate and reference separately to obtain L_p,L_*. Tighter direct closed-loop derivative bounds also apply. The node F_Z inverse bound in item 1 does not replace these state-space constants.

4.2 Computable candidate trajectory tube

Let y(t),t∈I, be a continuous piecewise-C¹ numerical curve from the candidate policy, not necessarily an exact ODE solution. Continuous joins suffice; derivatives/residuals are interpreted almost everywhere. Verify

  ||y_dot(t)-f_p(t,y(t))||≤r_num(t)，                  （P4.4）

with integrable r_num≥0, including interpolation and model/action evaluation errors. Additional jumps need explicit jump errors and are outside this continuous-curve version. An integrator's empirical tolerance is not automatically (P4.4).

Choose r_tube>0 and define

  T_y={(t,x):t∈I，||x-y(t)||≤r_tube}。                （P4.5）

Verify T_y⊂Ω_query, local ODE regularity in an open neighborhood and uniform (P4.2) on each ball section. This uses model validity, action definition and derivatives only; item 5 handles force/torque/task margins.

This is locally sufficient. If the ball cannot fit inside the action-defined query domain, for example at a state-domain boundary, certification may fail without implying nonexistent closed loops. Such tubes are not asserted for all R15 feasible states.

For L≥0,a≥0 and integrable b(t)≥0 define

  E_L[a,b](t)=exp(L(t-t_a))a
                +∫_{t_a}^t exp(L(t-s))b(s)ds。        （P4.6）

For L=0 use a+∫b. Let a_p≥||x_p,a-y(t_a)|| and a_*≥||x_*,a-y(t_a)|| and compute

  β_p(t)=E_{L_p}[a_p,r_num](t)，
  β_*(t)=E_{L_*}[a_*,r_num+Δ_f](t)。                 （P4.7）

Assume a verified strict tube margin

  sup_{t∈I} max{β_p(t),β_*(t)}<r_tube。               （P4.8）

All quantities come from candidate curves, known initial states/bounds, root error and domain constants; x_* need not be computed. For nonnegative constant L and nonnegative inputs, β is nondecreasing, so reliable terminal bounds suffice for (P4.8), with reliable integral estimates.

4.3 Theorem 4: trajectory and domain-retention certification

Under 4.1–4.2, both (P4.1) trajectories exist uniquely throughout I, remain strictly inside the tube and satisfy

  ||x_p(t)-y(t)||≤β_p(t)，
  ||x_*(t)-y(t)||≤β_*(t)。                             （P4.9）

Their direct difference satisfies

  ||x_p(t)-x_*(t)||≤ρ_x(t)，
  ρ_x(t)=E_{L_*}[e_a,Δ_f](t)。                        （P4.10）

For L_*>0,

  ρ_x(t)=exp(L_*τ)e_a
            +Gbar L_πZρ_Z [exp(L_*τ)-1]/L_*，
  τ=t-t_a；                                          （P4.11）

For L_*=0, the second term is Gbar L_πZρ_Z τ. If tighter, combine with (P4.9) to use min{ρ_x,β_p+β_*}. In particular, at common initial states over a fixed finite I with uniformly bounded constants, second-order parameter-prediction accuracy in ρ_Z transfers to a second-order nominal-trajectory error bound.

These compare exact nominal loops. r_num only proves retention around the numerical center and must not be counted again as a true disturbance in (P4.10).

4.4 Proof

Step 1: integral error inequalities before first exit.

Continuity/local Lipschitz regularity gives unique local solutions. Derive bounds only on their common pre-exit existence interval, without presuming existence to t_b.

Let r_y(t)=y_dot(t)-f_p(t,y(t)). Subtract candidate integrals:

  x_p(t)-y(t)=x_p,a-y(t_a)
     +∫_{t_a}^t[f_p(s,x_p(s))-f_p(s,y(s))-r_y(s)]ds。

Norms with (P4.2),(P4.4) give

  ||x_p(t)-y(t)||≤a_p
     +∫_{t_a}^t[L_p||x_p(s)-y(s)||+r_num(s)]ds。       （P4.12）

At common state y,
  ||f_p(t,y)-f_*(t,y)||
      =||G_g(t,y;η_1)(π_p(t,y)-π_*(t,y))||≤Δ_f。
Add/subtract f_*(s,y(s)) in the reference/center difference to obtain

  ||x_*(t)-y(t)||≤a_*
     +∫_{t_a}^t[L_*||x_*(s)-y(s)||+r_num(s)+Δ_f]ds。 （P4.13）

For e(t)≤a+∫(Le+b), let v(t)=a+∫_{t_a}^t(Le+b). Then e≤v and v_dot≤Lv+b. Integrating after multiplication by exp(-L(t-t_a)) gives e≤E_L[a,b]. Apply to (P4.12),(P4.13), obtaining (P4.9) before first exit.

Step 2: exclude finite-time exit and premature termination.

By (P4.8), a uniform σ>0 exists such that β_p and β_* never exceed r_tube-σ on I. If either trajectory first reaches the tube boundary, (P4.9) holds before that time. Continuity of the trajectory and y preserves distance ≤r_tube-σ in the limit, contradicting boundary distance r_tube.
A maximal solution ending before t_b without exit remains in a compact tube around continuous y, positively separated from its boundary. Continuous locally Lipschitz fields are bounded in an open neighborhood of this compact set, allowing continuation past that endpoint, a contradiction. Both solutions extend to t_b and (P4.9) holds throughout. Thus conditional pre-exit bounds become full-interval conclusions.

Step 3: direct closed-loop comparison.

Both trajectories stay in the same tube sections. Write

  f_p(t,x_p)-f_*(t,x_*)
    =[f_p(t,x_p)-f_*(t,x_p)]
       +[f_*(t,x_p)-f_*(t,x_*)]。

The first norm is ≤Δ_f and the second ≤L_*||x_p-x_*||. Subtracting trajectory integrals gives

  ||x_p(t)-x_*(t)||≤e_a
       +∫_{t_a}^t[Δ_f+L_*||x_p(s)-x_*(s)||]ds。

The integrating-factor argument yields (P4.10); evaluating the constant-input integral gives (P4.11). QED.

4.5 Closing item-3 actual-impedance bounds

For exact nominal policies, set ε_Q=dbar_g=0 in item 3, (P3.9), and replace unknown e_x with the proved ρ_x to obtain

  ||g_p(t)-g_*(t)||
    ≤e_g,a+L_πZρ_Z τ+L_πx∫_{t_a}^tρ_x(s)ds。         （P4.14）

5.2 Sufficient finite-grid checks

Take t_a=t_0<t_1<...<t_N=t_b, Δ_l=t_{l+1}-t_l, l=0,...,N-1 and I_l=[t_l,t_{l+1}]. Verify intervalwise constants/bounds:

  ||x_c(t)-y(t)||≤β_c(t)≤βbar_c,l，

  sup_{t∈I_l} ε_out,j(t)≤εbar_out,jl，                 （P5.4）

State pairs x,z lie in the same T_y time section, with parameter bounds along corresponding segments. Nominal cases need no L_η,jl. For nonspherical error sets use direct output-variation bounds instead of L_η,jl ε_η.

Require h_j(t,y(t);η_1) absolutely continuous on I_l with almost-everywhere bound

  |d h_j(t,y(t);η_1)/dt|≤M_jl。                       （P5.5）

For example, if a chain derivative exists along y, take
  M_jl≥sup_{I_l}|∂_t h_j+∇_x h_j^T y_dot|。
Bounds |∂_t h_j|≤H_t,jl, ||∇_x h_j||≤L_x,jl and ||y_dot||≤V_y,l also allow H_t,jl+L_x,jl V_y,l. Bound continuous pieces separately and take a uniform maximum. Bounded first derivatives suffice; second contact derivatives along the whole trajectory are unnecessary.

For numerical grid values htilde_jl obtain

  |htilde_jl-h_j(t_l,y(t_l);η_1)|≤ε_eval,jl。           （P5.6）

This covers point evaluation/data representation, without duplicating trajectory-integration error in β_c. Use analytical or reliable numerical bounds; finite samples do not prove (P5.4),(P5.5).

  htilde_jl-ε_eval,jl

    -L_η,jl ε_η-εbar_out,jl≥m_j，
  for every j and l.                                  (P5.7)

m_j=0 gives nonnegative feasibility. Choose m_j>0 for physical constraints required strictly interior by local HJB assumptions. Do not impose artificial strict slack on actions allowed to be active.

5.3 Theorem 5: continuous-time candidate certificate

If (P5.1)–(P5.6) hold and every (P5.7) check passes,

  h_j^c(t)≥m_j≥0，
  for all t∈I, j=1,...,m_h.                            (P5.8)

Thus exact nominal candidates satisfy all checked original (32) trajectory constraints. Reliable parameter/output budgets extend the conclusion to the actual quantities explicitly defined by (P5.3).

Proof: choose j,l,t∈I_l. Absolute continuity and (P5.5) give

  h_j(t,y(t);η_1)
    ≥h_j(t_l,y(t_l);η_1)-M_jl(t-t_l)
    ≥htilde_jl-ε_eval,jl-M_jl Δ_l。                   （P5.9）

Using (P5.1),(P5.4), move from center to candidate at the same time:

  h_j(t,x_c(t);η_1)
    ≥h_j(t,y(t);η_1)-L_x,jl βbar_c,l。                （P5.10）

Then account separately for parameter and direct-output errors:

  h_j^c(t)
    ≥h_j(t,x_c(t);η_1)-L_η,jl ε_η-εbar_out,jl
    ≥htilde_jl-ε_eval,jl-M_jl Δ_l-L_x,jl βbar_c,l
        -L_η,jl ε_η-εbar_out,jl
    ≥m_j。                                             （P5.11）

Finitely many closed I_l cover I including endpoints, giving (P5.8). Almost-everywhere output budgets extend to every instant for continuous actual outputs; discontinuous outputs require budgets at jumps too, otherwise conclusions remain almost everywhere. QED.

5.4 Actions, gains and energy boundaries

Under exact nominal execution, π_p(t,x_p) minimizes over the original action set, so A_uπ_p≤b_u along the existing trajectory. With u_g=π_p and original gain/energy dynamics, componentwise

  d(g_a-g_min)/dt≥-α_g(g_a-g_min)，
  d(g_max-g_a)/dt≥-α_g(g_max-g_a)，
  d(E_T-E_T,min)/dt≥-α_E(E_T-E_T,min)。                （P5.12）

For scalar gap z(t) and α>0, multiply by exp(α(t-t_a)) and integrate:

  z(t)≥exp(-α(t-t_a))z(t_a)≥0。                       （P5.13）

Thus feasible initial gains/energy, exact nominal state equations and almost-everywhere action feasibility preserve gain and energy boundaries throughout I. Rate bounds follow directly from U. This proof applies after item 4 establishes trajectory existence and domain retention; it does not replace the ODE well-posedness checks in item 4.

Numerical actions feasible at every execution instant for the corresponding model data also permit (P5.12) under the same nominal dynamics. Item-3 dual gaps certify accuracy; feasibility needs separate verification. Small action error does not preserve active constraints automatically.

If gain or energy dynamics include deviations, the nonnegativity conclusion (P5.13) cannot be carried over directly. For example,
  z_dot≥-αz-dbar_z(t)
only gives
  z(t)≥exp(-α(t-t_a))z(t_a)
        -∫_{t_a}^t exp(-α(t-s))dbar_z(s)ds。
Disturbances may exhaust margins. Include actual gain/energy gaps in (P5.2) with β_e tubes, or check the explicit bound nonnegative, without double-budgeting the same error.

With sample hold/delay, u_l∈U(t_l,x(t_l)) does not ensure u_l∈U(t,x(t)) throughout I_l. Enclose every executed-action slack b_u(t,x(t))-A_u(t,x(t))u_l over the hold interval. For fixed u_l these are additional scalar h_j checks; check both sides at switches. Without interval coverage, sampled feasibility is not continuous-execution feasibility.

(c) Fix interval, initial conditions and controller identity.
Certificates cover specified I, initial enclosure, η_1,W_p and execution assumptions. Midinterval changes of candidate, parameters or references require checks before reuse. Item 7 handles triggers/continuation; global recursive feasibility is not proved here.

(d) Include parameter and true-output effects.

This feasibility certificate is not unique to ANEOC and needs no forced F=0 correction. ANEOC supplies controlled-error candidates; items 1–4 support root/trajectory comparisons, item 5 checks feasibility and item 6 proves local near-optimality. Item 8 separately addresses speed.

6. Local near-optimality separating predictor and critic/configuration errors

Bound candidate-to-finite-root policy cost separately from finite-root-to-continuous-optimal cost, at fixed nominal model, declared local domain and common initial state. Finite collocation roots are not continuous HJB solutions.

6.1 Comparison problem and horizon

Fix η_1,t_a<T,x_a and H_T=T-t_a. Use original (33) ℓ,Φ and f=f_0+G_g u. Define

  J(u;t_a,x_a,η_1)
    =∫_{t_a}^T ℓ(t,x_u(t),u(t);η_1)dt+Φ(x_u(T);η_1)。 （P6.1）

Specify local state/time D with sections D_t. Let A_D comprise measurable controls/absolutely continuous trajectories starting at x_a, obeying nominal dynamics, u(t)∈U(t,x_u(t);η_1) almost everywhere, all listed state/contact constraints, and x_u(t)∈D_t throughout. Let

  V_D^*(t_a,x_a;η_1)=inf_{u∈A_D}J(u;t_a,x_a,η_1)。     （P6.2）

The infimum need not be attained and V_D^* need not be differentiable. Declare/fix D and restrictions before certification; do not exclude better feasible trajectories after seeing candidate results to improve reported bounds.

Use exactly recovered π_p,π_* from item 3. Items 4–5 must cover the entire [t_a,T] and show that their trajectories x_p,x_* from the same x_a both belong to A_D. Certify reference-policy feasibility separately under item 5, 5.5(b). Thus A_D is nonempty and both policy costs are finite. Write J_p=J(π_p), J_*=J(π_*).

Certificates ending at t_b<T cannot establish near-optimality to T. Certify the remaining continuation. Replacing T by t_b and introducing another terminal cost changes the problem and is not assumed.

6.2 Predictor-cost and configuration-error conditions

First compare J_p,J_*. Obtain reliable constants along both certified trajectories and necessary state/action comparison paths

  |Φ(x;η_1)-Φ(z;η_1)|≤L_Φ||x-z||，
  |ℓ(t,x,u;η_1)-ℓ(t,z,v;η_1)|
        ≤L_ℓx||x-z||+L_ℓu||u-v||，
  ||π_*(t,x)-π_*(t,z)||≤L_πx||x-z||。                （P6.3）

Norms use item-3/4 physical coordinates. For ℓ=ℓ_0+u^T R_g u with ||u||,||v||≤ubar, take L_ℓu=2||R_g||ubar; local ℓ_0 derivatives bound L_ℓx. Φ bounds must cover terminal states. On nonconvex domains, bounded internal gradients do not replace checks on connecting paths.

Using same-initial-state ρ_x from item 4, define

  ε_pred=L_Φρ_x(T)
       +∫_{t_a}^T [(L_ℓx+L_ℓu L_πx)ρ_x(t)
                       +L_ℓu L_πZρ_Z]dt。            （P6.4）

All quantities are supplied by preceding items or bounded through explicit cost functions. A tighter valid bound for ρ_x from item 4 may be used; observed separation between two uncertified numerical trajectories is not a substitute. The integral can be computed explicitly using (P4.16).

For continuous-control comparison define the exact finite-root critic

  U_*(t,x)=V_N(t,x;W_*,η_1)
           =Φ(x;η_1)+ψ(t,x)^T W_*，
  r_*(t,x)=∂_t U_*(t,x)
     +min_{u∈U(t,x;η_1)}
         [ℓ(t,x,u;η_1)+∇_x U_*(t,x)^T f(t,x,u;η_1)]。 （P6.5）

U_* is a trial function, not V_D^*. Require a valid chain rule on all A_D trajectories, for example C¹ in a neighborhood of D with integrable terms. U(t,x;η_1) must be nonempty on the residual domain. Obtain a reliable uniform bound

  |r_*(t,x)|≤ε_B， (t,x)∈D。                          （P6.6）

Original ψ(T,x)=0 enforces U_*(T,x)=Φ(x;η_1) exactly, with no terminal representation error. A future representation change requires a new error budget.

(P6.6) must cover every trajectory used in the (P6.2) infimum. Node-only, candidate-only or reference-only residual checks are insufficient. Section 6.4 gives a computable sufficient construction without exact W_*.

6.3 Theorem 6: local near-optimality

Under 6.1–6.2,

  |J_p-J_*|≤ε_pred，                                  （P6.7）
  0≤J_*-V_D^*≤ε_conf， ε_conf=2H_T ε_B，               （P6.8）

Thus the candidate satisfies

  J_p-V_D^*=(J_p-J_*)+(J_*-V_D^*)，
  0≤J_p-V_D^*≤ε_pred+ε_conf=:ε_J。                    （P6.9）

The first difference can be negative because π_* is finite-root feedback, not the continuous optimum; its guarantee is absolute. The second includes finite-basis, node/configuration and local-root domain approximation errors and need not vanish at F(Z_N^*,η_1)=0.

Proof, part 1: along certified trajectories compare same-state root effects, then reference-policy state variation:

  ||π_p(t,x_p(t))-π_*(t,x_*(t))||
    ≤L_πZρ_Z+L_πxρ_x(t)。                             （P6.10）

Apply (P6.3) to running/terminal cost and integrate to get (P6.4),(P6.7), without unproved optimality or cost stationarity.

Proof, part 2: for any u∈A_D, the chain rule along its trajectory gives

  dU_*(t,x_u(t))/dt=∂_t U_*+∇_xU_*^T f(t,x_u,u)。

Its feasible-action Hamiltonian is no smaller than the minimum over U at that point, hence

  ℓ(t,x_u,u)+dU_*(t,x_u)/dt≥r_*(t,x_u)≥-ε_B。

Integrating with the exact terminal condition gives

  J(u)≥U_*(t_a,x_a)-H_T ε_B。                         （P6.11）

Uniformity over A_D yields V_D^*≥U_*(t_a,x_a)-H_Tε_B without an attained optimum.
For π_*, actions exactly minimize (P6.5) at every query, turning the first inequality into equality:

  J_*=U_*(t_a,x_a)+∫_{t_a}^T r_*(t,x_*(t))dt
      ≤U_*(t_a,x_a)+H_T ε_B。                         （P6.12）

Together with (P6.11) and π_*∈A_D this gives (P6.8). π_p∈A_D gives J_p≥V_D^*; algebraic decomposition with (P6.7),(P6.8) gives (P6.9). QED.

This proof uses interior Hamiltonian minimization to construct comparison inequalities; it does not claim to solve state-constrained HJB boundary conditions. All comparison controls obey original state constraints and have actions in U. Even if some actions in U cannot preserve state constraints, minimization over this larger set still gives the lower-bound direction required by (P6.11). Complete candidate and reference feasibility is independently required.

6.4 Computing critic/configuration error

Unknown exact W_* does not prevent residual bounds. Fix known physical weights W_b and define
  U_b(t,x)=Φ(x;η_1)+ψ(t,x)^T W_b，
and their minimized Bellman residual r_b as in (P6.5). W_b may be numerical-anchor or candidate weights, evaluated with new η_1 terminal terms/dynamics.

Given reliable e_W,b≥||W_*-W_b||, for W_b=W_p use e_W,b=||E_W||ρ_Z. For anchor weights at η_0 with root bound ρ_0, theorem 1 permits

  e_W,b=||E_W||[ρ_0+c_Z||η_1-η_0||]。                （P6.13）

Over D and all allowed actions obtain

  B_W≥sup ||ψ_t(t,x)+J_ψ(t,x)f(t,x,u;η_1)||。        （P6.14）

using fixed rate bounds and domain bounds on basis/dynamics. Let
  a_W(u)=∂_tU_W+ℓ+∇_xU_W^T f。
At common (t,x,η_1,u), exact weight differences are

  a_{W_*}(u)-a_{W_b}(u)
      =[ψ_t+J_ψ f]^T(W_*-W_b)。                       （P6.15）

Two residuals minimize over the same U. Pointwise differences bounded by B_W e_W,b imply the same minimizer-value difference, hence

  |r_*(t,x)-r_b(t,x)|≤B_W e_W,b。                     （P6.16）

Under explicit fixed time/state scales, choose validation points ξ_l=(t_l,x_l) covering D with radius d_fill: every ξ∈D has ||ξ-ξ_l||≤d_fill. Verify residual Lipschitz L_B for these pairs and numerical residual errors
  |rtilde_b,l-r_b(ξ_l)|≤ε_r,l。
Then

  ε_B,b=max_l(|rtilde_b,l|+ε_r,l)+L_B d_fill，
  ε_B=ε_B,b+B_W e_W,b                                （P6.17）

suffices for (P6.6): select a covering point, use Lipschitz bounds, then (P6.16). d_fill and L_B share fixed scales; B_W uses physical weights. Do not mix units.

At validation points, item-3 feasible-action objectives and dual lower bounds enclose Hamiltonian minima; include other evaluation errors in ε_r,l. Exact action solves are unnecessary. Zero root residual at finitely many nodes does not replace d_fill or L_B.

Prove Lipschitz continuity of r_b on certified query-data/action-recovery domains, or use reliable direct residual-range enclosures. Node F∈C² in item 1 alone does not establish residual bounds over all D. Covering high-dimensional D may be expensive; this is a finite, checkable sufficient implementation, with no assertion that its cost is suitable online. Existing anchor domain certificates can be reused when model-parameter/weight-change bounds are explicitly covered; item 8 counts maintenance and checking costs.

ε_conf always concerns J_*-V_D^*. If W_b=W_p is used in (P6.17), its numerical upper bound may contain ρ_Z because of conservative certification of unknown W_*. This changes neither the two-level object decomposition (P6.9) nor the fact that this is not pure ANEOC truncation error.

6.5 Third term for numerical execution and true-model limits

The core theorem concerns exact nominal policies. Numerical actions/other bounded errors add a third difference. Let J_e^nom evaluate the execution trajectory with the same nominal ℓ,Φ. Always

  J_e^nom-V_D^*
    =(J_e^nom-J_p)+(J_p-J_*)+(J_*-V_D^*)。            （P6.18）

Suppose execution/nominal-candidate state separation is proved ≤β_ep(t), action error relative to π_p(t,x_e) is ≤ν_u(t), the candidate state Lipschitz constant L_πp,x is certified on relevant states, and (P6.3) cost constants also cover execution states/actions. The same cost comparison as (P6.7) gives

  |J_e^nom-J_p|≤ε_exec，
  ε_exec=L_Φβ_ep(T)
     +∫_{t_a}^T[(L_ℓx+L_ℓu L_πp,x)β_ep(t)
                            +L_ℓuν_u(t)]dt。         （P6.19）

Compute β_ep by item-4 comparison with reference f_p and execution disturbances, or use β_e+β_p when both are certified around y. Choose the tighter bound without adding them. Keep initial state and running/terminal costs consistent.

(P6.18) gives J_e^nom-V_D^*≤ε_exec+ε_pred+ε_conf. Nonnegativity additionally requires execution in the same A_D. With actual dynamics differing from nominal, this is a nominal-benchmark cost comparison, not actual-problem optimality gap.

A proved true-versus-nominal cost bias can be added. Replacing the benchmark by V_{D,true}^* also requires bounding V_D^*-V_{D,true}^*, including changed feasible sets. No such extra value-perturbation bound is assumed; the core claim remains nominal local near-optimal deployment.

6.6 Orders, meaning and scope

(a) Item-2 ideal quadratic root error gives ε_pred=O(||Δη||²) on fixed finite horizons with uniform constants, not automatic reduction of ε_conf. Fixed basis/node approximation floors require independent (P6.6) control; tighter root solves do not remove them.

(b) No fourth-order cost error is claimed. A collocation HJB–KKT root does not establish zero complete first-order closed-loop cost variation in critic weights, especially with action/state constraints. Such a claim needs proof. Reliable first-order Lipschitz comparison (P6.4) suffices here.

(c) Local D is part of the performance claim. A larger class leaving D generally gives V_global^*≤V_D^*. (P6.9) does not imply the same global bound. Extend only with residual coverage of the larger class or another domain-restriction cost bound; global extension is not required.

(d) Meaningful near-optimality requires numerical budgets. For predeclared ε_J,allow, certify ε_pred+ε_conf≤ε_J,allow plus full-horizon feasibility/domain coverage before releasing the corresponding nominal certificate. A finite but oversized bound is not high-accuracy near-optimality.

(e) Item 6 does not replace item 5: small cost gaps do not ensure feasibility, nor vice versa. Without residual coverage or reference feasibility retain only already-established root/policy/trajectory/candidate-feasibility results, not theorem-6 acceptance.

Record (t_a,x_a,η_1,T),D,A_D, both feasibility justifications, ε_pred constants, W_b,e_W,b, residual coverage/enclosures, ε_B,ε_conf,final ε_J and allowed loss. Execution extensions additionally record their third term and actual benchmark.

Mathematical sources
Predictor-cost bounds follow from item 3–4 errors and Lipschitz comparison of original cost functions. Continuous-control lower bounds follow directly from Hamiltonian comparison and chain-rule integration along arbitrary feasible controls, formalizing original R15 appendix I.5. The proof requires no smooth true value function and does not identify finite roots with true HJB solutions.

End of item 6.


7. Multistep errors and sufficient publish/correct/re-anchor/re-solve triggers

Prove finite successive-prediction recursion and local certified-domain corrector convergence, then combine items 1–6 into release/recovery conditions. Each release covers its fixed model, initial condition and horizon, not global safety/optimality under arbitrary switching.

7.1 Records and valid domain

A certificate records fixed equations/scales, (Zbar,ηbar),B_Z,P_cert,R,q,κ,c_Z,M_Z, derivative/branch bounds, and latest verified numerical anchor (Zhat_a,η_a) with E_a and sensitivity error ε_C,a for Chat_a. Index a denotes the latest verified anchor and k the parameter update.

Predicted candidates, certified anchors and released policies are distinct records. Release can use nonzero-error critics; publishing neither makes error zero nor generates a new sensitivity certificate.

First consider finite η_0,...,η_n within one item-1 domain. Check each η_k∈P_cert and write

  d_k=||η_{k+1}-η_k||，
  ||Zhat_k-Z_N^*(η_k)||≤E_k，
  ||Chat_k-D_ηZ_N^*(η_k)||≤ε_C,k。                    （P7.1）

If Chat_k uses derivatives at the current numerical point, check Zhat_k∈B_Z and certify derivatives/linear residuals under item 2. Window shifts must still be parameter changes in fixed F. Basis/node/scale/horizon/layout changes require a new configuration certificate.

7.2 Theorem 7A: multistep root recursion

If prediction k uses
  Zhat_{k+1}=Zhat_k+Chat_k(η_{k+1}-η_k)+e_p,k，
  ||e_p,k||≤ε_p,k，
and its parameter segment stays certified, propagate

  E_{k+1}=E_k+ε_C,k d_k+(M_Z/2)d_k²+ε_p,k。           （P7.2）

E_{k+1} is a bound, not necessarily monotonically growing actual error. Take the minimum with a tighter valid postprediction residual bound (P2.18) if available.

If sensitivities are recertified at each current numerical point, let

  a_C=κ(L_Zη+L_ZZ c_Z)，
  b_C,k=κ(ε_lin,k+ε_K,k+ε_J,k ν_C,k)，

Item 2 permits ε_C,k≤a_C E_k+b_C,k, hence

  E_{k+1}≤A_k E_k+B_k，
  A_k=1+a_C d_k，
  B_k=b_C,k d_k+(M_Z/2)d_k²+ε_p,k。                  （P7.3）

After finite n steps,

  E_n≤E_0 ∏_{k=0}^{n-1}A_k
      +Σ_{i=0}^{n-1} B_i ∏_{k=i+1}^{n-1}A_k。         （P7.4）

Empty products equal 1. Proof: apply theorem 2 exact decomposition (P2.14) at each step to obtain (P7.2); substitute (P2.4) for (P7.3); repeatedly substitute the recursion for (P7.4). Each step retains the existing error rather than relabeling the previous candidate as an exact root. QED.

Fixed tolerances generally accumulate in these bounds; predictor-only operation is not guaranteed indefinitely. Growing bounds do not prove equally growing actual errors. Add item-2 uncertainty for the current target separately, without repeatedly adding the same target error.

7.3 Frozen sensitivity and direct-anchor prediction

If Chat_a remains frozen, theorem-1 second-root bounds give, for η_k,η_a∈P_cert,

  ||D_ηZ_N^*(η_k)-D_ηZ_N^*(η_a)||
     ≤M_Z||η_k-η_a||，
  ε_C,k=ε_C,a+M_Z||η_k-η_a||                         （P7.5）

as sensitivity-error bounds at the new point. The first follows by integrating D_η²Z_N^* along the segment, the second by the triangle inequality. Use them in (P7.2), rather than reusing ε_C,a unchanged at every new base point.

A simpler single-anchor implementation computes each query as

  Z_p,k=Zhat_a+Chat_a(η_k-η_a)+e_p,k，
  E_p,k=E_a+ε_C,a||η_k-η_a||
             +(M_Z/2)||η_k-η_a||²+ε_p,k。             （P7.6）

Theorem 2 applies whenever the full anchor/target segment is certified. Intermediate estimates need not be processed and intermediate candidate errors do not accumulate, but anchor distance/numerical precision still limit accuracy. Sequential addition with identical Chat_a telescopes to the linear part of (P7.6) in exact arithmetic; floating-point accumulation needs separate accounting. Directly compute anchor-to-target differences with certified ε_p,k.

This retains original ANEOC directions without a new optimization problem. Certificate conditions determine anchor updates, not an unjustified fixed prediction count.

7.4 Theorem 7B: inexact corrector within the certified domain

Fix η∈P_cert and target Z_*=Z_N^*(η). Given correction start Z^0 with reliable E^0>0 satisfying

  ||Z^0-Z_*||≤E^0，
  ||Z^0-Zbar||+2E^0<r_Z，
  a_N E^0<σ<1， a_N=κ L_ZZ/2。                      （P7.7）

E^0 may be a prediction or certified-residual bound. E^0=0 already identifies the root and needs no convergence argument.
Consider returned full corrections d^j and stored-update errors e_up,j:

  Z^{j+1}=Z^j+d^j+e_up,j， ||e_up,j||≤ε_up,j，
  ||F(Z^j,η)+F_Z(Z^j,η)d^j||≤δ_j。                   （P7.8）

This residual is relative to exact mathematical F,F_Z. For approximate matrices/functions, bound δ_j by reported linear residual, function error and Jacobian error times ||d^j||, including rounding.

Let

  E^{j+1}=a_N(E^j)²+κδ_j+ε_up,j，
  κδ_j+ε_up,j≤(σ-a_N E^0)E^j。                       （P7.9）

If every iteration reaches this accuracy, all iterates remain in B_Z and

  ||Z^j-Z_*||≤E^j≤σ^j E^0→0。                        （P7.10）

Every positive ε_Z,target is reached after finitely many j with σ^j E^0≤ε_Z,target. This is an iteration result within a certified basin at attainable accuracy, not finite completion of every recovery path or a runtime guarantee.

Proof: suppose Z^j∈B_Z; convexity contains its segment to Z_*. Set J_j=F_Z(Z^j,η), r_j=F(Z^j,η)+J_j d^j. Integral expansion gives

  Z^{j+1}-Z_*
    =J_j^{-1}∫_0^1[J_j-F_Z(Z_*+s(Z^j-Z_*),η)]
                    (Z^j-Z_*)ds
      +J_j^{-1}r_j+e_up,j。

Using ||J_j^{-1}||≤κ and Jacobian Lipschitz L_ZZ bounds error by a_N||Z^j-Z_*||²+κδ_j+ε_up,j≤E^{j+1}.
(P7.9) and E^j≤E^0 give E^{j+1}≤σE^j≤E^0. Also

  ||Z^{j+1}-Zbar||
    ≤||Z^0-Zbar||+||Z^0-Z_*||+||Z^{j+1}-Z_*||
    ≤||Z^0-Zbar||+2E^0<r_Z。

so the new point lies in B_Z; induction gives (P7.10). QED.

Exact arithmetic/linear solves give δ_j=ε_up,j=0 and a_N(E^j)², standard local quadratic convergence. Fixed nonzero errors may eventually prevent (P7.9), precluding arbitrary-precision claims. Unverified damping/out-of-domain backtracking does not inherit this full-step theorem.

7.5 Release certificates and action meaning

For a candidate, define PublishOK as all established checks passing:

(i) Parameters, configuration and required branches remain covered by item 1; item 2 or this item supplies reliable ρ_Z≤ε_Z,allow; candidate membership passes when in-domain bounds are used there.
(ii) Item-3 arbitrary-query recovery and numerical-action certificates are valid.
(iii) Item-4 required full interval, well-posedness and domain retention are valid, as is item-5 candidate feasibility.
(iv) Item-6 domain, reference feasibility, residual coverage to T and ε_J≤ε_J,allow hold; any claimed execution extension also passes.
(v) Actual release initial state, parameters, references and timestamps match the certificate or are covered by explicitly certified initial/parameter/execution budgets.

Publish activates certified feedback/gain generation, not an exact-root label for Z_p. If feasibility passes without (iv), record candidate feasibility only, not release under this near-optimality PublishOK claim.

The robot moves during computation. Condition (v) requires rollout/remaining-horizon alignment to actual release state or an enclosure covering computation-period change. Certificates at computation start do not automatically cover delayed initial states. Equal numerical η with different physical reference-time origins still needs checking.

7.6 Sufficient triggers and guarantees

For the core claim, check release first, local recovery second, then exit the old certified branch. Preserve failure reasons rather than labeling every failure insufficient root accuracy.

Rule P: if PublishOK, publish directly.
Guarantee: items 3–6 give executable, feasible candidates with explicit ε_J in the declared nominal interval/domain, without a full HJB–KKT re-solve for this candidate. F(Z_p,η)=0 is unnecessary.

Rule C: if PublishOK fails but branch/configuration remain valid, a (P7.7) local start exists and (P7.9) accuracy is attainable, correction is permitted.
Guarantee: theorem 7B converges to the same certified root at attainable accuracy. Recheck PublishOK after corrections; convergence does not imply feasibility/performance acceptance.
This addresses numerical failures such as excessive root error. Repeated correction has no proved remedy for dominant ε_conf, physically infeasible candidates/references or invalid model domains.

Rule A: re-anchor when the current point supports a new item-1 certificate and accumulation, aged sensitivities or depleted domain margins no longer support needed future queries.
A computable warning is that worst (P7.2)/(P7.6) error over the declared next-query set exceeds budget, or that set leaves P_cert. Correct first only if needed for the current point to certify directly as a new anchor.
Guarantee: reset the recursion to (Zhat_a,new,η_a,new,E_a,new,Chat_a,new) only after the new item-1 record and anchor/sensitivity errors actually certify. E_a,new is generally nonzero. Relabeling indices, copying candidates or refactorizing a matrix alone is not certified re-anchoring.

To claim continuation of the old root, verify same-root identity at a shared parameter. Sufficiently, the new certified-root enclosure lies entirely in old B_Z and its parameter lies in old P_cert; old-domain uniqueness excludes another root. Local uniqueness joins branches nearby. Without overlap or equivalent certification, same-branch continuation is unproved.

Rule R: if configuration changes or needed parameter/contact/active branches lack old coverage and cannot be locally recertified, stop old smooth-predictor guarantees and enter re-solving recovery.
Coverage failure includes failed Jacobian/complementarity/contact certificates; it does not prove singular roots or necessary active-set switches. Old derivatives cannot cross changed branches unchecked; recovery must handle actual new constraints/branches.
Guarantee: re-solving yields an uncertified numerical candidate. Resume releases under this claim only after a new root record, items 3–6 and PublishOK pass. It may be another local root; do not assert identity without checking. Global solution from arbitrary starts is not proved.

If finite critic/configuration error dominates, resolving the same F may not help. First assess tighter residual certification; necessary basis/node changes define a new configured-root problem with rebuilt certificates, not ANEOC continuation under the old fixed configuration.

7.7 Theorem 7C: preservation of certified releases and recovery limits

Consider finite or locally finite update events, with finitely many in any bounded physical interval. Maintain reliable error records, apply 7.6 and enable candidates only under PublishOK. Every actual release then carries the preceding local error/feasibility/performance guarantees; numerical recovery never automatically certifies unverified solutions.

Proof: first release passes PublishOK, so items 3–6 apply. Given reliable previous records, prediction preserves bounds by 7A/(P7.6), correction preserves errors/same-root identity by 7B, and re-anchoring/re-solving replaces records only after actual certification. PublishOK rechecks initial states, models and required intervals. Induction proves certificate validity event by event, not existence of a next release after every recovery request. QED.

Operation between releases must remain covered by the executed policy's certificate. Reuse old certificates only for covered current states/parameters/remaining intervals; absence of a new release does not make the old policy safe in a changed environment.

If no PublishOK is obtained within budget and old coverage expires, switch to an independent fallback/supervisor. Safety during this period requires an existing guarantee on its current entry set. No generic safe controller is invented, and full solving/holding old gains is not a safety guarantee. Without a suitable interface/entry condition the theory cannot certify continued operation; report the gap.

Not all recovery paths terminate for arbitrary environmental changes. The 7B iteration bound is not wall-clock timing; deadlines require separate reliable solve/validation/scheduling costs.

Fixed-problem near-optimality does not imply global cumulative-cost near-optimality under repeated switching/receding horizons. That needs cross-window comparison beyond this claim. Local finiteness only excludes infinitely many switches in finite time; arbitrary triggers are not proved to satisfy it.

For correction record (P7.7) margins, (P7.8) residuals and actual recursion; for re-anchoring record new domains and required same-root overlap checks.

These give sufficient conditions for direct release, correction/re-anchoring and termination of old smooth-branch guarantees. They are not universal repair theorems and do not establish faster ANEOC; item 8 separately examines conditional computational advantage.

8. Conditional ANEOC computational-advantage proposition

Compare candidate generation and identical release certification on the same finite-dimensional HJB–KKT problem. Determine when maintained root sensitivities reduce critical-path latency/amortized cost, without claiming irreplaceability or inferring speed from error order.

8.1 Comparison basis and three candidates

Fix common F,nodes,basis,scales,parameter family,local branch,initial/trajectory domains,root accuracy and ε_J,allow. n=n_Z=22n_W and p=p_η=9+7(p_r+1). If only a declared parameter subset changes, compute its sensitivity columns and let p denote their count; do not ignore changes in excluded columns.

Use common (Z_a,η_a) and d=η-η_a. First define the cores at exact F(Z_a,η_a)=0 in exact arithmetic:

  J_a=F_Z(Z_a,η_a)， K_a=F_η(Z_a,η_a)，
  J_a C_a=-K_a，
  Z_A=Z_a+C_a d，                                     （P8.1）

  Z_C=Z_a-J_a^{-1}F(Z_a,η)，                          （P8.2）

  Z_N=Z_a-F_Z(Z_a,η)^{-1}F(Z_a,η)。                   （P8.3）

A is precomputed-sensitivity ANEOC, C cached-J chord reusing old factorization, and N one Newton step with new-parameter Jacobian. Inverses denote solves, not explicitly formed matrices. All may reuse the same old factorization and common model/certificate data; do not weaken chord by refactorizing each query.

Numerical variants include their own function/derivative/solve/arithmetic errors. A uses item 2; C/N may use item-1 residual/root certificates, and N item-7 correction bounds. Certify all at equal accuracy/feasibility under items 3–7.

RTI is a one-Newton/SQP-update online paradigm allowing preparation. Original RTI concerns finite-horizon trajectory optimization, not this collocation HJB root. Thus (P8.3) is a same-root one-Newton baseline, not a cost/convergence statement about all RTI. Compare concrete structured preparation/feedback RTI using actual phases and equal control requirements, without forcing full online matrix recomputation.

8.2 Equal-order candidates do not establish exclusive ANEOC advantage

Assume item-1 conditions throughout the comparison region, Z_a=Z_N^*(η_a), and the d segment in P_cert. Theorem 2 gives

  ||Z_A-Z_N^*(η)||≤(M_Z/2)||d||²。                    （P8.4）

At fixed Z_a, parameter expansion gives

  F(Z_a,η)=K_a d+r_η，
  ||r_η||≤(L_ηη/2)||d||²。

Hence Z_C=Z_A-J_a^{-1}r_η and

  ||Z_C-Z_N^*(η)||
      ≤(M_Z+κL_ηη)||d||²/2。                         （P8.5）

For (P8.3), the new-parameter Newton identity with
  ||Z_a-Z_N^*(η)||≤c_Z||d||，
gives

  ||Z_N-Z_N^*(η)||≤κ L_ZZ c_Z²||d||²/2。             （P8.6）

This needs Z_a, the new root and their segment in B_Z and new η∈P_cert, supplied by the common certificate. The constants have no fixed ranking; inexact implementations add their own floors.

All three may meet the same release budget. Quadratic ANEOC prediction alone does not imply fewer corrections, larger certified radii or better control. Count actual baseline corrections when needed, without presupposing them.

8.3 Unified cost model

Consider a valid reuse interval between anchor/configuration updates with Q queries. Common anchor solves, old factorizations and genuinely identical work form C_common. Cancel only truly identical work, not merely identically named tasks.

Initially use a shared-resource serial within-query dependency model. All costs share units and include data movement/accuracy needs. For parallel devices replace sums by actual dependency critical paths; operation counts are not wall-clock time.

Let P_A,P_C,P_N be preparation beyond common work. A includes K_a evaluation, multi-right-hand-side C_a solution, storage and sensitivity/domain-certificate maintenance. Write
  P_A=C_K+C_multi(n,p)+C_store+C_A,certprep。
Include allowed baseline precomputation in P_C,P_N too; they need not be zero.

Without further specialized structure, query work decomposes as

  O_A,l=C_mv(n,p)+C_ax(n)，
  O_C,l=C_F,l+C_solve(n,1)+C_ax(n)，
  O_N,l=C_F,l+C_J,l+C_fact(n)+C_solve(n,1)+C_ax(n)。    （P8.7）

C_mv multiplies stored C_a by d; C_ax updates vectors; C_F evaluates new residuals; C_J,C_fact evaluate/factor new Jacobians. Use actual joint evaluation costs without duplication. Replace terms for low-rank updates, Schur elimination, sparse/matrix-free methods; do not impose dense costs.

For i∈{A,C,N}, also write

  V_i,l: candidate/release certification, including root-error checks,
          arbitrary-state action recovery, rollout, constraints/performance,
          release-time alignment and required scheduling/data movement;
  R_i,l: actual additional correction, recertification or recovery for this query;
  S_i,l=O_i,l+V_i,l+R_i,l。                            （P8.8）

Share common certificates where valid, retaining differences from candidate-specific grids, bounds or recoveries. R_i,l=0 for predictor-only successes. Accepted numerical roots without PublishOK are not completed comparable releases.

8.4 Proposition 8: sufficient critical-path/amortized comparisons

Require equal information-availability times, accuracy/release conditions and actual certification completion. Let U_i,l≥0 be unfinished preparation blocking the candidate at parameter arrival, and W_i,l≥0 comparable scheduling/resource waits. In the serial model, event-to-release latency is

  L_i,l=U_i,l+W_i,l+S_i,l。                            （P8.9）

For any B∈{C,N}, if

  S_B,l-S_A,l>
        (U_A,l-U_B,l)+(W_A,l-W_B,l)，                 （P8.10）

ANEOC has strictly lower latency for that query. With all preparation complete, equal waiting and no correction, this reduces to

  O_B,l-O_A,l>V_A,l-V_B,l。                            （P8.11）

For Q queries over the same reuse interval, total service cost is

  T_i=C_common+P_i+Σ_{l=1}^Q S_i,l。                  （P8.12）

If

  Σ_{l=1}^Q(S_B,l-S_A,l)>P_A-P_B，                    （P8.13）

then T_A<T_B, hence lower mean service cost over the same tasks. If S_B,l-S_A,l≥d_B>0 reliably for each query and P_A≥P_B, a sufficient check is

  Q>(P_A-P_B)/d_B。                                   （P8.14）

For P_A<P_B use (P8.13) directly, without forcing a nonnegative break-even count. To beat both C and N, satisfy both inequalities separately.

Proof: subtract (P8.9) to obtain
  L_B,l-L_A,l=(S_B,l-S_A,l)
                -(U_A,l-U_B,l)-(W_A,l-W_B,l)，
so (P8.10) makes the difference positive. Subtract (P8.12):
  T_B-T_A=Σ(S_B,l-S_A,l)-(P_A-P_B)，
Thus (P8.13) suffices; using Qd_B gives (P8.14). QED.

These are testable conditions, not big-O comparisons of constants. Strict timing certification from intervals requires ANEOC upper bounds versus baseline lower bounds; two upper bounds do not establish actual ordering. Measured averages support only the corresponding empirical/statistical setting.

8.5 Parameter dimension and query frequency

For generic dense direct algebra with old factorization already common, one right-hand side typically costs Θ(n²), multiple right-hand sides Θ(n²p), full sensitivity storage Θ(np) and prediction Θ(np). New dense factorization typically costs Θ(n³). These describe operation composition; both methods may exploit all structure of the same F.

To make (P8.14) concrete, a chosen calibrated model may set

  C_multi=b n²p， C_solve=s n²， C_mv=m np，
  P_A=C_K+b n²p+C_store+C_A,certprep，                 （P8.15）

with positive model coefficients b,s,m, not assumed equal. For chord with only common old factorization, P_C=0. If neither recovers, vector updates match and certification costs are V_A,V_C,

  d_C=C_F+s n²-m np+V_C-V_A。                         （P8.16）

Only if d_C>0 and

  Q>[C_K+b n²p+C_store+C_A,certprep]/d_C               （P8.17）

does this model give amortized advantage over that chord implementation. Effective extra baseline precomputation requires its actual P_C/query costs instead.

(P8.16)–(P8.17) favor relatively few changing parameters, many queries per anchor, expensive residual/linear solves and controlled certificate overhead. Large p or quickly invalid anchors may erase gains through preparation/memory traffic. p<n alone does not establish (P8.17).

A new-Jacobian Newton query also includes C_J+C_fact, but completed RTI preparation moves those off the feedback critical path into preparation costs. Denying RTI precomputation cannot justify ANEOC advantage.

For reuse duration H_reuse, actual Q depends jointly on query rate and certificate validity. At fixed f_query use actually completed Q in (P8.14), not unverified expected reuse to guarantee worst-case amortization. Branch failures, re-anchoring and reconfiguration preparation belong in new intervals or (P8.8) recovery, not nonexistent future queries.

8.6 When spatial preview enters latency conditions

Spatial contact preview can supply earlier information, but guarantees neither exact knowledge of final descriptor η nor a fixed-length preparation window. With anchor and model data available, C_a preparation is independent of the final arriving d and can precede it. Only actual scheduling/resources that complete the preparation allow U_A,l=0 in (P8.9).

The computational role is earlier certifiable local environment/task information, reusable anchors/sensitivities, then covered parameter queries. Record data availability, preparation start/completion and final parameter arrival. Preview distance alone is not a proved time budget.

Give baselines the same sensor data/preparation time. If full new η is already known, they can precompute residuals, Newton steps or whole solutions, changing U_B,O_B; ANEOC has no inherent exclusive advantage. Background work sharing online resources adds waits W_i,l and is not free.

No artificial requirement places all adjustments between leading-edge contact and center arrival. Actual timing and (P8.10),(P8.13) determine advantage.

8.7 Required equivalence/no-advantage cases

(a) If chord also linearizes the new residual as K_a d, at an exact anchor its update is
  Z_a-J_a^{-1}K_a d=Z_a+C_a d。
Caching -J_a^{-1}K_a makes it identical to (P8.1). No strict exclusive ANEOC advantage exists over an equivalent computation. Position contributions in the concrete sensitivity/preview/certified-release method and its conditions, not renaming a linear operation as indispensable.

(b) If F is affine in parameters at the fixed anchor, (P8.2),(P8.1) may coincide mathematically; structural precomputation may equalize query costs too. For nonlinear parameters, candidate error constants differ and PublishOK acceptance needs actual checking.

(c) One query, unfinished preparation, high parameter dimension, expensive domain certificates or frequent branch failures give no unconditional acceleration. Even faster candidates may barely improve end-to-end latency when V_i,l dominates.

(d) Equal-order errors do not guarantee common radii. Margins, budgets and numerical errors differ; A-favorable queries are not universal performance. Record successes, failures and recoveries on one predefined sequence. If comparing only all-direct-release successes, limit conclusions to that intersection and separately report excluded events.

(e) Item 7 does not guarantee all recovery termination; do not invent finite service costs for algorithms failing equal-task completion. Report noncompletion/reasons rather than comparing unequal quality/completion as equal speed.

8.8 Conclusions and validation records

The same-order properties and sufficient ANEOC critical-path/amortized conditions under explicit cost/timing assumptions are proved. They neither require saving one Newton step on active branches nor follow automatically from items 1–7.
