R15: contact estimation, optimal impedance and ANEOC: supplementary derivations
Companion document: method_design.md

Scope
This appendix supports the complete method in the main text. A–C derive contact relations, spatial integrals and derivatives; D–F support identification, online solution and uncertainty treatment; G specifies contact-model and estimation validation. H–M cover impedance dynamics and energy, HJB and finite-dimensional roots, certified prediction and on-demand correction, closed-loop release conditions and supplementary numerical checks.
The complete certification proofs are in theoretical_proofs.txt in this directory, referred to below as the certification proofs. Items 1–2 correspond to J, 3–5 to L, 6 to I.5, 7 to K, and 8 to K.4 and M. Z^* here and Z_N^* in those proofs denote the same finite-dimensional root; physical and normalized quantities are converted using fixed scales.
Homogeneous cylindrical relations, a cross-region effective model, finite-dimensional HJB roots, continuous optimal control and real-tissue closed-loop operation are different levels. The analytical statements below apply to the specified models and local conditions. Numerical checks do not replace validation of real contact, closed-loop performance or real-time execution.
Reference numbers follow the main text. All numerical optimization variables, residuals and tolerances must use fixed reference scales; numerical magnitudes in N, N·m, m and Pa cannot be compared directly.

A. Homogeneous cylindrical contact and indentation reference

A.1 Load, pressure and units
The local right-handed coordinates are x along scanning, y along the cylinder axis, and z along the normal force exerted by tissue on the probe. R, L and a are lengths.
P=F_n/L has units N/m; E* has units Pa; q_n=Lp_H has units N/m.
The cylindrical Hertz solution is
  p_H(x)=E*/(2R) sqrt(a²-x²)，|x|≤a，
  P=∫p_H dx=πE*a²/(4R)，F_n=πLE*a²/(4R)。
Thus a²=4PR/(πE*), consistent with main-text equation (4). [3]
For a rigid cylinder contacting isotropic tissue, E*=E_t/(1-ν_t²). Identifying E* does not require separate identification of E_t and ν_t.

A.2 Why indentation needs a reference
Far-field displacement in a two-dimensional half-space contains a logarithmic term; the spherical Hertz indentation relation cannot be copied directly.
Fix a lateral distance ℓ=ℓ_ref>a and define δ as the normal displacement of the contact center relative to the mean displacement at the two reference positions. The cylindrical half-space displacement solution [3] gives
  δ=Ψ(a)
   ={ℓ²-ℓ sqrt(ℓ²-a²)+a² ln[(ℓ+sqrt(ℓ²-a²))/a]}/(2R)。
This defines a displacement-difference reference; it neither fixes the actual tissue support boundary at two points nor gives an exact finite-thickness-layer solution. Probe pose, calibrated reference surface and γ must use the same displacement reference. Absolute robot position alone does not determine δ.
γ describes normal motion of that reference, and d is probe indentation position relative to the calibrated reference, so δ=d-γ. ℓ is a fixed calibrated geometric reference; b is the material-boundary position to estimate. They are unrelated.

To avoid cancellation in subtracting ℓ sqrt(ℓ²-a²) from ℓ², evaluate
  Ψ(a)=a²/(2R){ℓ/[ℓ+sqrt(ℓ²-a²)]+ln[(ℓ+sqrt(ℓ²-a²))/a]}。
Direct differentiation gives
  Ψ'(a)=a/R ln[(ℓ+sqrt(ℓ²-a²))/a]>0，0<a<ℓ。
Hence Ψ is strictly increasing on the chosen interval [a_min,a_max]⊂(0,ℓ), with
  δ_min=Ψ(a_min)，δ_max=Ψ(a_max)，a_δ=1/Ψ'(a)。
This establishes uniqueness and local differentiability of the half-width solution.
For ℓ/a≫1,
  Ψ(a)=a²/(2R){ln(2ℓ/a)+1/2+O(a²/ℓ²)}。
a²/(2R) is a geometric height-difference scale within contact, not the complete δ under this displacement reference.

B. Cross-region loads, resultant forces and moments

B.1 Nature of the spatial model
The main text chooses
  q_n(x)=C E*(s+x;b) g(x)，C=L/(2R)，g(x)=sqrt(a²-x²)，
  a=Ψ^-1(d-γ)，
with a two-region piecewise-constant parameter field along scanning.
At sample i the footprint is C_i=[s_i-a_i,s_i+a_i], with boundary coordinate r_i=b-s_i. Scanning along +x, r_i=a_i is first boundary contact by the leading edge s_i+a_i; for 0<r_i<a_i, material ahead contributes load before the center reaches the boundary; for -a_i<r_i≤0, the center has crossed while the footprint remains mixed; for r_i≤-a_i, the whole footprint is in the forward region. These relations locate information availability, not a required algorithm-completion position.
The expression assumes a shared contact width and Hertz shape on both sides, with local modulus scaling load amplitude. Its homogeneous limit exactly recovers A.1. Elastic displacement in heterogeneous regions generally has nonlocal coupling, so this is not an exact heterogeneous-half-space contact solution. Positive moduli and small contrast alone do not establish a specific approximation-error order.
Here θ denotes effective contact parameters over this operating range. Interpreting estimates as true tissue material constants requires independent material calibration and cross-region contact validation. Generating and fitting data with the same formula checks inversion implementation, not the spatial approximation.
The same spatial parameterization directly supplies MHE and future-contact prediction; its inputs, parameters and integration weights are not redefined between them.

B.2 Closed-form integrals
Write r=b-s, I_0=πa²/2 and I_2=πa⁴/8. For partial coverage |r|<a, let w=sqrt(a²-r²) and t=arccos(r/a). Then
  I_f  = (a²t-rw)/2，
  I_1f = w³/3，
  I_2f = a⁴t/8+rw(a²-2r²)/8。
These are respectively ∫_r^a g dx, ∫_r^a xg dx and ∫_r^a x²g dx.
All three are 0 for r≥a; for r≤-a they are I_0, 0 and I_2. The first moment of the complete footprint is 0.
Let ΔE=E_f*-E_0*. Then
  F_n=C[E_0*I_0+ΔE I_f]，
  m_1=CΔE I_1f。
E_0*,E_f*>0 and a>0 imply F_n>0. Nonnegative load alone does not establish a three-dimensional continuum energy model or full closed-loop passivity.

B.3 Tangential distribution and homogeneous limit
Fix the sliding direction σ=sgn(v) and take
  q_t(x)=-σ μ(s+x;b)q_n(x)-v k_d(s+x;b)g(x)/I_0。
The second term is the assumed distributed equivalent viscous resistance; k_d in N·s/m is a resultant-force coefficient for the specified L.
Since ∫g/I_0 dx=1, integration gives
  F_t=-σ(μ_0N_0+μ_fN_f)-v[(1-α)k_d,0+αk_d,f]，
  N_0=CE_0*(I_0-I_f)，N_f=CE_f*I_f，α=I_f/I_0。
For homogeneous parameters, F_t=-σμF_n-k_dv.
Under the uniform relative sliding velocity v of this local model,
  F_t v=-|v|(μ_0N_0+μ_fN_f)
        -v²[(1-α)k_d,0+αk_d,f]≤0。
This is only a tangential-resistance dissipation relation. It omits external power from tissue-reference motion and is not an impedance-controller stability proof.

The spherical-geometry correction in T-RO III-A cannot be transferred unchanged to a cylinder. [2] The main text uses the small-slope tangential-resultant structure; the exact cylindrical profile is
  z_c=R-sqrt(R²-x²)=x²/(2R)+O(x⁴/R³)。
This specifies geometric profile-truncation error, not the total error order of all contact/friction approximations. Normal–tangential deformation coupling still needs validation.

B.4 Moments, sensor transforms and signs
The contact reference is the lowest point of the cylinder cross-section. An element at (x,0,z_c) carries force (q_t,0,q_n)dx, hence
  dM_y=(z_c q_t-xq_n)dx。
Substituting the main-text small-slope profile gives the complete closed-form observation:
  M_y=-CΔE I_1f
      -σ C/(2R)[μ_0E_0*(I_2-I_2f)+μ_fE_f*I_2f]
      -v/(2R I_0)[k_d,0(I_2-I_2f)+k_d,f I_2f]。
Tangential load can produce a moment about this reference even in homogeneous material. Thus m_1=-M_y cannot be assumed; subtract the tangential-moment contribution to recover the normal first moment.

Let ^B T_P,i be the forward-kinematic probe pose and ^P T_C the calibrated probe-to-contact-reference transform. From
  ^B p_c,i=trans(^B T_P,i ^P T_C)
obtain the contact-reference position. The local scanning frame is fixed within each MHE window; its origin is ^B p_o and its basis vectors e_x,e_z are expressed in the base frame. Under the positive-indentation convention of the main text,
  s_i=e_x^T(^B p_c,i-^B p_o)，
  d_i=-e_z^T(^B p_c,i-^B p_o)，
  v_i=(s_i-s_(i-1))/h。
This difference must use synchronized timestamps. For noisy data, preprocessing may use a local velocity estimate with known phase delay, included in time alignment. Significant tangential tissue motion makes s_i cease to be a material coordinate, outside the stated scope.

First compensate sensor readings for gravity and inertia under the chosen sign convention. If r_SC points from sensor origin to contact reference in sensor coordinates and R_CS rotates sensor coordinates to contact coordinates, then
  F_C=R_CS F_S，
  M_C=R_CS(M_S-r_SC×F_S)。
Extract y=(F_C,z,F_C,x,M_C,y)^T and compare with H_H=(F_n,F_t,M_y)^T. Calibrate compensated signs as forces exerted by tissue on the probe.
These three components are modeled for local straight scanning. A six-dimensional sensor does not imply independent parameter information in all six components.

C. Derivatives, half-width solution and contact dynamics

C.1 Analytical integral derivatives
For |r|<a, take each partial derivative holding the other independent variable fixed:
  ∂_r I_f=-w，        ∂_a I_f=a t；
  ∂_r I_1f=-r w，     ∂_a I_1f=a w；
  ∂_r I_2f=-r²w，     ∂_a I_2f=(a/2)(a²t+rw)；
  ∂_a I_0=πa，        ∂_a I_2=πa³/2。
Also r_s=-1, r_b=1 and a_δ=1/Ψ'(a), so
  F_n,δ=C[E_0*πa+ΔE a t]/Ψ'(a)，
  F_n,s=CΔE w，F_n,b=-CΔE w，
  F_n,E0=C(I_0-I_f)，F_n,Ef=C I_f。
Since t∈(0,π), E_0*π+ΔE t=E_0*(π-t)+E_f*t>0, hence F_n,δ>0.
Use the corresponding homogeneous-region formulas in the two complete-coverage regions.
Other output derivatives follow by product and quotient rules applied to B.3–B.4. Derivatives of α must include the dependence of denominator I_0 on a. For a fixed sign of v,
  F_t,v=-[(1-α)k_d,0+αk_d,f]，
  M_y,v=-[k_d,0(I_2-I_2f)+k_d,f I_2f]/(2R I_0)。

C.2 Smoothness
The functions are smooth for a>0 within |r|<a or either open complete-coverage interval.
At r=±a, I_f, I_1f, I_2f and their first derivatives join continuously, but some second derivatives contain 1/sqrt(a²-r²). Uniformly bounded second derivatives across the edges cannot be claimed.
First-order chain differentiation may therefore use the continuously joined model. Classical second-order Taylor remainders and conclusions requiring second-order regularity must be restricted to the corresponding smooth neighborhoods.
Friction-sign changes at v=0 are outside the fixed-direction sliding window considered here.

C.3 Scalar solution
Each contact evaluation solves Ψ(a)-δ=0 on [a_min,a_max]. Monotonicity gives a unique root when endpoint signs differ.
Maintain a bracket [l,u] and first try the Newton candidate
  a_N=a-[Ψ(a)-δ]/Ψ'(a)。
If it lies outside a fixed central portion, for example [l+0.1(u-l),u-0.1(u-l)], use the midpoint. Update the bracket using the sign of Ψ. Every step contracts the interval while retaining the true root.
At termination report both the half-width interval and indentation residual. If Ψ'≥m_Ψ>0 throughout the interval,
  |a_num-a*|≤|Ψ(a_num)-δ|/m_Ψ。
C.1 derivative bounds propagate this error to H_H. Choose a_min>0 and a_max<ℓ to avoid endpoint degeneracy. Model evaluation needs only this scalar root and closed-form integrals.

C.4 Chain rule for force states
In a homogeneous region with fixed parameters and sliding direction,
  F_n=πLE*a²/(4R)，F_t=-σμF_n-k_dv，M_y=a²F_t/(8R)。
Combining a_dot=(d_dot-γ_dot)/Ψ'(a) gives
  F_n_dot=[πLE*a/(2RΨ'(a))](d_dot-γ_dot)，
  F_t_dot=-σμF_n_dot-k_dv_dot，
  M_y_dot=a a_dot F_t/(4R)+a²F_t_dot/(8R)。
This is main-text equation (6c), the cylindrical-model counterpart of the T-RO force-state derivative.

For a fixed material field within a window, δ=d-γ and ds/dt=v. Cross-region contact additionally requires the spatial term, yielding
  dF_n/dt=F_n,δ(dδ/dt)+F_n,s(ds/dt)，
  dH_H/dt=H_H,δ(dδ/dt)+H_H,s v+H_H,v(dv/dt)。
The boundary enters F_n,s through r=b-s, already accounting for scan-induced spatial parameter variation.
If material parameters themselves vary substantially in time within a window, include H_H,ϑ ϑdot. The current constant-parameter-window assumption excludes this variation. Numerical estimator updates must not be treated directly as true material time derivatives.
Robot Jacobian, wrench and moment reference must agree. A rotating contact frame also requires time derivatives of coordinate transforms; the locally fixed frame assumed here produces no such additional term.

D. Why estimation is possible and which information is indispensable

D.1 An identifiable example for magnitude and position of a normal-property change
Assume a and E_0* are known, the normal first moment has been separated from total moment, |r|<a and ΔE≠0. Let
  ΔF_n=F_n-CE_0*I_0=CΔE I_f，
  ρ=m_1/ΔF_n=I_1f/I_f。
By C.1,
  dρ/dr=w(ρ-r)/I_f>0。
Indeed, ρ is the mean of x on (r,a) under positive weight g(x), hence strictly exceeds r.
For given a, the ratio ρ therefore uniquely determines r within partial coverage; then
  ΔE=ΔF_n/(C I_f)
recovers the change magnitude. The Jacobian can also be checked directly:
  det[∂(ΔF_n,m_1)/∂(ΔE,r)]
    =C²ΔE w(I_1f-rI_f)≠0。
This establishes distinguishable magnitude/position cases in this spatial model, not sufficiency of a single observation when surface, bias and all friction parameters are simultaneously unknown.
For ΔE=0 these normal signals do not locate the boundary; as r approaches a, forward contribution vanishes and noise sensitivity grows sharply. Other observations may still locate friction changes: invisibility in normal signals does not imply invisibility in all observations.

D.2 Excitation of friction parameters
If E_0*, E_f*, b and a_i are known, write tangential force as the linear regression
  -F_t,i =
  [σ_iN_0,i, σ_iN_f,i, v_i(1-α_i), v_iα_i]
  [μ_0, μ_f, k_d,0, k_d,f]^T。
Full column rank of the stacked regression matrix is necessary and sufficient for unique noiseless identification of these four parameters.
With fixed a and nonzero v, N_0=CE_0*I_0(1-α) and N_f=CE_f*I_0α, making the corresponding column pairs proportional. Coverage variation alone cannot separate μ from k_d. Sufficient load/velocity-regressor variation and effective coverage of both sides are needed. Moments add observations but do not replace checking the actual joint Jacobian rank.
Reliable historical calibration of the current region constrains θ_0; parameter priors are not identification information supplied by new data.

D.3 Surface motion and bias
Unknown γ changes a; unknown β directly changes observations. Both can confound material changes.
Under the specified local surface-motion parameterization, let J_θ and J_n be material and nuisance Jacobians of normalized stacked observations. Use
  J_eff=(I-J_n J_n^†)J_θ
to check which first-order material variations cannot be explained by allowed nuisance changes.
If all three biases can vary freely at every instant, J_n spans every observation direction and J_eff=0. The main text limits these variations through calibrated initial states and the short-time statistics stated in Q. Estimation accuracy thus depends on these assumptions; unconditional joint identifiability cannot be claimed.
A positive-definite regularized Hessian only establishes a locally determined regularized problem, not data identification of all parameters. Explain data, surface-motion assumptions and parameter-prior contributions separately.

E. Mathematical MHE problem and online solution

E.1 Variables, timing and existence
ϑ=(E_0*,μ_0,k_d,0,E_f*,μ_f,k_d,f,b) has 7 variables;
z_i=(γ_i,γdot_i,β_i^T)^T has 5 variables.
For N observations, the joint variable dimension is 7+5N, with N observation-residual terms and N-1 state-propagation terms. z_j is the state at the first observation.
P is the window-start state-prior covariance; Λ is material/boundary prior precision; Q describes short-time adjacent-state variation; R_y describes effective random observation error. Units and sampling periods must be consistent.
With calibrated geometry, a nonempty compact variable domain and δ restricted to [Ψ(a_min),Ψ(a_max)], H_H and the objective are continuous, so the exact constrained problem has a global minimizer. This establishes neither uniqueness nor global convergence of SQP.

E.2 Practical iteration
After shifting the window, warm-start retained times from the previous solution and propagate the new state with A; update the start prior by estimate propagation. The arrival mean/covariance for θ_0 comes from the pretransition homogeneous region or previous window, and MHE adjusts it within that covariance. Wider physical priors initialize θ_f and b, then new-window data update them. Previous estimates are not zero-uncertainty truth; treating both θ_0 and θ_f as free without arrival information cannot justify identifiability of parameter changes.
At the first transition window, initialize b within the observed contact-change range. Given this candidate b, initial indentation and I_f>0,
  E_f,init*=E_0*+[F_n-CE_0*I_0]/(C I_f)
gives an initial modulus consistent with the change signal; project it to physical bounds. Initialize friction from history and window regression. This constructs an initial guess, not exact one-step inversion with unknown γ and β.
Identical initialization of all parameters on both sides may give zero sensitivity to b; such an initialization does not already contain boundary information.

At each iteration:
  Compute all δ_i and solve a_i using C.3;
  Evaluate H_H using B.2–B.4 and chain derivatives using C.1;
  Stack whitened residuals and constraints and construct an SQP subproblem;
  Backtrack using a merit function including constraint violation;
  Reevaluate contact at the candidate point until stopping criteria hold.
Contact integrals and derivatives scale linearly with N; outer linear algebra depends on sparsity and iteration count, so this does not establish O(N) cost for all MHE.

E.3 Local convergence and stopping
Classical local SQP convergence requires a solution within one smooth region, linear independence of active constraints, strict complementarity, second-order sufficiency, appropriate Hessian approximations and globalization. Second-order smoothness conclusions do not directly extend across footprint edges.
Report normalized feasibility ε_feas, KKT stationarity/complementarity ε_stat and contact-root error ε_a online. Numerical thresholds must be below allowed contact-prediction error scales. Iterates failing the criteria are not converged local optima.
Finite windows, warm starts and low-dimensional closed forms provide a computational approach, not fixed iteration/sample-count guarantees. MHE reference [4] supports constrained finite-window estimation; its stability results retain their own assumptions and do not prove convergence of all parameters in this model.

F. Local uncertainty and prediction error

F.1 Joint covariance
Write the main-text J_k as ||r(𝒵)||², including covariance-whitened observations, propagation and prior residuals. Use the local quadratic approximation of (1/2)||r||² as a Gaussian negative log posterior, with J_r=∂r/∂𝒵.
With no active constraints and full-column-rank J_r,
  Σ_𝒵≈(J_r^T J_r)^-1。
For local free directions parameterized by a tangent basis T of fixed active constraints,
  Σ_ζ≈S T(T^T J_r^T J_r T)^-1 T^T S^T，
where S selects ζ=(ϑ,z_k). The reduced information matrix must be invertible.
This is a local Gauss–Newton/Laplace approximation. Active inequalities restrict it to a fixed face; it is not the exact covariance of a truncated posterior. If regularization is nonstatistical, its inverse is only a local curvature indicator.
Numerical damping cannot be interpreted as new real information. Report the limitations of the local covariance when parameters are mainly prior-determined, the information matrix is ill-conditioned or other fitting branches exist.
From the linear map Δθ=θ_f-θ_0,
  Σ_Δθ=D_ΔΣ_ζD_Δ^T，
including cross-covariances between sides. Adding marginal variances while ignoring correlation is invalid.

F.2 Model mismatch
R_y can represent calibrated random error; increasing covariance weights does not remove systematic contact-approximation bias.
At a zero-residual reference with fixed local free directions and small perturbations, if the whitened residual at fixed parameters changes externally by δr_ext,
  δξ≈-(J_red^T J_red)^-1 J_red^T δr_ext，
where J_red is the whitened-residual Jacobian in free coordinates. Taking norms gives
  ||δξ||≲||δr_ext||/σ_min(J_red)。
Weak information amplifies model bias. This is not a global bound for arbitrary nonlinear residuals. Exact optimizer sensitivity at nonzero residual requires the full Lagrangian Hessian and constraint derivatives, not only Gauss–Newton curvature.
True cross-region model error requires independent data or a credible reference contact solver. The spatial approximation cannot certify its own small error.

F.3 Future-contact prediction
For a fixed material field, the state predicted ℓ_p sampling steps ahead is
  z_hat,k+ℓp=A^ℓp z_hat,k。
If process noise is independent as assumed in the main text, its additional state covariance is
  Q_pred=Σ_(j=0)^(ℓp-1) A^j Q(A^j)^T。
Let B_ℓp=blkdiag(I_7,A^ℓp). Then
  Σ_ζ,pred≈B_ℓpΣ_ζ,kB_ℓp^T+blkdiag(0_(7×7),Q_pred)。
For candidate motion, use the physical output G(ζ_pred)=H_H(d-γ_pred,s,v;ϑ) to compute
  Σ_W≈G_ζΣ_ζ,predG_ζ^T。
β does not enter G, so its direct derivative is zero. Uncertain candidate motion requires separate propagation of its error.
This propagates only a local approximation to the stated random uncertainty. Report systematic contact-model bias separately; covariance is not a global guaranteed range for true force.

G. Operating range, numerical checks and practical feasibility

G.1 Establishing the operating range
Validate the cylindrical approximation for target R, L, loads and scanning velocities: homogeneous force–indentation behavior, sliding resistance and cross-region forces/moments must meet task accuracy. Finite-thickness/end effects, inelasticity and unobserved tangential tissue motion contribute real mismatch.
Independent cross-region observations must test B.1; homogeneous fitting alone does not validate it. Allowed model error follows downstream impedance-task prediction requirements, rather than being relaxed arbitrarily to pass fitting.
This is a specific cylindrical effective-contact-model route. Insufficient accuracy means current parameter inversion has not solved the real-tissue research question.

G.2 Completed synthetic algebraic checks

with E_0*=30000 Pa and E_f*=45000 Pa. Independently integrate the three B.2 integrals using SciPy, check Ψ' by central differences, then invert r and E_f* from ΔF_n and m_1 using D.1.

G.3 Timing and presently supported conclusions
Actual latency includes data accumulation, synchronization, all MHE iterations, parameter extraction and model transfer; subsequent algorithms add impedance solution and execution. Report estimation/prediction error and latency versus contact-change speed together.
No mandatory leading-edge-to-center completion position is imposed, but rapid adjustment after contact changes still requires latency compatible with task timescales. Fast single contact-function evaluation is insufficient.
The derivations establish homogeneous cylindrical relations, observation integrals under a selected spatial approximation, nondegenerate identification examples and implementable local MHE equations. Real-tissue cross-region approximation error, full-MHE runtime and accuracy still require independent validation, beyond algebraic checks.

H. Robot–contact–impedance state space and energy

H.1 Coordinates, forces and state closure
For a robot with n_r joints and q_r,w_r∈R^{n_r}, within a fixed contact-frame segment take
  ξ(q_r)=(p_c,x,p_c,y,p_c,z,ϑ_1,ϑ_2,ϑ_3)^T，J_ξ=∂ξ/∂q_r。
The final three entries use a fixed smooth local orientation chart, not globally subtractable angles under arbitrary rotations. Fix its operating domain with the calibrated probe-pose range. The first three entries use the section 3 origin, so s=p_c,x and d=-p_c,z. Do not mix d with the physical +z normal convention.
Controller generalized force f_ξ is power-conjugate to ξ_dot:
  w_r^T J_ξ^T f_ξ=(J_ξw_r)^T f_ξ。
J_c maps w_r to physical linear/angular velocity at the same contact reference, hence
  w_r^T J_c^T 𝒲_c^(6)=(J_cw_r)^T𝒲_c^(6)。
The local-orientation-coordinate Jacobian generally differs from the physical-angular-velocity Jacobian. Use J_ξ^T for control and J_c^T for contact wrench; local orientation generalized force need not be called a physical torque.

For fixed η, γ(t)=γ_0+tγdot_0; ξ_d(t) and F_d(t) are fixed-degree polynomials. Compute δ,s,v from q_r,w_r, then a and 𝒲_c using A–C. Substituting main-text τ_imp from (28) into (24) yields a_r depending only on (t,q_r,w_r,g_a,η), with no unresolved acceleration. Equation (30) is therefore closed.
Positive-definite M_r and locally Lipschitz terms permit the corresponding local ODE existence/uniqueness results for locally Lipschitz feedback or bounded piecewise-continuous actions. H_H being C^1 at contact joins does not imply bounded higher derivatives of F. ODE closure here does not require global second-order smoothness.

State dimension is 2n_r+5; four actual gains and one energy state cannot be replaced by critic weights. Descriptor dimension is 9+7(p_r+1): 6 material entries, 1 boundary, 2 surface-motion entries and polynomial coefficients for six-dimensional pose plus one force target. Reference degree, T and scales stay fixed across the root family. Σ_ζ is not part of nominal dynamics or another free state; L.1 uses it for parameter-error information.

H.2 Contact-output derivatives and relative control influence
Let a_r be joint acceleration and J_d=∂d/∂q_r. For fixed η,
  δ_dot=J_dw_r-γdot_0，s_dot=J_sw_r，
  v_dot=J_sa_r+(D_qJ_s[w_r])w_r。
Thus
  𝒲_c_dot=H_H,δ(J_dw_r-γdot_0)+H_H,s J_sw_r
           +H_H,v[J_sa_r+(D_qJ_s[w_r])w_r]。
This supplies the explicit right-hand side of main-text (23) within closed robot dynamics.
If an independent force state y_c integrates the same right-hand side, require y_c(0)=H_H(0); exact continuous integration gives d(y_c-H_H)/dt=0. It duplicates the algebraic relation but may drift numerically, so this method uses algebraic outputs.
u_g appears directly in neither F_n nor F_n_dot. Its force effect generally passes through gains and acceleration and can degenerate, for example when relevant pose/velocity errors vanish. An inequality for a first-order force derivative independent of u_g cannot prove normal-force bounds by clipping u_g. Physical constraints remain trajectory-wide, and the internal HJB approximation has a restricted domain.

H.3 Explicit gain/energy action constraints
Main-text (31) has the following fixed order of 17 rows:
  A_u=col(I_4,-I_4,-I_4,I_4,p_g^T)，
  b_u=col(rbar_g,rbar_g,α_g(g_a-g_min),α_g(g_max-g_a),
          χP_D+α_E(E_T-E_T,min))。
For g_min≤g_a≤g_max and E_T≥E_T,min, u_g=0 is feasible. If
  d(g_a-g_min)/dt≥-α_g(g_a-g_min)，
  d(g_max-g_a)/dt≥-α_g(g_max-g_a)，
  d(E_T-E_T,min)/dt≥-α_E(E_T-E_T,min)，
multiplication by the corresponding exponential integrating factors preserves nonnegativity of each component during continuous execution. This directly proves gain/energy bound invariance.
The energy ledger has no artificial recharge or discrete reset. Over a finite prediction horizon, if |P_D|≤Pbar_D, ||p_g||≤pbar and ||u_g||≤rbar, then
  E_T(t)≤E_T(0)+T(χPbar_D+pbar rbar)。
This can define a finite critic energy-computation range; its upper limit is not an additionally claimed invariant physical bound.

H.4 Energy identity
Assume symmetric positive-definite M_r, a dynamics representation satisfying w_r^T(M_r_dot-2C_r)w_r=0, and accurate gravity compensation/torque execution. With v_ξ=J_ξw_r and e_dot=v_ξ-ξd_dot,
  d(1/2 w_r^TM_rw_r)/dt
    =v_ξ^T(f_d-Ke-De_dot)-w_r^TB_rw_r+(J_cw_r)^T𝒲_c^(6)，
  d(1/2 e^TKe)/dt=e^TKe_dot+P_K。
Combining terms using v_ξ=e_dot+ξd_dot gives
  d(S_kin+S_K)/dt=(J_cw_r)^T𝒲_c^(6)
        +v_ξ^Tf_d-ξd_dot^T(Ke+De_dot)-P_D+P_K。
Adding E_T_dot=χP_D-P_K yields main-text (48). Storage is lower-bounded because K≻0, M_r≻0 and E_T≥E_T,min>0. Integration gives
  S(t_1)-S(t_0)≤∫[(J_cw_r)^T𝒲_c^(6)+P_ref]dt。
This inequality concerns robot/controller contact and reference supply ports; it does not require a proven elastic potential for the heterogeneous contact approximation. If ξd_dot=0 and f_d=0, then P_ref=0. Nonzero feedforward can supply power even with stationary reference, so v_ξ^Tf_d cannot be omitted.
Additional actual torque Δτ contributes w_r^TΔτ. If measured dissipation updates energy, compute P_D from the same actual errors and gains. Unmodeled servo errors do not automatically cancel in the nominal identity.

H.5 Reference/surface updates and evidence boundaries
When receding windows rewrite polynomial coefficients, reference position should remain continuous in physical time, usually with continuous velocity too; sliding T changes only the local time origin. If ξ_d jumps while q_r,g_a remain fixed, elastic storage jumps by
  ΔS_K=1/2 e_+^T K e_+-1/2 e_-^T K e_-。
This is reference-port supply and cannot be ignored by switching descriptors. Scanning references are continuous here; arbitrary reference jumps do not generate impedance changes. Updating γ estimates changes predictions, without resetting robot, gains or energy ledger.
Equation (48) proves neither tissue passivity, vanishing tracking errors, contact safety under unknown models nor recursive feasibility. Each requires separate evidence. The supported conclusions are the specified execution-interface energy identity and action-constraint invariance.

I. Finite-horizon HJB, single critic and root interpretation

I.1 Feasibility and HJB domain
For each η, allowed trajectories satisfy main-text (30)–(32) over the entire [t,T]. States with no such trajectory lie outside the finite-value feasible domain. For differentiable interior value functions, short-time dynamic programming gives
  V(t,x)=inf_u{ℓ(t,x,u)dt+V(t+dt,x+f dt)}+o(dt)。
Taylor expansion and dt→0 yield V_t+min_u(ℓ+V_x^Tf)=0 with V(T,x)=Φ(x), main-text (35).
Active physical state boundaries need feasible-direction and state-constraint boundary interpretations; the interior PDE is not itself a boundary condition. The local root applies in a common trajectory neighborhood with strictly positive contact/force/pose/torque margins, while action, gain-rate and energy constraints may activate. Independent trajectory checks establish these margins. Without this local condition, converged interior collocation equations do not solve the full state-constrained problem.
This does not replace global optimality proof with checking. The finite root is called only a local HJB approximation; I.5 supplies conditional residual results bounding performance loss. No unconstructed stabilizing terminal controller is assumed from a finite-horizon terminal cost.

I.2 Explicit basis, nodes and initialization
For fixed integer p≥2, use total-degree polynomials of normalized y=(t/T,bar x_1,...,bar x_nx):
  φ_α(y)=∏_{j=0}^{nx} y_j^{α_j}, with nonnegative integers α_j and Σα_j≤p,
  n_W=binomial(nx+1+p,p)，ψ_α=(1-t/T)φ_α。
Order the basis by total degree then lexicographically and differentiate analytically. x_c and S_x are fixed physical scales, not shifted with each estimate. Since Φ(x;η) is defined at T, its explicit t derivative is zero; η derivatives must include γ(T;η), F_d(T;η) and ξ_d(T;η).
Generate more than n_W candidate nodes in the specified compact domain, remove t=T, no-contact and uncalibrated states, and select n_W independent nodes with column-pivoted QR of the basis-evaluation matrix. Freeze physical coordinates, basis order and scales; do not reselect nodes after η updates to reduce residuals. Nodes must remain in the allowed computation domain throughout the local parameter neighborhood. Full basis-matrix rank does not imply invertible F_Z; check it separately.
Initially take W^0=0 if desired, initialize each node with p_i=∇Φ, solve the unique action minimizer from (37), and choose consistent nonnegative KKT multipliers. Nonunique multipliers signal insufficient active-constraint independence and must be identified offline; an arbitrary multiplier does not establish regularity. Run (40) from Z^0 and use online continuation only after obtaining a qualified local root and independent error checks. This gives a reproducible start, not global Newton convergence from arbitrary starts.
Polynomial degree, reference degree, node domain, T, tolerances and actual robot model are fixed deployment inputs. They determine all equations; choose values from task accuracy and computation budget, without inventing a completed high-dimensional instance. Four action dimensions do not make the complete critic solution four-dimensional.

I.3 Action recovery and KKT
For given (t,x,W,η), let c=G_g^T∇V_N and solve
  min_u u^TR_gu+c^Tu，A_uu≤b_u。
R_g≻0 and a closed, bounded, nonempty set give a unique action. Interior actions satisfy 2R_gu+c=0; constrained actions satisfy
  2R_gu+c+A_u^Tλ=0，s=b_u-A_uu≥0，λ≥0，λ⊙s=0。
These conditions are sufficient for this convex quadratic problem. A four-dimensional active-set solver can recover actions directly. This algebraically minimizes the Hamiltonian; it introduces neither another contact model nor another control objective.
For active rows 𝒜, local actions/multipliers satisfy
  [2R_g  A_𝒜^T; A_𝒜  0][u;λ_𝒜]=[-c;b_𝒜]。
This matrix is invertible when R_g≻0 and A_𝒜 has full row rank. Actions/multipliers are smooth with fixed active set and smooth data. Repeated active rows or simultaneous zero multipliers/slacks invalidate direct use of this conclusion.

I.4 The finite root is not the KKT system of weight optimization
The first F block contains n_W HJB collocation residuals; the other two contain nodewise action-minimization KKT conditions. W represents the value function; no separate min_W J_W is defined. Thus F=0 is not the complete optimality system of a weight objective. The main-text star in Z^* denotes an accepted local HJB–KKT root and its Hamiltonian-optimal actions.
Strictly convex action subproblems do not prevent multiple nonlinear collocation roots or absence of the desired root. A locally unique branch requires an accepted root and invertible F_Z. A cold solve converging to another root at a different descriptor cannot establish same-solution acceleration against continuation.
Root regularity admits a concrete check. With independent nodewise active rows and strict complementarity, eliminate u_i(W),λ_i(W) locally. Since the action set does not depend on W, A_𝒜 ∂_Wu_i=0, while ∂_{u_i}𝓑_i=-λ_𝒜^T A_𝒜. Hence action variation contributes zero extra Bellman derivative. Rows of the reduced n_W×n_W Jacobian are
  L_i=ψ_t,i^T+(f_0,i+G_i u_i)^T J_ψ,i^T。
Under those nodewise KKT conditions, F_Z is invertible exactly when L is, by Schur elimination. This identifies the matrix to check and explains why full basis-evaluation rank is insufficient: L evaluates closed-loop directional basis derivatives rather than basis values. Use this equivalence at roots on the same smooth active branch; initial solution and domain approximation checks remain necessary.
No direct inheritance of original NNBO pointwise convergence for arbitrary weights is assumed. Instantaneous feature outer products have limited rank; small instantaneous residuals do not identify every weight. This method checks the fixed-system Jacobian and residuals.

I.5 Local near-optimality and two error classes
Fix (t_a,x_a,η,T) and a declared comparison domain D. V_D^* is the cost infimum over controls obeying the same nominal dynamics, action/state constraints and remaining in D on [t_a,T]. Certify candidate π_p and finite-root policy π_* separately as members of this class.
Let U_*=V_N(t,x;W^*(η),η) and define
  r_*=∂tU_*+min_{u∈U(t,x;η)}{ℓ+∇U_*^Tf}。
If the chain rule holds for U_* and |r_*|≤ε_B throughout D, every feasible comparison control satisfies ℓ+dU_*/dt≥-ε_B, while π_* satisfies ℓ+dU_*/dt=r_*. The representation exactly enforces U_*(T,x)=Φ(x). Integrate and take inf to obtain
  0≤J(π_*)-V_D^*≤2(T-t_a)ε_B=:ε_conf。
Using the L trajectory bound ρ_x and cost Lipschitz bounds, let
  ε_pred=L_Φρ_x(T)
      +∫[ (L_ℓx+L_ℓu L_πx)ρ_x(t)+L_ℓu L_πZρ_Z ]dt，
Integrating over [t_a,T] yields |J(π_p)-J(π_*)|≤ε_pred. Therefore
  J(π_p)-V_D^*=[J(π_p)-J(π_*)]+[J(π_*)-V_D^*]，
  0≤J(π_p)-V_D^*≤ε_pred+ε_conf。
The first difference can be negative. This certificate needs feasibility of both policies over the complete remaining horizon and residual coverage of every comparison trajectory.
If W^* is unknown, use known W_b with ||W^*-W_b||≤e_W,b. Bellman expressions are linear in W at a common action. For B_W≥sup_{D,u∈U}||ψ_t+J_ψ f||, |r_*-r_b|≤B_W e_W,b. Independent validation points for r_b, reliable evaluation error ε_r,l, fill radius d_fill and Lipschitz constant L_B permit
  ε_B=max_l(|rtilde_b,l|+ε_r,l)+L_B d_fill+B_W e_W,b。
Verify all domain bounds and coverage radii; collocation residuals alone do not replace them. Account separately for J_exec-J(π_p). True-model optimal value is a different benchmark. See certification proof 6 for derivation and scales.

J. Complete-root derivatives and ANEOC prediction error

J.1 Block Jacobians
At fixed nodes let Ψ_i=J_ψ(t_i,x_i), f_i=f_0,i+G_g,i u_i and s_i=b_i-A_i u_i. Direct differentiation of main-text (38)–(39) gives
  ∂_W 𝓑_i=ψ_t,i^T+f_i^T Ψ_i^T，
  ∂_{u_i}𝓑_i=(2R_gu_i+G_i^Tp_i)^T，∂_{λ_i}𝓑_i=0，
  ∂_W 𝓢_i=G_i^T Ψ_i^T，∂_{u_i}𝓢_i=2R_g，∂_{λ_i}𝓢_i=A_i^T，
  ∂_{u_i}(λ_i⊙s_i)=-diag(λ_i)A_i，
  ∂_{λ_i}(λ_i⊙s_i)=diag(s_i)，∂_W(λ_i⊙s_i)=0。
Nodes couple only through shared W. F_Z is square but lacks the automatically tridiagonal structure of a discretized trajectory KKT system; reference [7]'s O(T) trajectory complexity cannot be transferred directly.

At fixed Z, differentiate in descriptor component η_l and write p_{i,l}=∂_{η_l}∇Φ(x_i;η). Then
  ∂_l𝓑_i=∂_lℓ_0,i+p_{i,l}^Tf_i
             +p_i^T(∂_lf_0,i+∂_lG_i u_i)，
  ∂_l𝓢_i=(∂_lG_i)^Tp_i+G_i^Tp_{i,l}+(∂_lA_i)^Tλ_i，
  ∂_l(λ_i⊙s_i)=diag(λ_i)[∂_lb_i-(∂_lA_i)u_i]。
R_g,ψ, scales and nodes are fixed and have no parameter derivatives. G_i,A_i,b_i depend on η through reference error e, P_D and p_g; these derivatives remain necessary even when actions only integrate gains.
For example, at fixed (t,x), contact derivatives in γ_0 and γdot_0 are
  ∂_{γ_0}H_H=-H_H,δ，∂_{γdot_0}H_H=-t H_H,δ。
C.1 and B.3–B.4 give material/boundary derivatives. Use analytical or consistent automatic differentiation online. Differentiate the implicit half-width relation using a_δ=1/Ψ'(a), not its numerical stopping-iteration count.

J.2 Implicit functions and active-set conditions
In a neighborhood of root (Z_0,η_0), assume:
  (i) F∈C^1，F(Z_0,η_0)=0；
  (ii) F_Z(Z_0,η_0) is invertible;
  (iii) λ_i,s_i are nonnegative and strictly complementary: exactly one entry in each pair is positive.
The implicit-function theorem yields a locally unique C^1 root map Z^*(η). Positive multipliers/slacks retain signs in a sufficiently small neighborhood; complementarity products retain the active set, so roots still represent nodewise convex Hamiltonian minima.
Applying the chain rule to F(Z^*(η),η)=0 gives
  F_Z C_η=-F_η。
A degenerate λ=s=0 pair makes its complementarity derivative row zero, invalidating this smooth-root theory. Independent active rows ensure nodewise KKT regularity; invertibility of the complete F_Z still needs separate verification.
For F∈C^2 with a bounded inverse nearby, Z^*∈C^2. Along unit parameter direction h, let C_h=C_η h and differentiate the implicit equation again:
  F_Z D²Z^*[h,h]
    =-F_ZZ[C_h,C_h]-2F_Zη[C_h,h]-F_ηη[h,h]。
This gives a computable second-root-variation equation. If ||F_Z^-1||≤κ, ||C_η||≤c, and second-partial bounds are L_ZZ,L_Zη,L_ηη, then
  ||D²Z^*||≤κ(L_ZZ c²+2L_Zη c+L_ηη)=M_Z。
This requires genuinely bounded derivatives, not merely F∈C^1.
For numerical anchors construct a local certificate with B_Z={Z:||Z-Zbar||≤r_Z}, P_cert={η:||η-ηbar||≤r_η} and fixed approximate inverse R_c. If throughout the product domain
  ||I-R_cF_Z||≤q_c<1，sup_{η∈P_cert}||R_cF(Zbar,η)||≤β，
  β+q_c r_Z<r_Z，
and active multipliers, inactive slacks and model-smoothness margins are positive, every parameter has a unique compatible root in B_Z, with ||F_Z^{-1}||≤||R_c||/(1-q_c). This follows by contraction of Z-R_cF; complete conditions are in certification proof 1. This certifies roots only.

J.3 Contact smoothness and parameter steps
H_H is smooth within open coverage regions; at r=±a only the stated first-order continuity is established. Second-order F bounds must cover node evaluations (t_i,x_i) and terminal evaluations Φ(x_i;η),∇Φ(x_i;η) at T, preserving the selected smooth branch throughout the parameter domain. Also fix sliding direction and maintain valid mass matrix/orientation charts. Check real-trajectory local Lipschitz conditions separately under L.
Check P_cert, complementarity and contact margins at each parameter update. Outside the original certificate, stop using its smooth prediction bound and recertify or solve under K.5.

J.4 Ideal and inexact prediction
If the parameter segment stays in the neighborhood, the integral Taylor remainder gives
  Z^*(η+Δη)=Z^*(η)+C_ηΔη
          +∫_0^1(1-s)D²Z^*(η+sΔη)[Δη,Δη]ds。
Thus ideal prediction error is ≤M_Z||Δη||²/2. Let
  e_0=||Zhat-Z^*(η)||，ε_C=||Chat-C_η||，
Including arithmetic error ||e_p||≤ε_p, Z_pred=Zhat+ChatΔη+e_p satisfies
  ||Z_pred-Z^*(η+Δη)||≤e_0+ε_C||Δη||+M_Z||Δη||²/2+ε_p。
This is the inexact budget in main-text (43).
If Chat solves Jhat Chat=-Fηhat+R_C at numerical Zhat, then
  ||Chat-C_η||≤||Jhat^-1||[||R_C||
        +||Fηhat-Fη*||+||Jhat-J*|| ||C_η||]。
Old-root error, derivative error and linear-solve residual all contribute. K.3 supplies a certified residual bound for e_0; certification proof 2 expands derivative/arithmetic errors. All norms use fixed normalization.

K. On-demand correction, root error and anchor management

K.1 Local exact-Newton bound
At fixed new descriptor, let target root be Z_* and J=F_Z. In a convex neighborhood containing iterate, root and their segment, assume ||J(Z)^-1||≤κ and ||J(Z)-J(Y)||≤L_J||Z-Y||. Full Newton gives
  Z_+-Z_*=J(Z)^-1∫_0^1[J(Z)-J(Z_*+s(Z-Z_*))](Z-Z_*)ds，
Hence
  e_+≤κL_J e²/2，e=||Z-Z_*||。
Within the J.2 certificate, initial error ≤E_0, ||Z^0-Zbar||+2E_0<r_Z and κL_J E_0/2<1 ensure iterates stay inside and converge. Invoke main-text (44) when candidate root accuracy is insufficient, then still check release conditions (45).
Far-field damping reduces merit, without guaranteeing the correct root from arbitrary starts. Under the local conditions and Armijo backtracking allowing full steps, α=1 is eventually accepted; quadratic error bounds apply only to such full corrections.

K.2 Inexact linear solution and backtracking
For m(Z)=1/2||F(Z)||², exact direction Jd=-F satisfies
  ∇m(Z)^T d=F^TJd=-||F||²。
Start at α=1 and halve until
  m(Z+αd)≤m(Z)-c_A α||F(Z)||²，0<c_A<1/2，
and model evaluation remains valid. Check nonnegativity and complementarity at final acceptance; decreasing m alone cannot authorize action release.
For Jd=-F+r_lin, the full-step bound becomes
  e_+≤κL_J e²/2+κ||r_lin||。
Quadratic convergence requires r_lin to decrease with e². Fixed solve tolerances leave an error floor; contact-root and floating-point errors also limit attainable residuals.

K.3 Certified residual-to-root error
Within the common J.2 certificate, Z∈B_Z and η∈P_cert give the fixed-point bound
  ||Z-Z^*(η)||≤||R_cF(Z,η)||/(1-q_c)。
For function-evaluation error ≤ε_F,eval, use a reliable upper bound on (||R_cFtilde||+||R_c||ε_F,eval)/(1-q_c). Take the smaller of this and J.4's prediction bound if useful; no prior correction to a fixed small residual is required. A new anchor needs recertified root enclosure, branch margins and sensitivity.

K.4 First-order comparison with ordinary warm starts
Since F(Z_k,η_k)=0, at the fixed old root
  F(Z_k,η_k+Δη)=F_η Δη+O(||Δη||²)，
  F_Z(Z_k,η_k+Δη)=F_Z(Z_k,η_k)+O(||Δη||)。
One ordinary warm-start Newton step at the new parameter gives
  Z_ws,1=Z_k-F_Z(Z_k,η_k+Δη)^-1F(Z_k,η_k+Δη)
        =Z_k+C_ηΔη+O(||Δη||²)。
Thus both have the same first-order direction; cached-J chord can also have second-order root error. A prepared ANEOC query is a matrix–vector product, but benefits depend on baseline residual/solve costs and both certification costs. With extra preparation P_i and per-query service S_i,l including correction/certification, advantage over Q queries requires Σ_l(S_B,l-S_A,l)>P_A-P_B. Latency includes unfinished preparation and resource waits. See proof 8 for full costs and equivalence cases.

K.5 Multistep prediction and release/recovery conditions
Stepwise prediction obeys E_{k+1}≤E_k+ε_C,k||Δη_k||+M_Z||Δη_k||²/2+ε_p,k. With frozen anchor sensitivity, ε_C,k≤ε_C,a+M_Z||η_k-η_a||. The main text predicts directly from anchors, using (43) for each query.
Publish when (45) and complete action/trajectory/performance conditions hold; correct if root accuracy is insufficient and K.1 convergence/solve-accuracy conditions hold; re-anchor if the old anchor no longer suits future queries and the new record passes J.2; re-solve and recertify if configuration/branch coverage fails. Release and re-anchoring are separate, and re-anchored error retains its certified nonzero bound.
Corrector convergence does not remove finite-root domain approximation error or trajectory infeasibility. Proof 7 details triggers, inexact convergence and recovery limits. Continue an existing policy only while its certificate covers current operation; otherwise rely on verified entry conditions of an independent existing supervisor.

L. Parameter error, policy error and actual execution

L.1 From MHE to descriptor, root and policy
For fixed reference coefficients, a linear selector D_η maps ζ to random descriptor components:
  Σ_η≈D_ηΣ_ζD_η^T。
Uncertain externally supplied references require their own covariance/cross-terms. Equation (26) uses the two sides' parameters, not independent copies θ_f and Δθ, retaining MHE correlation.
On a regular branch,
  Σ_Z≈C_ηΣ_ηC_η^T，Σ_W≈Π_WΣ_ZΠ_W^T。
These are local first-order propagation of random estimation errors, not guaranteed true-root enclosures. Numerical root error and systematic contact bias are separate.
For π_N(t,x;Π_W Z^*(η),η), the total parameter derivative is
  D_ηπ_N=∂_ηπ_N+∂_Wπ_N Π_WC_η。
The first term directly captures model, terminal-cost, action-channel and constraint dependence. Propagating only W is generally wrong. Differentiate I.3 action KKT equations on fixed active sets to obtain both terms. Parameter changes alter the OCP; current robot-state changes evaluate the same feedback law at a different state.

L.2 From root to action error
At common (t,x,η), the allowed action set is the same. Let two critics have linear terms c_1,c_2 and minimizing actions u_1,u_2. Adding their convex variational inequalities gives
  2λ_min(R_g)||u_1-u_2||²≤||c_1-c_2|| ||u_1-u_2||。
Therefore
  ||u_1-u_2||≤||G_g|| ||J_ψ|| ||W_1-W_2||/[2λ_min(R_g)]。
Including physical weight-extraction scale E_W=Π_WD_Z, take L_πZ≥sup||G_g^T J_ψ^TE_W||/[2λ_min(R_g)], giving ||π_p-π_*||≤L_πZρ_Z, without requiring identical active sets. A feasible numerical action with primal–dual gap ≤δ_Q has action error ≤sqrt(δ_Q/λ_min(R_g)). Cross-parameter comparisons also need L.1 direct effects; see proof 3.
True-optimal policy error additionally includes value-gradient approximation error. F=0 removes node-equation error, not basis truncation. I.5 supplies a separate conditional cumulative-cost bound without inventing a known gradient-error bound.

L.3 Candidate trajectory tubes, continuous feasibility and impedance error
From release state/phase, generate a continuous piecewise-C¹ candidate curve y(t) and certify defect ||y_dot-f_p(t,y)||≤r_num(t). Let the two closed-loop fields have state Lipschitz constants L_p,L_* and ||G_g||≤Gbar. Define
  E_L[a,b](t)=exp(L(t-t_a))a+∫_{t_a}^t exp(L(t-s))b(s)ds，
  β_p=E_{L_p}[a_p,r_num]，
  β_*=E_{L_*}[a_*,r_num+Gbar L_πZρ_Z]。
If these bounds are strictly below the candidate-centered certified tube radius and closed-loop dynamics are well-posed inside, a first-exit argument gives full-interval existence/uniqueness and ||x_p-y||≤β_p, ||x_*-y||≤β_*. The two same-initial-state trajectories satisfy
  ||x_p-x_*||≤ρ_x=E_{L_*}[0,Gbar L_πZρ_Z]。
Actual gains form a state subvector, so ||g_p-g_*||≤ρ_x; tighter rate-integration bounds are possible. Compute stiffness/damping matrix errors from diagonal entries; see proofs 3–4.
For candidate feasibility, split original (32) into h_j≥0. Let htilde_jl be center-curve grid values, evaluation errors ≤ε_eval,jl, center-curve time-variation bounds M_jl, state Lipschitz bounds L_h,jl and βbar_p,l≥sup_{[t_l,t_{l+1}]}β_p. Then
  Lower bound h_lower,jl=htilde_jl-ε_eval,jl-M_jl(t_{l+1}-t_l)-L_h,jl βbar_p,l.
If every h_lower,jl≥m_j, the candidate satisfies h_j≥m_j throughout. This is the nominal candidate bound in (45); do not add candidate/reference error ρ_x again. Check reference feasibility with β_* when needed. Proof 5 covers actions, energy boundaries and sampling intervals.

L.4 Model, sampling and execution error
Suppose the ideal candidate field has state Lipschitz constant L_f in the compared neighborhood and actual additive field error ≤dbar, including parameter/contact error, torque tracking, sample hold and action solution. Let τ=t-t_a. For initial error e_x,0 at t_a, Gronwall gives
  ||x_actual(t)-x_nom(t)||≤exp(L_f τ)e_x,0
                 +(exp(L_f τ)-1)dbar/L_f。
For L_f=0 the second term is τ dbar. If h_j directly depends on true parameters and ||Δη||≤ε_η, nominal margins must additionally cover
  L_h,j ε_x(t)+L_hη,j ε_η
to imply nonnegative actual constraints. Calibration/error evidence must supply dbar and ε_η; MHE covariance does not automatically provide deterministic bounds.
With zero-order hold, integrate g_a at the actual period and compute E_T from actual motion/gain changes over that period. A barrier condition at one instant does not guarantee the whole period. Verify the period using L.3-type derivative margins and reduce h_c to meet error budgets. Adding discrete sampling constraints to the Hamiltonian requires rebuilding the root family; post-hoc clipping is not the same optimal action.
For execution, replace β_p in L.3 by an enclosure β_e including those disturbances, and subtract direct parameter/output errors from constraint margins. Release certificates must cover current state, parameters and interval; expiry or failure invokes K.5.

M.4 Reproducible root-check code
The following ran with Python 3.10, NumPy 1.26.4 and SciPy 1.13.1; the root check itself needs only NumPy. Keeping code here avoids an algorithm version disconnected from the main text.

    import numpy as np
    U = 0.7
    def exact(eta):
        W = (-1 + np.sqrt(1 + 4*eta**2))/2
        if W < U:
            return np.array([W, -W, 0.0])
        W = (eta**2 + U**2)/(1 + 2*U)
        return np.array([W, -U, 2*(W-U)])
    def F(z, eta):
        W, u, lam = z
        return np.array([-W+eta**2+u*u+2*W*u,
                         2*u+2*W-lam, lam*(u+U)])
    def J(z):
        W, u, lam = z
        return np.array([[-1+2*u, 2*u+2*W, 0],
                         [2, 2, -1], [0, lam, u+U]])
    def correct(z, eta):
        for k in range(20):
            if np.linalg.norm(F(z, eta), np.inf) < 1e-13:
                return z, k
            z = z + np.linalg.solve(J(z), -F(z, eta))
        raise RuntimeError('Newton did not converge')
    for eta in [0.8, 1.4]:
        z0 = exact(eta)
        C = np.linalg.solve(J(z0), np.array([-2*eta, 0, 0]))
        Cfd = (exact(eta+1e-6)-exact(eta-1e-6))/(2e-6)
        print('derivative', np.linalg.norm(C-Cfd)/np.linalg.norm(C))
        for h in [.02, .01, .005]:
            pred = z0 + C*h
            one = pred + np.linalg.solve(J(pred), -F(pred, eta+h))
            sol, kp = correct(pred.copy(), eta+h)
            warm, kw = correct(z0.copy(), eta+h)
            assert sol[1]+U >= -1e-12 and sol[2] >= -1e-12
            assert np.linalg.norm(sol-exact(eta+h)) < 1e-10
            print(h, np.linalg.norm(pred-exact(eta+h)),
                  np.linalg.norm(one-exact(eta+h)), kp, kw)
