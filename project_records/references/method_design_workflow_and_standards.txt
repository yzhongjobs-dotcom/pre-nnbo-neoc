Scope (revised 2026-10-16):
This file is a professional reference for the failure-analysis/mechanism-transfer route, not a universal workflow for scientific method formation. Stable goals and execution principles are governed by project_records/long_term_requirements.md. Use the specific RCA, RMC and intervention-mapping requirements below only when choosing this route; other formation paths need not invent a failure, root cause or transfer story first.

Core of this route:
Find a problem in actual failure and analyze its root cause; solve it by transferring mechanisms from leading work in other fields. Two key questions are whether the cause can actually be identified (as in the earlier fixed-impedance example), and, more critically, whether a genuinely transferable mechanism can be found. Find a problem–intervention pair, check correspondence at the mechanism level, and transfer/adapt naturally.

---------------------
# 01 | ARIS Method Design: workflow and standards

## 1. Core understanding for this route

**Find problems and analyze causes through failures, then address them through method/mechanism transfer.**

Two capabilities matter:

1. Can the root cause actually be identified?
2. More critically, can transferable mechanisms in other fields be actively found?

The three key transfer steps are:

1. Find an actual **Problem–Intervention Pair**.
2. Examine **Intervention-Level Isomorphism**.
3. Transfer/adapt naturally under **Target Constraints**.

## 2. Purpose

Improve method design so that a harness can form methods with potential for a core contribution at a leading venue.

This is the general background of this reference, not a change of R15 into harness development or evidence that completing a workflow establishes journal-level quality.

Main route:

`Real Failure`
→ `Mechanism-Level Root Cause`
→ `Required Mechanism Change`
→ `Actively Search for a Strong Source Mechanism / Solution Principle`
→ `Test Transferability`
→ `Target-Constraint-driven Necessary Adaptation`
→ `Minimal Strong Method Seed`
→ `Top-Venue Strength / Causal Validation`

Principle:

**The question determines method choices. Understand mechanisms rather than component names; examine question value, grounds for the effect and plausible alternatives; avoid ungrounded modules.** This route seeks natural, testable failure repair. It does not require every contribution to be a minimal repair, uniquely indispensable algorithms, or exhaustive falsification before low-cost exploration.

## 3. Method genesis

Methods using this route should progressively explain the following. Exploration may begin with candidate explanations and revise them through evidence:

`Failure`
→ `Root Cause`
→ `Required Mechanism Change`
→ `Why This Source Mechanism Was Considered`
→ `Why It Can Transfer`
→ `Why Target Constraints Require This Adaptation`
→ `Final Method`

When transferring a mechanism, answer:

1. Why consider this source mechanism?
2. Why can it logically transfer?
3. What capability or understanding advances, and what evidence supports a strong contribution?

## 4. Failure → RCA → RMC

Describe failure as:

`condition → observable failure → consequence`

It must be real, important and within the target operating envelope.

Check reasonable simple repairs and strong comparators, including tuning, standard estimators/filters/observers, existing constraints/certificates, added capacity and local reparameterization. If they adequately solve the claimed difficulty under target conditions, correct the contribution judgment, update the field picture and probe important remaining questions. Do not automatically narrow the topic or require exclusive solution capability. Comparator checks and exploration may iterate around key uncertainties; exhaustive repair elimination is not a prerequisite.

RCA must go beyond “poor generalization,” “insufficient robustness,” “low accuracy” or “not adaptive enough.” Identify a main causal/computational mechanism sufficient to explain key failure and amenable to intervention, for example:

`state / relation / information path / representation / constraint / observability / computation / optimization / decision structure`

RCA must be:

- mechanism-level;
- falsifiable;
- evidence-calibrated;
- intervention-relevant;
- explicit about competing explanations/falsifiers;
- developed without inventing a cause backward from a preferred method.

Propose RMC from the currently supported RCA. During exploration, state hypotheses and pending tests; “accepted” does not require complete proof before exploration. Progressively specify:

`Current Causal/Computational Relation, State or Information Structure`
→ `Required Change`
→ `Change Direction`
→ `Causal/Intervention Position`
→ `Activation Condition`

Then form:

`Root Cause → Required Mechanism Change → Required Capability / Design Obligations`

Core principle:

**The research question determines required capabilities; within this route, causal analysis guides intervention design; algorithms implement it.** Other method-formation routes need not pass RCA first.

## 5. Source mechanisms and transfer

In this route, RMC guides source search. Other routes may seek mature tools through representation, theory, measurement or required capabilities. The key question here is:

> **Which existing mechanism has successfully changed the causal/computational variable or relation that needs to change here?**

For a strong source, recover:

`Source Problem`
→ `Source Intervention`
→ `Changed Variable / Relation / Structure`
→ `Mechanism / Causal Effect`
→ `Outcome`
→ `Assumptions / Boundary`

Transfer **the mechanism by which a method solves its problem, not its surface form**.

The essential correspondence is:

`Target Required Mechanism Change ↔ Source Intervention`

Check whether the relation actually changed by the source intervention realizes the target intervention. Examine direction, causal/intervention position, activation conditions and actual effects. A mapping may begin as a candidate and be tested theoretically/experimentally. Surface similarity does not establish it; prior proof of a uniquely necessary intervention is not required.

**A fully shared source and target root cause is not mandatory; intervention structure is the required correspondence.**

“Isomorphism” here means checking mechanism correspondence, not requiring strict mathematical isomorphism for every transfer. Assess substantive prior coverage through target question, conditions and capability; field labels, component overlap or tool maturity cannot decide it.

## 6. Target adaptation and natural derivation

Examine whether adaptation is needed for the target task; direct reuse is allowed. Where needed:

`Source Mechanism / Solution Principle`

- `Target Constraints`
  → `Assumption–Constraint Collision`
  → `Necessary Adaptation`
  → `Final Method`

Real target constraints drive adaptation. **Adaptation size is not a novelty criterion.** Without a real constraint, assumption mismatch or residual requirement, do not add modules to look innovative.

Removing the source-field backstory should leave valid target-task logic:

`Target Failure`
→ `Root Cause`
→ `Required Mechanism Change`
→ `Target Constraints`
→ `Final Method`

The final method should be a natural, testable repair, avoiding method soup. “Minimal” discourages unjustified modules; it does not require all contributions to be minimal repairs. The complete route must still provide the question's required capabilities.

## 7. Strong methods for leading venues

`Valid Method Seed ≠ Top-Venue Strong Method Seed`

Consider these dimensions relative to the claims. They are not universal compulsory items, and self-scores do not replace field judgment:

- Problem Value
- Necessity
- Target Intervention Novelty
- Mechanism Delta
- Scientific / Conceptual Delta
- Closest-Prior Separation
- Causal Coherence
- Minimality
- Natural Derivation
- Claim-Proportional Feasibility
- Discriminative Validation

Novelty concerns actual scientific/technical progress, not algorithm names. Causal intervention/mechanism change matters for this route; other contributions may advance representation, formalization, measurement or capability combinations.

An existing source primitive, mature source mechanism or small adaptation does not alone negate innovation.

Algorithm names, module counts and complexity cannot replace scientific advance. Judge performance gains through question value, existing capabilities, costs and evidence: neither reject them categorically nor infer a strong contribution solely from better numbers.

A weighted score cannot offset a critical scientific failure.

Examine whether target needs, selected mechanisms and actual method effects agree. The following is a relationship schematic, not a mandatory mathematical identity:

`Target Required Structural Change`
`= Selected Principle / Source Intervention`
`= Final Method Real Computational Change`

## 8. Evidence, tests and recovery

Match verification to claims. Mechanism explanations should discriminate candidate/rival principles or primary/rival RCA. Theory requires assumption/derivation checks; performance claims require fair comparisons and actual effects; other claims need appropriate evidence. Do not force causal attribution tests onto every contribution.

Evidence may change the principle, assumptions, applicability boundary, target operationalization, source–target mapping or RCA.

Return failures to the level that actually failed: implementation, test, mapping, source, principle, RMC/RCA, novelty/delta or feasibility. Revise the question when necessary. Local evidence supports its own level; also examine whether accumulated evidence supports higher-level change. Locality cannot protect a design forever.

Full causal validation for mechanism claims:

`Predicted Mechanism Change`
→ `Observed Mechanism Change`
→ `Discriminating Evidence`
→ `Performance Consequence`

Performance improvement alone does not validate a causal mechanism. Performance claims are judged through comparator choice, fairness, effect and scope; missing attribution tests do not categorically negate them.

## 9. How scientific standards develop

Do not freeze standards solely from theoretical assertions about what ought to hold.

Use actual leading papers to examine **Method Genesis** and **Method Genealogy**, combining:

`Leading Successful Cases`

- `Near-Failure Cases on the Same Route`
- `Necessary Counterfactuals`
  → `Cross-Case Causal Induction`

Distinguish:

- Necessary Conditions
- Non-Necessary Conditions
- Insufficient Conditions
- Interaction Conditions

Different papers may follow different formation logics. Do not invent root causes, source methods or transfer stories for all papers.

Turn lessons tested against real cases into principles that are:

**Executable by agents, assessable by reviewers, checkable by validators and correctable when problems appear.** Match check depth to stage and claim. Do not turn every suggestion into a fixed gate, conceal substantive defects with weighted scores or demand complete final evidence during exploration.
