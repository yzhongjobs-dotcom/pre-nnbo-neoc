Empirical reference distribution for methods at leading venues
Reference Corpus → Pairwise Comparison → Venue Profile
Scope: continuous physical/soft contact, force regulation, impedance control, online/predictive impedance selection, contact uncertainty, medical contact and robotic ultrasound
Version: v1.1 Audited (2026-09-07)

======================================================================
0. This teaches Codex what real leading-journal methods look like, rather than imposing rules
======================================================================

This is neither an abstract checklist nor a mechanical acceptance gate.
It translates researchers' empirical understanding of leading-venue methods into:

Reference Corpus (real reference examples)
→ Unified Paper Portrait
→ Pairwise Comparison
→ Venue Profile / Reference Distribution
→ Current-Method Distance from actual leading-venue work

The substantive questions are not:
Does this method contain a certain module?
How many new algorithms are used?
Are there enough equations?

Instead ask:
If anonymized among real neighboring T-RO / IJRR / ICRA / RSS papers,
would its problem scale, scientific increment, contribution framing, complexity, theoretical depth, execution and evidence
belong to the same professional distribution? If not, specifically where does it differ?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
0.1 Empirical labels
---------------------------------------------------------------------

[S] Stable Pattern
    Repeated across venues and different sample forms; a high-confidence empirical pattern.

[V] Venue Tendency
    Relatively stable for a venue in current samples, but neither necessary nor sufficient.

[E] Example-Specific
    Demonstrates acceptance of one published design, not a universal standard.

[R] Red Line
    Practices systematically distorting novelty, complexity or venue judgments.

Important:
Common in samples does not mean mandatory.
Absent from samples does not mean prohibited.
One complex leading-journal paper does not make complexity mandatory.
One simple leading-journal paper does not make simplicity sufficient.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
0.2 What this reference can and cannot judge (added after reverse audit)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The intended judgment is:
Whether the method aligns with actual neighboring leading-venue distributions, and how it differs from representative target-venue papers.

It is not an acceptance-probability predictor; publication venue is not absolute ground truth.
Venue quality distributions overlap; submission strategies, length, review history and timing affect placement.

Conclusions must therefore report:
- Method-level alignment;
- Nearest published exemplars;
- Dimensions below/above the reference distribution;
- Target-venue readiness;
rather than asserting guaranteed acceptance.

[S] Standards mean empirical alignment, not deterministic venue classification.
[R] A similar RAS/TIM/RA-L publication alone cannot prove inadequacy for T-RO, nor the reverse.

2. Read every paper with the same eight-dimensional portrait
======================================================================

D1 Problem Level
- What actual failure/bottleneck is addressed?
- Is it foundational theory, general control, a system issue or a narrow application?
- What concrete capability is missing without a solution?

D2 Scientific Delta
- What has the strongest prior achieved?
- What actual additional step is taken?
- Does novelty concern models, variables, feedback roles, algorithm structure, theory, real-time or system capability?

D3 Contribution Packaging
- What scientific object frames the actual increment?
- A new relation, generalization, unified framework, capability leap or system interconnection?
- Which mature tools are not claimed as separate innovations?

D4 Method Complexity
- How many principal and supporting mechanism layers exist?
- What do modeling, optimization/learning, stability/safety and real-time execution each do?

D5 Necessity / Structural Naturalness
- Does each major module address an actual failure?
- Is there an obviously simpler substitute?
- Do authors choose simple solutions when sufficient?

D6 Theory / Mathematical Closure
- What stability, passivity, convergence, physical consistency, constraints or robustness is established?
- Does theoretical depth match the claim?

D7 Engineering Closure
- Do optimizer/planner outputs actually enter control?
- Is execution online/real-time?
- Are interfaces, sensing, model errors and contact failures addressed?

D8 Evidence Closure
- Do baselines directly test paper-level claims?
- Are there ablations, disturbances, dynamic environments, hardware or user studies?
- Does evidence strength match claims and complexity?

[S] Judge the complete eight-dimensional portrait; no single dimension substitutes for it.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Dual contribution reconstruction (added after reverse audit)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

An author's introduction/contribution list is not automatically the scientific delta.
Record simultaneously:

A. Author-Stated Contribution
B. Auditor-Reconstructed Delta against strongest prior
C. Packaging Uplift from B to mechanisms/relations/capabilities/frameworks
D. Packaging Validity supported by equations and experiments

Strong A with small B indicates narrative-inflation risk;
reasonable B with module-list A indicates under-packaging;
consistent A and B indicate framing aligned with substance.

[R] Authors' novel/first/contribution wording does not independently prove novelty.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 Complexity vector (added after reverse audit)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Low/medium/high is only a summary; formal pairwise comparisons separate:

C = [
  C_model      : contact/environment/robot model layers and coupling,
  C_decision   : online variables and decision levels,
  C_solver     : optimizer/decomposition/inner–outer levels,
  C_guarantee  : robustness/passivity/safety/certification layers,
  C_execution  : high-level / low-level / estimator / asynchronous execution loops,
  C_evidence   : ablation/hardware/user-evidence burden needed to justify them
]

Use no fixed numerical thresholds; compare relative complexity for matched problem/delta/claims.

[S] Overdesign means a complexity axis far above comparable scientific deltas without corresponding capability/guarantee/evidence, rather than merely many modules.

3. Actual T-RO paper portraits
======================================================================

3.1 Wijayarathne et al., T-RO 2023

Problem：
Existing TO discovers contact and exploits forces, but accurate simultaneous motion/force tracking in soft environments remains limited;
full robot/deformable-contact constrained TO is difficult online.

Scientific Delta：
The core is none of Hertz, DDP, ADMM, IK or MPC individually, but:

deformable contact physics
→ constrained trajectory optimization
→ desired motion and force jointly planned
→ distributed solution enables real-time MPC

Relative to the authors' conference version, the journal extension preserves much of the core:
- 2-block → 3-block, separating IK;
- Online planner MPC;
- Stronger static/dynamic disturbance tests;
- Added low-level admittance control.

Packaging：
Soft-contact dynamics plus constrained force–motion TO and real-time MPC validation, not invention of DDP/ADMM/IK.

Complexity: medium to high.
contact model + robot/contact dynamics + DDP/IK/projection + ADMM + MPC + low-level force controller。

Necessity：
Dynamics, kinematics, constraints and online solution justify decomposition. Low-level admittance supports execution rather than a second scientific delta.

Theory：
Emphasis: contact dynamics and constrained-optimization closure, not an extensive new stability theory.

Engineering：
Real-time MPC, high-level planning, low-level control, static/dynamic disturbances.

Evidence：
Model validation, multiple Cartesian motions, force–motion tracking and disturbances.

Lessons:
[S] Many mature tools can coexist in a leading-journal paper when serving one new paper-level relation.
[S] Problem scale must justify solver complexity.
[E] DDP–IK–Projection plus ADMM is this implementation, not a T-RO requirement.

3.2 Pollayil et al., T-RO 2023

Problem：
Despite extensive impedance-control work, general real-time online stiffness/damping selection without extra F/T sensors is lacking.

Scientific Delta：
For given Cartesian inertia, plan stiffness/damping online to limit perturbation effects;
use analytical solutions for diagonally dominant inertia and numerical optimization for general nondiagonal cases, forming a more general planning framework.

Packaging：
Frame online impedance planning as a control-design problem, rather than merely optimization plus impedance control.

Complexity: low to medium.
One optimization core, analytical/numerical branches, stability/passivity and an online loop.

Necessity：
Use analytical solutions where possible; numerical solution only for general cases.
This demonstrates that unnecessary complexity is not rewarded.

Theory：
optimization formulation、performance bounds、stability/passivity。

Engineering：
No F/T sensor; quadruped and arm; real-time operation.

Evidence：
Multiple robots test generality and real-time applicability.

Lessons:
[S] Meaningful generalization with complete mathematics/hardware can support T-RO publication.
[S] Minimum sufficient complexity matters more than algorithm layers.

3.3 Li et al., T-RO 2025

Problem：
Classical adaptation is limited under nonlinear/dynamic environments and estimation error;
human-like impedance learning is laborious; RL/DRL faces safety, interpretability, convergence and overfitting issues.

Scientific Delta：
DRL feedforward learns impedance tuning
+ variable-impedance feedback closes force tracking
+ couch contact model bounds safe sim-to-real transfer
+ stability/convergence analysis + state-aware regression address RL weaknesses。

Packaging：
The paper-level capability is interpretable, safely transferable learned variable-impedance force tracking with theoretical closure under uncertain nonlinear environments.

Complexity: medium.
learning + VIC feedback + simplified contact model + stability/convergence analysis。

Necessity：
Each major component maps to a failure stated by the authors.

Theory：
Lyapunov/contraction arguments directly support stability/convergence claims.

Engineering：
Sim-to-real, real robots and static/quasistatic/dynamic environments.

Evidence：
Constant impedance, classical adaptation and proposed method; regression-vector comparisons support overfitting claims.

Lessons:
[S] Multiple components are not necessarily overdesign when closing required failures of one paper-level capability.
[S] More mechanisms require corresponding module-level evidence/ablations.

3.4 Fu et al., T-RO 2025

Problem：
Teleoperated medical-contact tasks jointly face force tracking/compliance, workload and whole-system passivity.

Scientific Delta：
QP,VIC,shared control and energy tanks have precedents; the delta connects:
active compliant force regulation
+ human–robot shared control
+ global passivity
into a complete medical-contact shared-control framework.

Packaging：
Authors acknowledge prior passivity/energy-tank/shared-control work; innovation is system interconnection and integrated capability.

Complexity: high.
leader/follower + bilateral teleoperation + Cartesian impedance + online QP stiffness + force compensation + global tank + shared control。

Necessity：
Force regulation, operator assistance and whole-system passivity make the claim itself broad enough to justify complexity.

Theory：
Passivity/energy arguments serve whole-system claims; QP serves stiffness selection/force performance.

Engineering：
ROS, haptics, robot and real-time QP/passivity. Communication delay is not fully addressed; retain this boundary.

Evidence：
Autonomous force tracking, shared-control validation and a 12-user study; evidence addresses force error, passivity and workload.

Lessons:
[S] Broader system claims can justify broader complexity/evidence.
[S] System contributions require system-level evidence.
[E] A 12-user study supports this paper's human-factors claim; it is not generally mandatory for T-RO.

4. Actual IJRR paper portraits
======================================================================

4.1 Haddadin & Shahriari — Unified Force-Impedance Control, IJRR 2024

Problem：
Robots need compliance and exact force regulation across rigid/flexible joints, measured/estimated forces and contact/noncontact transitions.

Scientific Delta：
This is not the first force/impedance combination. IJRR expands the UFIC lineage into a broader unified framework covering more robot/system conditions and arbitrary passive environments.

Packaging：
The unified framework is itself the scientific object; strength comes from generality, theoretical unity and practical coverage.

Complexity: high but unified; force regulation, impedance, tanks and transitions share one passivity framework.

Necessity：
Crucially, authors note contact loss could be treated entirely in the energy/tank domain, but avoid impractical complexity through direct state-based shaping.

Theory: deep; passivity/stability are core contributions.
Engineering: rigid/flexible robots and sensed/estimated forces.
Evidence: multiple robot settings support the broad framework.

Lessons:
[S] Complex IJRR frameworks generally need matching generality/theoretical unity.
[S] Unified-theory papers still avoid impractical over-unification.

4.2 Lachner, Nah, Hogan — Physically Consistent Stiffness, IJRR 2025/2026

Problem：
Traditional task-to-joint stiffness mappings neglect basis changes in curved configuration space, producing asymmetry and damaging consistency/passivity/energy properties.

Scientific Delta：
change of basis on curved space
→ conventional derivative misses connection term
→ incorporate Christoffel symbols
→ recover physically consistent symmetric stiffness。

Packaging：
The contribution repairs persistent physical inconsistency, rather than merely adding a correction term.

Complexity: low; one foundational correction dominates.

Necessity：
No MPC/learning/robust stack is needed for this mathematical/physical-consistency problem; extra modules would dilute it.

Theory: deep symmetry, energy and passivity implications.
Engineering: real-robot validation.
Evidence: physical consequences of correction, rather than large benchmarks.

Lessons:
[S] Leading-journal depth is not monotonic in method complexity.
[S] Foundational corrections can support IJRR with minimal methods.

4.3 Piedra, Jeffrey, Khatib — Explicit Force-Based Control, IJRR 2026

Problem：
Robotic ultrasound often uses implicit force/admittance control through motion loops, causing bandwidth/phase-lag limitations.

Scientific Delta：
Explicit force control already exists; the actual delta is:
identify motion-loop bottleneck in robotic ultrasound
→ directly regulate force/moment at end effector
→ integrate probe orientation / haptic framework
→ obtain much more responsive dynamic-contact behavior。

Packaging：
Explicit force-based architecture is the appropriate role for compliant robotic ultrasound, not invention of force control.

Complexity: medium.
explicit force/moment + 6-DoF haptic + force-based probe orientation + acoustic-coupling alignment。

Necessity：
If an implicit motion loop is the root cause, changing architecture may suffice without a default large robust-MPC stack.

Theory emphasizes force-control formulation, integration and physical response.
Engineering: dynamic phantoms, external disturbances, heterogeneity and actual ultrasound imaging.
Evidence：
Against state-of-the-art integral adaptive admittance: phase lag 121° → 5.76°, mean force-error reductions about 77.7% / 72.6%, plus image-quality evidence.

Lessons:
[S] A direct mechanism targeting a bottleneck with a substantial capability/performance leap can support IJRR.
[S] More elaborate prediction is not automatically more advanced.
[E] These improvement values are case-specific, not IJRR thresholds.

4.4 Xue et al. — Implicit Motor Adaptation, IJRR 2026

Problem：
Uncertain parameters impede contact-manipulation generalization; randomization can be conservative and explicit adaptation may need accurate identification/retraining.

Scientific Delta：
rough parameter distribution
→ tensor-train implicit policy representation
→ efficient parameter-conditioned policy retrieval
→ avoid precise identification/retraining while retaining adaptation。

Packaging：
Implicit motor adaptation is one clearly named mechanism directly comparable to prior work.

Complexity: medium; tensor-train representation, policy retrieval and theory, rather than independent controller stacking.

Necessity：
Efficient parameter-conditioned retrieval must justify tensor trains.

Theory: theoretical analysis included.
Engineering: multiple contact-rich primitives in simulation and reality.
Evidence: multiple physical-parameter instances test robustness/generalization.

Lessons:
[S] IJRR also accepts one core algorithmic object with theory and broad contact-rich evidence.

5. Actual leading-conference paper portraits
======================================================================

5.1 Jin et al., ICRA 2022

Problem: fixed impedance adapts poorly to changing precision/compliance/task constraints.
Delta: formulate variable-impedance updates as constrained QP producing executable parameters online.
Packaging: constrained variable impedance via QP is a focused technical increment.
Complexity: low to medium; VIC,QP,constraints.
Theory: necessary formulation/constraints within conference length, not broad unification.
Engineering/evidence: online implementation and experimental comparisons.

[V] ICRA accepts focused technical deltas with closed problem–method–experiment logic.

5.2 Beber et al., ICRA 2024

Problem: safe accurate probe/body interaction under changing tissue mechanics and contact loss.

Delta：
online tissue viscoelastic estimation
→ physical constraints for QP variable impedance
→ energy tank / passivity
→ safe ultrasound force control。

Packaging: tissue mechanics informs safe ultrasound variable impedance, beyond QP plus tanks.

Complexity: medium; tissue model/estimation,QP impedance,physical constraints,tank/passivity.
Necessity: each layer maps to target-task failures.
Engineering：real-time + patient dummy torso + contact loss。
Evidence: safety,force control and contact-loss behavior support claims.

[S] Conferences can contain complete multimodule chains; module count does not distinguish journals.

5.3 Reuss et al., RSS 2022

Problem: rigid dynamics are modelable, but history-dependent/partially observed friction/flexibility are difficult; high gains reduce compliance.

Delta：
physically consistent rigid-body prior
+ recurrent neural residual
→ better inverse dynamics feedforward
→ same tracking with much lower impedance feedback gains。

Packaging: hybrid inverse dynamics supports precision with compliant impedance, not simply physics plus RNN.

Complexity: medium-low; physical prior,RNN residual,feedforward impedance.
Necessity: memory addresses hysteresis/partial observability.
Engineering：7-DoF real robot。
Evidence closes model accuracy/generalization into actual feedback-gain reduction.

[V] A sharp mechanism with direct robot consequences is one strong RSS form.

6. Pairwise comparisons: learning ranges from real paper differences
======================================================================

6.1 T-RO：Wijayarathne vs Pollayil

Shared stable patterns [S]:
- A core control problem expressible in one sentence;
- No requirement that every underlying tool be original;
- Mathematical formulation and online/real-robot closure;
- Supporting mechanisms are not inflated into separate major innovations.

Differences:
Wijayarathne: broad soft-contact dynamics,constrained force–motion planning and real-time operation
→ Naturally complex methods.

Pollayil: focused online stiffness/damping planning and perturbation bounds
→ Focused methods.

Conclusion [S]:
T-RO has no fixed algorithm-layer count. The stable relation is:
Complexity scales with problem and scientific-delta scope.

6.2 T-RO：Li vs Fu

Commonality [S]:
Both use many existing ideas to form new complete capabilities, with major components addressing failures.

Li: algorithm/control-centered, one main objective, support for learning safety/interpretability/convergence.
Fu: system-centered force regulation,workload and whole-system passivity, with broader methods/evidence.

Conclusion [S]:
Assess multimodule overdesign against claim scope.

6.3 IJRR：Lachner vs UFIC

Lachner: foundational inconsistency → one correction → minimal method,deep theory,hardware.
UFIC: general force/impedance unification → broad robots,sensing,passive environments → extensive theory.

Shared conclusion [S]:
IJRR does not mean T-RO plus more modules.
It favors deep/general scientific objects with complete mechanisms,theory and evidence.

6.4 IJRR：Piedra vs UFIC

Piedra: specific domain bottleneck and architectural change produce major dynamic-response gains.
UFIC: general problem,framework and broad theory.

Conclusion [S]:
IJRR supports broad theory or persistent domain bottlenecks with strong mechanisms/evidence.
Application does not automatically lower value, nor broad systems automatically raise it.

6.5 ICRA Beber vs T-RO Fu

Beber ICRA already closes tissue estimation → QP VIC → passivity → ultrasound safety.
Fu T-RO places QP VIC within broader shared-control/system-passivity claims and adds user evidence.

Lesson [V]:
Journal upgrades often expand:
- Problem/capability scope;
- Separation from strongest prior;
- Theoretical/evidence depth and breadth;
rather than adding one algorithm.

6.6 ICRA Jin vs T-RO Pollayil

Both address online/constrained impedance selection.
Jin：focused QP VIC。
Pollayil: more general planning,inertia structure,analytical/numerical solutions,performance/stability and multiple robots.

Lesson [V]:
Focused conference-to-T-RO upgrades often add generality,mathematical closure and evidence breadth, not more algorithm names.

6.7 RSS Reuss vs T-RO Li

Reuss: short causal chain,hybrid model → better feedforward → lower feedback gains.
Li: broader learning,feedback,transfer boundaries and theory.

Shared conclusion [S]:
Strong papers connect mechanisms to robot consequences, beyond isolated metric improvements.


6.8 Paired-lineage comparisons take priority over unrelated-paper comparisons

For same-author/problem conference→journal,specialized-journal→T-RO or early→mature versions, prioritize:
- Added scientific delta;
- Expanded generality;
- Whether theory develops from local results into a complete account;
- Demonstration-to-closed-loop online/real-time execution;
- Single-task-to-multicondition/multirobot/system evidence;
- Technical-point-to-paper-level scientific framing.

[S] Such pairs reduce confounding from problems,author ability and research lines when learning venue boundaries.

7. Venue Profile / Reference Distribution
======================================================================

7.1 T-RO

[V] Problem: important specific control/manipulation failures, general or application-specific.

[S] Delta: tools need not all be new, but nontrivial paper-level progress against strongest prior is needed, for example:
- meaningful generalization；
- new control relation / role；
- integrated capability；
- system-level interconnection；
- previously infeasible → real-time/executable。

[V] Packaging: samples often have 1 main delta plus supporting contributions; counts are not thresholds.

[S] Complexity varies widely; alignment with problem/claim/delta is stable.

[V] Theory often exceeds short-conference depth, but type follows claims.

[S] Online/physical-contact claims require controller/robot execution closure.

[V] Complex methods/broad claims require corresponding disturbance,ablation,hardware/user evidence.

Empirical center:
important failure
+ defensible nontrivial Method delta
+ matched mathematical closure
+ real executable robot evidence

7.2 IJRR

[S] IJRR is not T-RO plus more modules.

[V] At least one of the following is more common:
- foundational inconsistency / deep mechanism；
- broad/general framework；
- general control understanding；
- target-domain persistent bottleneck with strong general significance/capability jump。

[S] Very low or high complexity does not define IJRR.

[V] Theory emphasizes explanation,generality,physical/mathematical depth or framework completeness.

[V] Evidence must match claim scope:
Foundational theory demonstrates physical consequences; general frameworks cover broad settings; application bottlenecks need direct SOTA/task-level comparisons.

Empirical center:
deep/general scientific object
+ complete mechanism/theory/algorithm
+ evidence strong enough to support generality or capability significance

7.3 ICRA

[V] Focused technical deltas include optimization formulations,constrained controllers,domain integration and safety/passivity/estimation mechanisms.

[S] ICRA also permits full multimodule routes; module counts cannot separate conference/journal work.

[V] Current conference samples more often concentrate problem scope,generalization,theory and evidence.

7.4 RSS

Nearby RSS samples are few, so retain only [V]:
A distinctive algorithmic/modeling mechanism with substantial direct robot/control consequences is one strong form.

[R] Reuss's physics-plus-RNN design is not a fixed RSS template.


7.5 Venue-distribution overlap (added after reverse audit)

[S] Method-quality distributions overlap across T-RO,IJRR,ICRA,RSS,RA-L,RAS,TIM and others.
Therefore:
- RAS/TIM/RA-L methods may already be T-RO-like on some dimensions;
- T-RO methods need not exceed other venues on every dimension;
- Venue labels are not perfect supervised-learning labels.

Use actual papers to establish forms/ranges and readiness, not a title-to-venue classifier.

======================================================================
8. Repeated stable patterns
======================================================================

[S1] Innovation units are paper-level scientific deltas, not atomic-tool novelty.
MPC,QP,DDP,ADMM,RL,tanks,sensitivities,Hertz and RNNs may be established; ask whether control roles,relations or capabilities change.

[S2] Compare prior problems,mechanism roles and capabilities, not keywords alone.
Prior sensitivity use does not automatically negate novelty; an unprecedented A+B+C combination does not prove it.

[S3] Valid framing is part of publication.
Real papers choose favorable truthful granularity: module → mechanism → relation → capability/framework.

[S4] Framing cannot create absent scientific increments.
Renaming,changing scenes/solvers cannot rescue a relation/mechanism/capability already covered by strongest prior.

[S5] Complexity must align with problem,delta and claim scope.
Aim for minimum sufficient professional complexity, neither maximum nor minimum complexity itself.

[S6] Every major module should serve at least one role:
Core Scientific Mechanism / Correctness-Stability-Safety Support / Real-time-Solver Support / Execution-Interface Support / Evidence Support。
A major module unmapped to the paper-level claim risks overdesign.

[S7] Simple repairs of deep bottlenecks can be stronger than large architectures.
Pollayil,Lachner and Piedra illustrate this.

[S8] Complexity raises evidence burden.
Claims of necessary support layers need ablation,condition comparisons or failure analysis showing changed outcomes.

[S9] Match theoretical depth to claim type.
Stability/passivity needs analysis; robustness needs evidence/guarantees; real-time claims need timing/hardware; force RMSE does not establish clinical safety.

[S10] Engineering closure is central to leading robotic-control papers.
Optimizer outputs are not the endpoint: show executable control consequences.

[S11] Contribution counts are not quality.
Ask whether one causal chain summarizes the whole method.

[S12] Judge venue last.
Reconstruct delta,complexity,theory and evidence before venue alignment; do not choose IJRR first and add modules backwards.

======================================================================
9. Contribution framing: methods and red lines
======================================================================

9.1 Valid framing

P1. Raise contribution granularity
Weak: MPC + variable impedance + uncertainty + sensitivity
Strong if truthful: future interaction uncertainty directly shapes executable feedback mechanics
Equations/experiments must actually support that relation.

P2. Assign mature tools new control roles
Not first sensitivity use, but changing sensitivity from an analysis quantity to an online feedback-mechanics decision quantity.

P3. Replace component lists with causal chains
A supplies X; B establishes X→Y; C makes Y tractable in real time; D makes Y executable;
The contribution is X→Y, not A+B+C+D.

P4. Choose appropriate strongest-prior comparison axes
Distinguish novelty prior,simpler method-fit competitors and same-venue exemplars.

P5. Treat supporting modules as infrastructure
Do not claim mature solvers,standard IK or supporting safety filters as separate innovations to increase counts.

P6. State capability boundaries
Beyond lower RMSE, specify changed real-time,uncertainty,guarantee or information-to-feedback capabilities, or performance leaps demonstrating mechanism differences.

9.2 Red lines

[R1] Claiming first work despite prior coverage of the same relation/capability.
[R2] Disguising scene substitution as mechanism innovation.
[R3] Treating a new solver name as scientific delta.
[R4] Using uncombined A+B+C as the only novelty evidence.
[R5] Comparing weak baselines instead of strongest prior/simpler competitors.
[R6] Inflating innovation with complexity,proof/equation counts.
[R7] Promoting all support modules into contributions and diluting the main delta.
[R8] Narrative inflation: narrow evidence framed as a broad framework.
[R9] Capability inflation: routine metric gains without changed boundaries.
[R10] Cherry-picking prior work to manufacture gaps.

10. Complexity alignment: excessive versus insufficient methods
======================================================================

Condition complexity comparisons:
C_match = Method Complexity conditioned on {Problem Size, Scientific Delta, Claim Scope, Evidence Budget}

Prohibited:
More than 4 modules means overdesign.
One core equation is insufficient for a leading journal.
Actual samples refute such rules.

10.1 Three observed method forms

Type F — Focused Method
Examples: Pollayil,Lachner,Jin
1 clear core mechanism with minimal support and aligned mathematics/experiments.

Type M — Medium Integrated Method
Examples: Li,Piedra,Reuss,Beber
1 principal mechanism plus 1–3 direct supporting failure remedies, with theory/execution/experimental closure.

Type S — System / Framework Method
Examples: Wijayarathne,Fu,UFIC
Multiple modules for multiconstraint,multiobjective/general-framework claims; each serves a necessary system function with expanded evidence.

[S] F/M/S can all support leading journals. Mismatch occurs with F-scale problem/delta and S complexity, or S claims with F methods/theory/evidence.

10.2 Overdesign Signals

O1. New modules do not change paper-level relations/capabilities.
O2. Overlapping robustness/safety layers lack evidence justifying combination.
O3. Multiple solvers/decompositions add complexity when simple solving meets timing/constraints.
O4. Certification exceeds claim needs without stronger publishable conclusions.
O5. Secondary problems are invented to preserve complex modules.
O6. Lengthy module explanations lack one coherent causal chain.
O7. Ablating modules barely changes core outcomes.
O8. More layers than matched leading-journal deltas without stronger capability/guarantees.
O9. Complexity adds computational/implementation risks without proportional gains.
O10. Support modules are incorrectly framed as separate innovations.

10.3 Underdesign Signals

U1. Online/predictive claims without real-time implementation.
U2. Robustness claims with nominal simulation only.
U3. Optimized impedance parameters never reach executable feedback.
U4. Passivity/safety claims lack corresponding guarantees/evidence.
U5. Soft-contact models do not close physically with target scenes.
U6. Final scores without strongest-prior separation or mechanism evidence.
U7. Broad uncertainty/safety/real-time/human problems with one-subproblem solutions.
U8. Broad journal-level claims supported only by narrow validation.

11. Audit methods through pairwise comparisons, not scores
======================================================================

Do not output Innovation=8/10,Complexity=7/10,Theory=9/10.
Such scores lack reference meaning.

First select comparator sets:
A. 1–3 strongest novelty priors
B. 2–4 same-venue/nearby-field exemplars
C. At least 1 simpler method-fit competitor

Report dimensionwise:
Dimension | Current Method | Comparator A | Comparator B | Distribution Position | Meaning

Preferred distribution positions:
BELOW
ALIGNED
ABOVE-BUT-JUSTIFIED
ABOVE-BUT-UNJUSTIFIED
DIFFERENT-MORPHOLOGY

Answer:
Q1 Problem: where does it lie relative to real target-venue examples?
Q2 Delta: below,near or above comparable papers against strongest prior?
Q3 Packaging: strongest truthful granularity, or support mislabeled as innovation?
Q4 Complexity: lighter,aligned or heavier for matched problem/delta? What does added complexity buy?
Q5 Necessity: can a Pollayil/Piedra-style direct alternative suffice?
Q6 Theory: missing closure or excess proofs without publication value?
Q7 Engineering: model→decision→controller→robot closure comparable to real online work?
Q8 Evidence: does it defend the framed contribution, with corresponding ablations?

Final output includes:
A. Current position: nearest 2–4 published methods and reasons.
B. Venue-aligned dimensions.
C. Below-distribution gaps.
D. Above-distribution/unjustified-complexity risks.
E. Fragmented,low-granularity or inflated framing.
F. Minimal convergence: needed additions,deletions,mergers,support demotions and main-delta refinement.
G. Venue fit last: ICRA/RSS-like,T-RO-like,IJRR-like or below target, grounded in explicit pairs.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 Comparator-selection protocol (mandatory selection; added after reverse audit)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not choose comparators to make the method look strong. Follow:

A. Strongest novelty prior (1–3 papers)
   Closest input-information→mechanism/decision→output-capability relations most likely to compress novelty.
   Adversarial selection: dangerous rather than friendly prior work.

B. Same-problem alternative (at least 1)
   Different methods addressing the same cause to test necessity.

C. Simpler method-fit competitor (at least 1)
   Simpler structure solving much of the same problem, testing overdesign/necessity.

D. Same-venue morphology exemplars (2–4)
   Closest problem/claim forms, not arbitrary famous papers.

E. Paired-lineage comparator (include when available)
   conference→journal / early→mature version。

Explain why each is a comparator; titles alone are insufficient.

[R] Weak-only or different-route-only prior selection is cherry-picking.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11.2 Fatal-veto/indeterminate logic (added after reverse audit)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

These are evidence/veto conditions, not mechanical scores:

VETO-N1 Novelty Veto：
If prior work covers the same problem–mechanism-role–capability and differences are mainly scene,solver,parameterization or narrative, leading-venue delta is not established.

VETO-N2 Closure Veto：
Unclosed mathematics/physics,inconsistent variables or nonexecutable control preclude readiness regardless of framing.

VETO-N3 Evidence Veto：
Missing direct theory/experiments for core capabilities cannot be replaced by auxiliary metrics.

VETO-N4 Complexity veto (soft):
ABOVE-BUT-UNJUSTIFIED complexity with timing/implementation risk needs refinement first; complexity cannot compensate for professionalism.

INDETERMINATE：
Insufficient strongest-prior,full-text,experimental or equation evidence requires INDETERMINATE/PROVISIONAL, not guessed alignment.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11.3 Claim–evidence matrix (added after reverse audit)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Every paper-level claim needs direct evidence:

Claim | Required Evidence | Actual Evidence | Strength | Missing Gap

Examples:
- Preview changes impedance decisions → preview/no-preview ablation,gain trajectories,task outcomes;
- “robust to persistent parameter mismatch” → parameter-held-across-horizon tests / worst-case comparison；
- “real-time” → worst-case/percentile timing + deadline behavior；
- Passive/safe → matching theoretical conditions and energy/safety experiments;
- System workload reduction → user studies,not force RMSE.

[S] Direct evidence limits contribution framing.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

After major revisions perform at least four test types:

T1 Leave-One-Out Positive Test
Temporarily remove one core leading-journal paper and assess it using remaining distributions.

T2 Out-of-Sample Positive Test

13. One-page final empirical reference
======================================================================

General principle
Leading methods are not defined by required algorithms/module counts, but:
Within actual same-field publication distributions,
problem,delta,framing,complexity,theory,engineering and evidence must align with each other and comparable target-venue work.

Novelty
1. Recover strongest prior;
2. Identify genuine paper-level delta;
3. Compare delta scale/type with published neighboring leading work;
4. Frame true increments at mechanism/relation/capability/framework level where justified;
5. Framing cannot invent novelty;
6. Existing tools do not imply existing paper-level relations;
7. Unprecedented exact combinations do not prove novelty.

Overdesign
1. Do not count modules absolutely;
2. Align complexity with problem,delta and scope;
3. More complexity than comparable deltas without capability/guarantees risks overdesign;
4. Broader claims can justify complexity with expanded evidence;
5. Simpler remedies increase the burden to justify elaborate architectures;
6. Complex methods require more ablation/evidence.

Key counterexamples from real samples
- Pollayil T-RO: focused,simple methods can qualify.
- Wijayarathne T-RO: complex solving fits complex force–motion/contact tasks.
- Li T-RO: multiple components address necessary failures of one capability.
- Fu T-RO: broad system claims justify complexity/multidimensional evidence.
- Lachner IJRR: one foundational correction; depth is not complexity.
- UFIC IJRR: extensive theory still avoids impractical unification.
- Piedra IJRR: direct architectural remedies may beat elaborate alternatives.
- Beber ICRA: a conference paper can already contain a complete QP + estimation + passivity chain; module count cannot distinguish venues.

Final review question
Place the method anonymously among these papers:
Which dimensions differ from actual T-RO/IJRR/ICRA/RSS work?
Insufficient delta,incorrect framing,complexity mismatch,incomplete theory,missing execution or weak evidence?

Answering this concretely demonstrates understanding of leading-venue methods.
