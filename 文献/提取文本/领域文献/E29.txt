        Particularly pertinent to this paper are studies that use nat-
        ural actor-critic (NAC) methods [15]–[17], which show that
        they can quite effectively handle high-dimensional continuous
        state/action control problems. Park et al. [18] proposed a recur-
        sive least-square (RLS) filter combined with the NAC algorithm
        that can improve the computational efficiency of the original
        NAC [15].
           This paper utilizes an episodic NAC (eNAC) algorithm based
        on the RLS filter for learning arm impedance during robotic
        contact tasks. A preliminary version of this paper, which reports
        on the application of the RLS-based NAC to learning task space
        stiffness, has appeared in [19]. Depending on the characteristics
        of the tasks, different task goals or performance indexes should
        be selected. In this paper, the performance indexes for three                     Fig. 1.   Conceptual model of the equilibrium point control hypothesis.
        contact tasks (opening a door, point-to-point movement in an
        unknown force field, and catching a flying ball) were chosen to
        be optimized. The performance of each task was tested through
        dynamic simulations.
           This paper is organized as follows: Section II introduces
        the equilibrium point control model of human motor control.
        In Section III, we describe the details of motor skill learning
        based on reinforcement learning. Finally, simulation results and
        discussion of the results are presented.

                                                                                                                       V. C ONCLUSION
                                                                                             Safety in robotic contact tasks has become one of the
                                                                                          important issues as robots spread their applications to dy-
                                                                                          namic human-populated environments. The determination of
                                                                                          impedance control parameters for a specific contact task would
                                                                                          be the key feature in enhancing the robot performance. This
                                                                                          paper has proposed a novel motor learning framework for deter-
                                                                                          mining the impedance parameters required for various contact
                                                                                          tasks. As a learning framework, we employed reinforcement
                                                                                          learning to optimize the performance of contact task. We have
                                                                                          demonstrated that the proposed framework enhances contact
                                                                                          tasks, such as door opening, point-to-point movement, and ball
                                                                                          catching. Indeed, a number of studies on animal brains have
                                                                                          shown the evidence of reward-based reinforcement learning
                                                                                          in the basal ganglia area of the brain related to motor skill
                                                                                          learning [40].
                                                                                             In our future works, we will extend our method to teach a
                                                                                          service robot that is required to perform more realistic tasks
                                                                                          in 3-D space. For translation and rotation in 3-D space, high-
        Fig. 12. Stiffness ellipse trajectory during the ball-catching task.
                                                                                          dimensional stiffness ellipsoids are required. In addition, we
                                                                                          are currently investigating a learning method to develop motor
                                                                                          schemata that combine the internal models of contact tasks with
                                                                                          the actor-critic algorithm developed in this paper. The motor
                                                                                          schema, which develops the internal model and the action strat-
                                                                                          egy altogether through learning, is known to be computationally
                                                                                          effective and biologically plausible [4].
