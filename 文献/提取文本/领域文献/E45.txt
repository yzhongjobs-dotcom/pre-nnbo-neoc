                                                                                          actual trajectory x is a tradeoff between x d and x e . According
                                                                                          to different cost functions, e.g., when the Q 2 parameter in
                                                                                          the cost function (46) is larger, the robot actual trajectory
                                                                                          will be closer to the desired trajectory and the interaction

                                                                                          tion/trajectory is assumed to be constant. In many cases, this is
                                                                                          not true. When the robot manipulator interacts with the objects
                                                                                          with curved surfaces or with human beings with variable
                                                                                          equilibrium point trajectory, the varying position or trajec-
                                                                                          tory of the environments must be considered to obtain the
                                                                                          optimized robot–environment interaction performance. The
